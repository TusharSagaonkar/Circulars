Circular FM No. 25 dated 29 December 2007

Opening of Deposit Accounts  

- Verification of customer identity, place of residence, etc.

 

Instructions have been issued from time to time through OC, ID, FM circulars, Office Orders and IDCs regarding precautions to be taken and procedures to be followed while opening new deposit accounts, particularly emphasizing the need of establishing customer identity and ensuring address and place of residence given are correct.  Care has also to be taken to ensure bonafides of the Introducer of new account like verifying Introducer's signature, ascertaining how and how long the Introducer knows the person being introduced, etc.  In addition to such instructions, comprehensive policy guidelines on KYC norms and AML standards have been circulated to branches (OC No. 336 dated 08.02.2006).  Despite such repeated reminders, stressing the importance of obtaining & verifying proper identity, address and introduction at the time of opening a new deposit account several lapses on the part of branches are reported in the internal inspection reports and also come to light because of customer's complaints received by the Head Office.

 

2.      Recently, we had occasion to cause a detailed scrutiny of the lapses in opening of deposit account at a branch in the context of the depositor's complaint that large amount of money was withdrawn from the account fraudulently by the Introducer.  Several lapses in opening of the complainant's account and allowing operations thereon were observed by the inspectors during the scrutiny.  Firstly, the impression gathered was that the account had been opened by the Introducer himself without the depositor calling at the branch for the purpose.  No proof of residence, of the depositor and/or address given by him like ration card, electricity bill, etc. were obtained.  Permanent address of the applicant was also not obtained/ascertained.  Specimen signatures of the accountholder in the account opening form appeared to be made in different modes at different places in the form.  There was a difference of as many as 25 days between the date of application and actual date of opening of the account in the books of the branch.  Signature of the Introducer who happens to be a member of the bank was not verified.  Opening of the account was authorized by the Manager despite such serious procedural lapses/deficiencies.  Monitoring of account operation was also found to be lax and the first and some subsequent cheque books had been issued to the Introducer which resulted in fraudulent cash withdrawal.  Thus, the inescapable conclusion was that negligence and non-observance of prescribed safeguards at the time of opening of the account and laxity in monitoring operations thereon by the branch facilitated perpetration of fraud.

 

3.      The foregoing findings of a detailed scrutiny of a single account point to the fact that branches still continue to open deposit accounts in a casual manner without observing due care.  All concerned are, therefore, advised to desist from treating the account opening procedure as a mere formality and ensure strict adherence to the procedural safeguards prescribed in various H.O. instructions referred to in para 1.  Recurrence/detection in future, of lapses of the nature mentioned in para 2 above will be viewed seriously and accountability of the staff will be fixed and appropriate disciplinary action will be taken against the concerned employees.          

 

 

                                                                        M. C. Shetty

   Managing Director
