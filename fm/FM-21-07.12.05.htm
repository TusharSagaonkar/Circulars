Circular FM No. 21 dated 07 December 2005

 

Frauds perpetrated through accounts opened with fake/tampered documents.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------

 

          Instances of frauds perpetrated through accounts opened with fake documents and through misrepresentation by changing photographs in the PAN Card or pasted in the depositor's Pass Book at our branches have come to light in the recent past.  The modus operandi adopted by the fraudster was briefly as under:-

 

a.      At one of the branches a new SB account was opened by the culprit presenting fake PAN Card and Ration Card purportedly as proof of his identity and address.  The PAN Card had eleven digits number instead of ten digits number allotted by the Income Tax Authorities.  Culprit's age according to the date of birth in the PAN Card would be 25 years but the photograph given was of a person of about 40 years or more of age.  The discrepancies were not noticed by the branch staff.  Two days after opening the accounts, a cheque of Rs. 25,000/- was deposited and entire proceeds were withdrawn in cash in the following two days.  The fraud came to light after the drawer approached the branch and the drawee bank.  The Account holder is not traceable as his address on record also proved to be fake.

 

b.     In the same branch an another SB account was opened by a fraudster, producing fake documents viz., PAN Card and Ration Card as proof of identity and address.  Within a week of opening of account an outstation cheque of large amount was deposited for collection and substantial amount was withdrawn within the following two days after realization of cheque.   Later, on a visit to the addresses of the account holder, it transpired that the address given was fake.  Further, the introducer's account was found to be inoperative and he had also shifted from his address on the branch record. Both the account holder and introducer are not traceable.

 

c.     In another case at a different branch, a person misrepresented himself as the account holder and sought to withdraw cash from a saving bank account with withdrawal slip accompanied by the Pass Book. The culprit had changed the photograph pasted in the Pass Book that he presented with the withdrawal slip.  Although, the attempt was foiled because of alertness the Cashier and the Branch Manager, the Culprit who was handed over to the police admitted that on earlier two occasions he had succeed in withdrawing cash in a similar manner from the same account.

 

d.     Recently, at yet another branch, a fraudster could obtain new cheque book after submitting printed form for new cheque book of another customer's existing account indicating "cheque book lost" by forging signature of the account holder who had given him a bearer cheque earlier as consideration for his business transaction. Subsequently the fraudster has withdrawn larger amounts through bearer cheque/ CRC cheque by forging signature for about four times. The lapses observed were non-verification of the signature on the cheque book requisition form, non-obtainsion of recommendation from the manager for issuing new cheque book. Non-levy of charges for issue of duplicate cheque book, stop payment of cheque leaves lost, etc. More seriously the on all fraudulent instruments signature which was totally different from the one on record was not verified properly and appropriate enquiry had not been made at the time of payment of cash.  The  fraudster is himself an account holder in the same branch and  defaulter in respect of loan availed from the same branch. 

 

The modus operandi of the frauds is narrated to enable the staff of all the branches to note the weaknesses in the systems and procedures and the tricks employed by the fraudsters, and to emphasize the need for diligence required to be observed while dealing with the customers, both new and old.

 

1.     In the context of the foregoing incidents attention of all concerned is invited to the instructions laid down in OC 115 and in Circular FM 20 regarding precautions to be taken while making cash payment at the branch counters.  In addition to the safeguards indicated in the two circulars, it is advised that all concerned should ensure adherence to the following further safety measures.

a.      While opening new account, the customer may be requested to give his latest photograph in black and white so that while scanning the photograph clear impression can be obtained.

 

b.     In case of new deposit accounts depositor's photograph should be scanned at the time of scanning of signature.  Similarly, in case of the existing (old) accounts steps may be taken to scan the depositors' photographs from the account opening form.  It will help cashers/ passing officers to verify depositor's signature and photograph both at the time of making/ authorizing payment.

 

c.     It has been advised in Circular FM 20 that customer's signature across the photograph affixed on the application form may be obtained at the time of opening the account.  Some of the branches were found to have made the depositors sign across their photograph pasted in the pass books.  This practice is in correct and should be stopped forthwith.

 

d.     In addition to confirmation in writing to be obtained from the introducer of new account as advised in Circular FM No. 20 dated 15 July 2005, it is now advised that branches should also  obtain address confirmation slip from new account holder, which was sent by post (Code No. 542).  After receipt of confirmation slip of account holder, the cheque may be accepted for collection and/ or payment may be effected in the account and also issuing cheque book to the new account holder.

 

In case of urgency, branch staff may visit and verify the address of new account holder before making payment and/ or issuing cheque book to the new customer.

 

e.      If the introducer of the account is accompanying the new customer opening the account, thorough enquiry should be made with the introducer regarding period of his relationship with the new account holder, his/ her address, working place, any other bank accounts introduced by him, etc., It should also be ensured that the introducer is a regular customer of the branch/ bank and he is not himself a new customer and operation of his account is satisfactory.

 

f.       Cash Payment through bearer cheque to a third party in SB/ Current account of Rs. 15,000/- and above should be passed for payment only by Accountant/ Manager/ In-charge of the Branch after due inquiry and ascertaining identity of the bearer and in case of doubt, after cross checking with the account holder.

 

3.  In order to ensure that the branch staff are kept informed about the lapses that facilitated frauds and to impress upon them the need to be more vigilant and observe the prescribed safeguards while opening new accounts, collecting cheque deposited in such accounts and allowing withdrawals from such accounts the branch in-charges are advised to take action on receipt of these instructions in the same way as advised in para 3 of Circular FM 20 dated 15 July, 2005. 

        

                                                                                  M. C. Shetty

                                                                             Managing Director
