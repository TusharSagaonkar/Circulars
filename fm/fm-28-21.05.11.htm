Circular FM No. 28 dated 21st May, 2011

Attempted Fraud on Banks – remittance towards

Lottery Prize Money – Issue of fraudulent

letter for acceptance of deposit

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 

Reserve Bank of India vide its letter No. UBD.CO.BPD. 9190/09.50.001/2010-11 dated 11th April, 2011 and Letter No. DBS.CO.FrMC. 13094/23.02.016/2010-11 dated 25th March, 2011 has communicated us the following cases of attempted fraud :-

 

I. a)   A firm M/s. M.M. Enterprises (MME) opened a current account with a branch of a Commercial Bank in December 2009. A few days after opening the account, MME informed the Branch that it would receive, from aboard, a remittance of  Rs.5000.00 Crore into the account and requested the branch to issue a 'Letter for acceptance' (LOI) for the said amount as deposit. Since the amount was neither received in remittance nor did the customer submit any document evidencing such remittance having been effected from abroad, the branch refused to issue the LOA. Subsequently, two other branches of the same bank forwarded to the branch fax copies of LOA purportedly issued by it and sought confirmation from the branch for having issued the LOA.

 

b) The so called LOA read 'We are ready to accept the fund in the Current Account No. 30881842890 Rs.5000.00 Crore (Five Thousand Crore) under the name of Reserve Bank of India for 10 years one day". The above letter was prepared on the letter head of the branch and contained the signature of the Manager (Deposit) with his Employee Code and Bank's IFCS Code. The said letter was in the nature of an "acceptance letter" expressing the willingness of the branch to accept the above amount as deposit. A close scrutiny of the above LOA revealed that the customer had forged the letter in the name of the Branch.

 

II. In another incident reported by the same bank, one of their customers,  Shri. Sampatrao Appa Rao, having a Savings Bank account at their Srikakulam Branch, deposited for collection, a Cheque for Rs.50.00 Crore issued from his SB Account maintained with an Urban Co-operative Bank. Before receipt of proceeds of the cheque he requested the branch to issue an Special Term Deposit Receipt (STDR) for Rs.50.00 Crore. As proof of availability of funds at the end of the Co-operative Bank, he showed the Branch Manager a photocopy of a cumulative Deposit Receipt (CDR) for  Rs.100.00 Crore purportedly issued by the Urban Co-operative Bank in his favour. The branch issued the STDR as requested by the customer. Shri Rao then requested the branch to block funds in his SB account and issue a "Blocked Funds Letter" (BFL) favouring one Shri. Paramjit Singh Saund of London indicating availability of a blocked amount of Rs.50.00 Crore.  Shri. Rao requested the branch to transmit the BFL to Shri. Saund through a SWIFT message. Accordingly, the branch sent a message to Lloyds TSB Bank PLC. The SWIFT messages sent, first to Lloyds Bank and subsequently to SBI, London were not accepted as there were some errors in the message. Also the Urban Co-operative Bank returned the Cheque for Rs.50.00 Crore unpaid with reason "insufficient funds". The Urban Co-operative Bank also informed the bank that the CDR for Rs.100.00 Crore issued in favour of Shri. Rao was cancelled by them in June 2010 and that Shri Rao had requested them in writing, not to furnish any information about his deposits and source of funds to any other bank.

 

III. a)          Another Commercial Bank has reported an incident involving submission of a SWIFT message in MT-799 format purportedly representing issuance of a Stand-By Letter of credit (SBLC). One of the Bank's foreign branches was approached with a business proposition involving USD 45 million by Mr. Chen Zhen Kui, Proprietor of M/s. China Shenzen Zhanxian Electronic Co. Ltd.,   Mr. Peter Clark of M/s. A & l Global Electronics and Mr. Francis Ng Chin Heng, Regional Representative of Yayasan Amanat Tujuhbelas Tongat Comando, Nusantara. The business proposal was backed by copies of letters issued by P T Swadeshi Bank and a SWIFT message (MT-799) purportedly issuing SBLC for USD 45 million, As the SWIFT message was not in proper format the branch suspected the transaction and contacted P T Swadeshi Bank to confirm genuineness of the transaction. The bank confirmed that the letters and SWIFT message were not issued by them.

 

 b) In the case relating to MME, although the branch has maintained that the LOA was written by the customer himself fraudulently, his access to the Bank's stationery and mention of employee code/ IFCS Code of the signatory raise questions regarding the systems and controls at the branch level. It is also possible that the branches might be, in practice, issuing such letters indicating their willingness to accept the deposit even before receipt of the deposits / remittances.  Accordingly, the customer in question might have presented to the Bank some business proposal, to trick the branches into issuing such LOA.

 

2.      The above incidents of attempts to defraud banks are brought to your notice to enable you to caution you while dealing with such proposals received from prospective customers. Besides, if your branch has the system of issuing above type of "LOA/BFL/Blocked Funds Confirmation", the same shall be discontinued forthwith. Also, necessary controls, if any, may be put in place to prevent unauthorized issuance of Letters of Acceptance/blocked Funds Letters/Blocked Funds Confirmation. 

 

3.      Please take suitable note of these incidences and bring the contents of this circular to the notice of all concerned members of the staff at the Branch and be cautious and take extra ordinary care while dealing with similar cases.

 

 

     V.S. Morye

Managing Director
