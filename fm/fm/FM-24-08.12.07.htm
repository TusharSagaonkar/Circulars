Circular FM No. 24 dated 8 December 2007

 

Fraudulent encashment of Fake/Stolen/Altered

DDs, Cheques, Dividend Warrants, etc.–Use of Drop Box Facility

 

          Branches have been advised from time to time through Circulars in FM series, the modus operandi adopted by fraudsters for collection of negotiable instruments through banking channel, posing as the real beneficiaries in all such cases.  Encashment of fake/stolen/altered demand drafts, cheques, dividend warrants, etc. are very common type of frauds.  The safeguards to be observed by branches to prevent such incidents have also been indicated in the respective FM Circulars referrred to abvoe.  Strict adherence of KYC norms and ALM standards is also intended to act as effective deterrent against frauds of the nature described in the caption above.  It, however, appears that the fraudsters are ingenious and have kept ahead of the preventive measures and safeguards introduced by banks and have now taken advantage of the innovative service approach adopted by the banks.  RBI in their recent communication have advised details about a new trick resorted to by the fraudster wherein they apparently open deposit account without proper or real identity and succeeded in collecting proceeds of stolen instruments by recourse to cheque drop box facility.  The relevant communication from RBI is reproduced overleaf and branches are advised to ensure not only the observance of KYC norms while opening and monitoring new deposit accounts but also should exercise vigil while providing collection/payment facilities to new accountholders in respect of instruments such as cheques, DDs, etc. lodged in drop boxes for collection.  The branches when they are acting as the collecting bank should also extend all cooperation to the paying banks which become victims of frauds through clearing process as advised by RBI in the circular under reference. 

 

                                                                        M. C. Shetty

   Managing Director

 

 

 

 

Reserve Bank of India – Letter DBS.CO.FrMC.No.6880/23.04.001/2007-08 dated 20.11.2007

 

Frauds through encashment of fake/altered cheques, demand drafts,

pay orders, dividend/interest warrants.

 

A number of instances of banks being defrauded by presenting fake and altered cheques, demand drafts, dividend warrants etc. have been reported to Reserve Bank of India by various banks in the recent past. In most of the cases, the fraudsters deposited the stolen and altered instruments in drop boxes at centres other than the place where they maintained accounts and on collection and credit of the proceeds to their accounts, the amounts were withdrawn quickly. While the paying banks failed to detect the forgery at the stage of passing the instruments presented in clearing, the collecting banks were also unable to restore the funds to paying banks as the funds were withdrawn by the time the frauds were detected. Some of the paying banks have reported that the collecting banks were not co-operating with them in identifying the fraudsters. Failure to observe KYC norms might also be one of the reasons for the inability of collecting banks to trace the fraudster. In this connection it is necessary that paying banks should exercise vigil while passing instruments for payment. Collecting banks should ensure that extant KYC norms are followed in the opening and operations of accounts and that full co-operation is extended to the paying banks in identifying fraudsters and recovering the amounts involved in frauds.

2. The above modus operandi is circulated to alert the banks against the aforesaid nature of frauds. Banks are advised to take necessary action to prevent such frauds.
