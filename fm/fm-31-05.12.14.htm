Circular FM No.31 dated 5th December, 2014

Frauds in Cheques Presented in Outward Clearing

 

We wish to bring to the notice of all staff members the modus operandi of frauds taken place while presenting cheques in the outward clearing (CTS) in our bank’s branches. We furnish below details of the reported fraud cases.

1.   A CTS cheque drawn on a bank for `3,56,874/- was deposited in our branch for crediting in the current account of a customer. The cheque processing staff through oversight wrote wrong branch code as part of the 15 digit account number. Subsequently, the CTS cheque was processed and credit passed on to the wrong account holder. On clearance of the cheque, the account holder withdrew the money in cash over the counter as well as through ATM in a gap of three days. The account is presently showing debit balance of `3,56,874/-

2.   A cheque originally drawn in favour of a Private Limited Company was sent through post from London. The cheque was intercepted in between and altered and deposited in one of our branches. After clearance of the cheque the customer withdrew the amount within 2 days from different cash counters. The customer’s account had an initial deposit of `500/-. On inspection at the address provided by the customer, it was revealed that he had shifted long time back.

3.   A multi-city high value cheque was deposited for credit to SB account of our branch. The drawee bank informed our Central Clearing Dept. by email that the said cheque was fake. On enquiry it was found that the person who presented the cheque was a professional cheater and his account was opened in May 2014 and there were no other transactions except initial cash deposit.

4.   A customer had deposited a cheque for `3.50 lakhs drawn by a Finance Company, which was returned with the remark “drawer’s signature   incomplete”. On scrutiny it was found that it was a savings account cheque meant for individual customers.

It could be observed from the modus operandi adopted as stated above that the fraudsters are using ABB route in connection with cheque/cash related frauds. All staff are advised to be extra vigilant whenever there is an ABB transaction either for cash withdrawal or depositing of the cheque for credit to beneficiaries’ account. Staff should minutely scrutinize the instrument for any material alterations through maker/checker, use of ultra virus lamps and contacting the drawer in respect of cash withdrawal by third parties. Where there are several withdrawals on the same day the staff to be extra cautious and bring to the notice of the higher authorities. The CTS credit entries in the accounts should be verified from the details appearing in the cheque before posting. Also staff has to be extra careful in entering correct code of the branch, as otherwise the credit will go to the wrong party.

The IT Dept. to examine whenever there are multiple withdrawals in an account in a short gap of time, whether any pop-up message with different background colour could be incorporated in the system, so as to alert the staff. Further, whether multiple cash/ATM withdrawals on the same day could be captured in ‘exceptional transaction report’.

The Bank’s Staff Training College may provide intensive training to all staff members working on the counters as to how to identify forged/altered cheques. Series of training programme in the staff college as well as on the job training to be conducted for updating knowledge of all officials and staff in the subject matter. In view of the frequent instances of fraud, mistakes committed by staff and irregularities observed in the clearing and cash area, there is a need for intensive training and creation of awareness among staff.

The Incharge of Branches are advised to bring these instructions to the notice of all the officials/staff concerned and ensure strict compliance.
