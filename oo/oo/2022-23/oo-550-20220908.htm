Office Order No. 550 dated 8th September 2022

I.      Formation of Risk Management Executive Committee of the Board

II.    Creation of the Position of Chief Risk Officer in the Bank

III.  Designation of Shri. Pradeep V. Kamat as the Chief Risk Officer

IV.   Role and Responsibilities of the Chief Risk Officer

V.    Reconstitution of the Three Risk Management Committees

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

I.   1. Introduction

Please refer to the OC Circular No. 593 dated 29th March 2012 on the subject of “Implementation of Risk Management Policy”. In terms thereof, a Risk Management Department was created in our Bank which was until recently functioning under an Assistant General Manager. The philosophy of Risk Management, functions and responsibilities of the Risk Management Department, the three committees, apart from the Asset Liability Committee (ALCO), constituted to work on the three major categories of risks in the Bank namely the Market, Credit and Operations committees and also those of various formations in the Bank starting from the Board down to the branches (and the horizontal interactions across offices and departments) have been given in detail in the said circular.

 

2. Recent instructions received from the Reserve Bank of India

Reserve Bank of India, through its circular no. RBI/2021-2022/62 DOR.CRE(DIR). REC.26/ 21.04.103/2021-22 dated 25th June, 2021 has instructed the UCBs having a business size such as ours to appoint a Chief Risk Officer in a senior position with the definition of his role and responsibilities in such a manner that he will perform the said role and act independently as per the responsibilities so defined.

 

II. Risk Management Executive Committee of the Board

Hitherto, the full Board dealt with the function of overall Risk Management and its superintendence. This has been changed so that this function is taken over by the Risk Management Executive Committee of the Board. The constitution of the Risk Management Executive Committee of the Board and its functions are given in the Annexure-I hereto.

 

III.   Asset Liability Committee (ALCO)

The ALCO plays an important role in recommending measures for Risk Management in the spheres of capital augmentation and adequacy, working capital funds and their profitable deployment through maintenance of optimum Net Interest Margin, etc. Therefore, its constitution, functions, periodicity of meetings and other relevant matters are given in the Annexure-II hereto.

 

IV.  Constitution of the three Risk Management Committees according to the broad risk-classification

1.     The reconstitution of the three Risk Management Committees and their respective roles are as given in Annexure-III (A, B and C) hereto.

2.     The MIS department has now been designated as the Risk Management and MIS department (RM and MIS department). The Assistant General Manager, MIS department who was looking after the risk management in the Bank has been redesignated as AGM (RM and MIS) and he will report to the Chief Risk Officer. The entire redesignated RM and MIS department is now headed by the Chief Risk Officer. The Assistant General Manager (RM and MIS) will also function as the convener of the meetings of the three Risk Management Committees.

 

V. Designating Shri. Pradeep V. Kamat, Deputy General Manager as Chief Risk Officer in the Bank

1.     In implementation of the instructions contained in the RBI circular referred to in the Sub-Para I.2 above, Shri. Pradeep V. Kamat, Deputy General Manager, has been designated as the Chief Risk Officer in the Bank.

2.     He has taken over as Head of the Risk Management and Management Information Systems Department (RMMIS).

3.     Shri. Pradeep V. Kamat has a fixed tenure of 2 years. He will continue to function in the said role without any change till the specified tenure expires, except that the role may be changed by a decision of the Board which will be communicated to the Reserve Bank of India.

4.     The orientation expected of the Chief Risk Officer, the constituents of his role and responsibilities are given in Annexure-III.

 

VI.  Initiation and process of identifying the risks and their mitigation

1.     The Risk Management Function in the Bank has a widely distributed ownership across major functional and specialist departments namely Credit department, Integrated Treasury department and the Department of Operations and Human Resources.

2.     The Zonal Offices, Regional offices and branches also have a role in identification of and reporting on the risk-dynamics and implementation of the Risk Containment Measures.

3.     The major functional and specialist departments, by conducting structured and other studies of the macroeconomic developments, indicators and regulatory policy formulations shall assess the static and dynamic risks through projected scenarios.

4.     The above-named functional and specialist departments shall update the parameters for risk management in their respective areas. In case of a new business initiative or a change or addition of a new dimension to an existing business or an operational procedure, appropriate new parameters will be evolved.

5.     The said departments may take the software support of the Risk Management and Management Information System (RMMIS) Department for financial and economic forecasting to compute dynamic risks and relative risk parameters.

 

VII. Orientation, Role and responsibilities of the Chef Risk Officer

The broad constituents of the orientation of the Chief Risk Officer, and the key components of his role and responsibilities in the said position, are listed hereunder. This should not however be interpreted narrowly but needs to be widely read in and inferred from the Risk Management Policy already in place.

 

i.     Orientation of the Chief Risk Officer

The Chief Risk Officer is expected to have

a.  sound grasp of the macroeconomic, financial and regulatory environment with particular regard to its implications over the foreseeable future,

b.  knowledge and awareness of various business and operational verticals of the Bank with their current status as to their strengths and vulnerabilities, enabling an overall understanding of the risks involved in them.

 

ii.    Role and responsibilities of the Chief Risk Officer

a.  The Chief Risk Officer has been made the head of the Risk Management and the Management Information Systems department (RMMIS). He will guide the department in its activities. The Assistant General Manager who was until recently heading the RMMIS has started reporting to the Chief Risk Officer. The entire team of the previous RMMIS will work under the Chief Risk Officer through the AGM.

b.  The Chief Risk Officer shall be a special invitee to the three (Credit, Market and Operational risk) Committees. He shall also be an invitee for the ALCO committee meetings.

c.  Primarily in the meetings but also outside thereof, he shall examine the components of the respective risks assessed and the measures for mitigation placed before the committees for consideration. Various parameters for businesses and operations, whether pertaining to the exposures (credit, market etc.), ratios (credit), eligibility (credit), delegation of financial and administrative powers, solvency and liquidity, profitability (interest rates on deposits and advances, costing of and charges for non-credit products) shall be studied by him for suitability and relevance.

d.  After the matters of risk assessment and their mitigation are deliberated upon in the risk committees (and ALCO), the proposals shall be forwarded under the signatures of the chairman of the respective committee to the Chief Risk Officer for his formal concurrence.

e.  The proposals after his due endorsement shall be placed by him for approval of the Executive Committee of the Board for Risk Management.

f.   The Chief Risk Officer shall periodically monitor the adequacy or relevance of the risk mitigation parameters. The indicators of inadequacy or irrelevance of the parameters could be defaults (credit), losses (market), frauds and complaints (operations), audit irregularities (breaches of delegated authority and other matters) and regulatory observations. Towards this, the Chief Risk Officer may call for the reports on trends of NPAs, synopsis of frauds and complaints, and lists of serious irregularities pointed out by the internal, concurrent, statutory and the RBI auditors.

g.  The Chief Risk Officer may commission studies in respect of risks in the areas in which he is of the opinion that such studies are warranted. He may, with the permission of the Managing Director, rope in such experts from other functional and specialist departments as he considers fit to work on the studies. Literature, software support and institutional training (in CAB, IBRDT, NIBM etc.) may be arranged with prior permission of the Managing Director. In case of all such studies and arrangements for training, cost-benefit analysis with timeline for completion must be submitted for approval.

h.  The Chief Risk Officer is expected to place before the respective committee the findings resulting from his monitoring, the studies conduced or commissioned by him and from his overall grasp of the macro-economic and financial developments. He will ensure that the risk mitigation measures are appropriately modified or introduced in light of his inputs.

i.   The Chief Risk Officer shall place before the Risk Management Committee of the Board a consolidated report every quarter containing the following (apart from the specific proposals for revision of the Risk Mitigation Parameters).

·      The number of the meetings of the three committees attended by him.

·      The number of the meetings of the ALCO attended by him.

·      The number of studies conducted.

·      Specific findings of the study.

·      Submission will be made regarding due weightage in determining/ modifying the risk mitigation parameters as above.

·      Results of the exercise of monitoring the adequacy or relevance of the risk mitigation parameters & a presentation on any need perceived to further modify or introduce a new risk parameter? A need so perceived will be clearly spelt out.

 

VIII.   While the Risk Management Department will collect and process the information internally generated and draw suitable inferences for policy formulation and/or action, one important aspect of the Risk Management Policy (put in place vide the OC Circular No. 593 dated 29th March 2012 referred to above) which captures the distributed and shared responsibility of all the formations in the bank in respect of Risk Management is reiterated hereunder for sensitisation of and action by all the branches, offices and departments. The words in parentheses are added for bringing clarity to the original text.

“The Risk Management Department will collect necessary information from the branches as well as various departments from time to time in this regard (i.e. in regard to the Risk Management). (However), it may be borne in mind that the respective departments will continue to monitor (and) manage the risk as 1st line of Risk Management and the Risk Management Dept. (RMD) will be the 2nd line of defense and not a substitute for departmental efforts. The assessment of the risk by the RMD will be dispassionate and more macro and comprehensive in nature ------”.

IX.   Please bring the contents of the instant circular to the notice of all the members of the staff for careful study and compliance.

 

 

P.S. Salian

Managing Director
