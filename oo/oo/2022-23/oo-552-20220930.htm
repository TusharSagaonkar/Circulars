Office Order No. 552 dated 30th September 2022

I.      Extremely High Percentage of ECS/NACH Debit Mandates returned

(Consistently more than 25% as against the Tolerance Level of 4%)

II.     Cautionary Letters issued by NPCI to our Bank

III.   Branches not paying attention to Instructions to reduce the Incidence of Returns

IV.   Branch In-charge and Official concerned to be Personally Accountable

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

I.       Background

1.  Under the current policy framed by the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) in respect of clearing, the tolerance limit for ECS/NACH debit returns is 4%. Such returns result in the system-wide settlement risk. Therefore, the percentage of returns from all the members of the clearing system is closely monitored by the NPCI and cautionary letters are issued to the members whose percentage of returns breaches the tolerance limit.

 

2.  The statistics generated by the NPCI and shared by them with us over some period show that the returns percentage of the said debits is extremely high. It has been as high as 33.37% and never below 25%. This is not at all acceptable. The NPCI has written to us thrice during the last one year cautioning and asking us to bring down the percentage below the tolerance limit.

 

II.  Internal instructions so far

1.  Even before the letters from the NPCI, the Bank at the apex level was already seized of the problem and Branch Incharges were being repeatedly advised to bring down the percentage. The Clearing department has been sending several communications to the branches and Zonal Offices, by way of IDCs and emails, calling for urgent attention.

 

2.  In respect of each letter from NPCI mentioned in Para I.2, specific internal follow-up action has been taken through IDCs from the General Manager (HRM and Operations) to branches and their controllers, calling for immediate remedial measures. Possible reasons for returns are listed in the IDCs and steps to be taken so as to arrest the incidence thereof are also prescribed. The prescribed steps include contacting the parties and obtaining letters from them. The forms of the letter to be addressed to parties and the letter to be obtained from them have been provided. Lists of borrowers who repeatedly figure in the lists of debit returns are provided to branches. Branches have been instructed to submit time-bound reports regarding the action taken in respect of each party which is listed.

3.    i. The form of the letter to the parties concerned is again attached hereto as

 Annexure-I.

ii. Similarly, the form of the letter to be obtained from such parties is also attached hereto as Annexure II.

iii. The format of the list of parties appearing frequently in the debit mandates returned, which is periodically supplied to branches for information and expected action, is also provided again herewith as Annexure-III. It now contains additional columns for branches to indicate the specific desired actions taken so that the reporting becomes meaningful.

 

III.   Continuing incidence of a very high level of ECS/NACH debit mandates in spite of repeated communications from the Clearing department and the GM (HRM and Operations) leads to the unfortunate but definite conclusion that the Branch Incharges are utterly negligent in paying attention to the instructions on the subject. It is also obvious that the controllers are lacking in their efforts to follow up with branches and ensure prompt and meaningful remedial action. Branches and Zonal Offices do not appear to realise that any action by the NPCI for repeated breaches not only has a reputational but also an operational risk.

 

IV.  Therefore, we are issuing the following instructions for immediate compliance.

1.    Each Branch-In-Charge shall go through the list of parties/borrowers having the ECS/NACH debit mandate returns beyond the tolerance level, which is periodically supplied by the Clearing department.

 

2.    Each such party/borrower must be contacted and a letter as per Annexure-I must be served upon it/him/her/them within two days of receiving the list from the Clearing department.

 

3.    A letter as per Annexure-II hereto must be obtained from each such party/borrower. This is towards cancellation or change in the debit mandate. This task must be completed within five days of service of the letter as per Annexure-I (i.e. within seven days of receiving the list from the Clearing department).

 

4.    The cancellation of/change in the mandate must be uploaded through the Mandate Management System. This must take place on the day of receiving the Annexure-II (Cancellation/change in the mandate) or latest on the next day.

 

5.    Meaningful compliance report incorporating action taken as prescribed (i.e. mentioning YES for the indicated action with the date and reference no. of branch communication), or mentioning NO (which is not expected in normal circumstances) with the reason for not taking action, with further logical action of uploading the mandate in the Mandate Management System or follow up in case of the parties not responding to the letter and visits, as applicable, must be submitted to the Clearing department within ten days of receiving the list of parties from the Clearing department as per Annexure-III.

 

 

V.    It must be noted that the Branch-In-Charge and the official concerned shall be held personally accountable for lapses in taking action as per Paragraph IV above.

 

 

P.S. Salian

Managing Director
