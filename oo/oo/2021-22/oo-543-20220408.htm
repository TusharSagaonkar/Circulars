Office Order No. 543 dated 8th April 2022 

Designation of Shri. Rajesh Paralkar, Dy. Gen. Manager (Credit Monitoring Dept.) as Chief Compliance Officer (CCO)

 

 

I. This has reference to Office Order No. 540 dated 10th December 2021 regarding allocation of duties among the General Managers and Deputy General Managers. The above Office Order and its Annexure define the updated allocation of duties among General Managers & Deputy General Managers.

 

II.  RBI vide their Circular No. UBD.BSD.1.PCB.11/12.05.01/2002-03 dated 16.08.2002 advised all UCBs to designate a senior official as Compliance Officer. Bank has been designating Compliance Officers from time to time in accordance with the same.

 

III. It was decided, in terms of the Board Resolution dated 29th December 2021, to designate Shri. Rajesh Paralkar, Dy. General Manager (Credit Monitoring Department) as Compliance Officer in Bank. However, as there are a few compliance officers in Bank in some specified areas, it was considered appropriate to designate Shri. Rajesh Paralkar as Chief Compliance Officer (CCO) of the Bank and this fact has accordingly been advised to Reserve Bank of India through a letter dated 3rd January 2022. The role as CCO of Bank, which Shri. Paralkar has since assumed, is in addition to his role as Deputy General Manager (Credit Monitoring Department). Shri. Paralkar has taken over the function of erstwhile Compliance Officer (now named as Chief Compliance Officer-CCO) and relative records from the previous incumbent.

 

IV.Compliance function in Bank

The compliance function in Bank is described in the relative policy circularised under Office Order No. 402 dated 31st December 2012, the relative Para of which is reproduced below.

 “The Compliance function has to ensure strict observance of all statutory provisions contained in various legislations such as Banking Regulation Act, Reserve Bank of India Act, Foreign Exchange Management Act, Prevention of Money Laundering Act, Income Tax Act, Societies Act etc. as well as to ensure observations of other regulatory guidelines issued from time to time, standards and codes prescribed by BCSBI, IBA, FEDAI, FIMMDA etc., and also each Bank’s internal policies and fair practices code.

The compliance laws, rules and standards generally cover matters such as observing proper standards  of  market  conduct, managing  conflicts  of  interest, treating customers fairly and ensuring the suitability of customer. They typically include specific areas such as Prevention of Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing, and may extend to tax laws that are relevant to the structure of Banking products or customer advice. Compliance laws, rules and standards have various sources, including primary legislation, rules and standards issued by  legislators and supervisors, market conventions, codes of practice promoted by industry associations, and internal codes of conduct applicable to the staff/members of the bank.”

V.  Role of CCO

The role of CCO has two parts. The first requires a broader vision and involves policy-action, and the other requires application of mind and devotion of attention to transactions and activities being conducted in the Bank.

 

1.   Role of CCO requiring broader vision and involving policy action

i.Assess various risks, such as regulatory and legal, being faced or may potentially be faced by Bank and thinking out strategies and measures for their mitigation. This calls for wide study, monitoring of websites of regulators, Self-Regulatory Organisations (such as FEDAI and FIMMDA) and industry bodies (such as Indian Banks Association and NAFCUB), and following legal developments and economic and financial news.

ii.     Put before the Board the assessment of risks and the steps required for their mitigation at yearly intervals.

     (Note-A: - To enable the CCO to perform this function, each department will first assess the risk profile of its products and activities and ways of risk mitigation pertaining thereto, and forward the relative document to the CCO well before the date of the meeting. Consultations may be had by the department concerned with the CCO before the latter firms up his view in the matter.

     Note-B: - The CCO should also scan the banking environment to assess the emerging generic risk (apart from a specific activity or product-related risk) and suggest measures to mitigate the same in the said half-yearly/yearly meeting.

iii.   Participate in group meetings for modification of existing products, systems and procedures as well as the development of new products, systems and procedures, so as to ensure that they are compliant with regulations, enactments and standards.

     (Note-C:- The department concerned will provide to the CCO comprehensive notes on the products, systems and procedures, to be introduced a new or to be modified, with the primary analysis of risks and ways of their mitigation done).

iv.   Examine circulars, office orders and manuals, which have been prepared by the departments concerned, from a regulatory compliance angle, after they are finalized by respective departments but before their issuance.

 

2.   Role of CCO requiring attention to transactions and activities being conducted in the Bank.

i.   CCO is the face of the Bank before RBI for compliance purposes. Therefore, his role consists of ensuring that transactions put through by Bank are in compliance with

a.     the various directives, guidelines and instructions issued by Reserve Bank of India and other regulators such as SEBI and IRDA, and Government(s), and,

b.     the guidelines, directions and suggestions by Self-regulatory or umbrella organisations (such as FIMMDA and FEDAI) and industry bodies (IBA and NAFCUB) in respect of special or general functions.

 

[NOTE-D :-

ii There are compliance/nodal officers in Bank who carry out compliance functions in defined limited areas. Their positions and the system of reporting by them are as under.

ü  Compliance officer for Forex Transactions: - The report is submitted to CCO.

ü  Nodal officer for fraud monitoring including forged notes: - While the report will continue as hitherto to be submitted directly to RBI, copies of reports on incidents actually taking place will also be submitted to CCO.

ü  Compliance officer for KYC-AML (reporting directly to FIU-IND: - Only monthly synopsis of ATRs in terms of statistics will be submitted to CCO.

·     Further, a three-tiered structure of customer service committees is in place in the Bank, and standards of various services, complaints and disposal of assets of deceased customers are monitored through it.  CCO will be supplied with a copy of monthly Minutes-cum-Action-Taken-Report to the Board level Committee on Customer Service for a managerial look.

·      CCO will rely on compliance/nodal officers appointed for special functions and need not look into compliances in respect of their functions parallelly]

 

3.   Mechanism of internal certification in respect of compliance pertaining to transactions and activities across departments and offices of Bank

i.     Departments and offices to furnish compliance certificates

a. Each department and office of the Bank will furnish the relative comprehensive certificate of various compliances at the end of each quarter in respect of its transactions and activities during the quarter.

b. Department-wise and office-wise lists of categories of transactions and activities capturing compliances with directives / guidelines / instructions issued by the Reserve Bank of India, other regulators, self-regulating organizations and industry bodies etc. (contained in the comprehensive certificates referred to in sub-Para a. above) shall be supplied by Chief Compliance Officer to respective departments / offices as and when they are modified. The system of modifying and updating is further explained in sub-Para ii below.

 

ii.   Steps for ensuring comprehensive certification

a. Heads of departments and offices to update lists of items in certificates

·     In order that the certificates obtained from various heads of departments and offices continue to cover the essential items of compliance, the head of each department and office shall update the list of categories of transactions and activities handled by it from time to time in consultation with the CCO.

·     Towards this, each departmental and office head will be required to keep himself/herself abreast of all the instructions from regulators, self-regulatory organisations and industry bodies. Important Govt. (both Central and State) notifications and enactments bearing on banking shall also be monitored. Instructions not published in the public domain (say on the website of a body concerned) but communicated to the Head Office of Bank will be passed on to the department or office concerned for inclusion in the certificate concerned (apart from indication of action expected thereon if any).

b. Complementary role of CCO in respect of updating the lists

CCO will ensure that the lists so updated are indeed sufficient to capture the essential aspects of tasks and activities pertaining to each department and office. Towards, this CCO will also scan the website of RBI.

c. CCO to receive an index of important communications

Secretarial Department will furnish through email to the CCO every month an index of important communications received from regulators, self-regulatory organisations and industry bodies having bearing on policies, systems and procedures and products of the Bank in particular and banking in general. The format of the index is given in Annexure-hereto.  CCO may call copies for any of them, not in the public domain, to perform the complementary role as per sub-Para b. above.

iii.  Primacy of responsibility of heads of departments and offices

It is however made clear that the primary responsibility of scanning websites of regulatory and self-regulatory bodies, and Govt. enactments and notifications, shall be that of the department or office concerned for the business activity, task or subject.

iv.  Sample cross-check by CCO of compliance certificates

a. CCO shall call for statistics from the Inspection and Audit department regarding deviations from laid down instructions to cross-check the compliance certificates submitted by departments and offices.

b. CCO may also take help from MIS and IT departments to develop tools for monitoring compliance in critical areas.

v.   Detailed subject-wise lists of audit points to be developed by the Inspection and Audit department to facilitate the development of comprehensive lists of department-wise/office-wise activities and products.

i.   It is necessary to develop detailed subject-wise lists of audit points which can take care of many compliance issues.

ii. Inspection and Audit department shall develop such lists as comprehensively as possible. The department should take up this task in hand and complete it by 30th June 2022.

iii.          The lists once developed and put in place will also help generate statistical information regarding compliance which will enable CCO to take a managerial view.

 

4.   CCO to ensure compliance of Supervisory Action by RBI

One important aspect of the CCO’s role is to ensure strict compliance in Bank of a Supervisory Action if any taken by RBI.

 

5.   Audits and investigations to be commissioned by CCO in exceptional situations

CCO is authorised to commission a special audit or investigation in a case considered appropriate by him. Such cases are expected to be rare. While such an audit can be entrusted to members of the Inspection and Audit team, an investigation may be entrusted by him to an official or executive of his choice.

 

VI.   Compliance report finalised by Inspection Department in respect of RBI audit

The inspection department will furnish a copy of the compliance report of the RBI’s inspection Report to the CCO promptly after the same is finalised and submitted to RBI.

 

VII.  Responsibility of compliance is on all the executives and officials

1. It must be noted that the branches, departments and offices are responsible for compliance in their respective areas. Executives and officials heading the branches, offices and departments are not absolved of their responsibility if compliance pertaining to the branches, offices or departments under them are pending, or wanting in some aspect, for whatever reason.

2. In case of failure on the part of a Branch-In-Charge or an official in an office or department to do compliance, a proper note along with supporting documentary evidence (such as copies of correspondence with the Branch-In-Charge or official of the office/department) should be placed before the executive concerned in the bank. Such matters will be required to be taken up in the Audit Committee meeting and the delinquent official may be called to submit an explanation for the lapse.

 

VIII.    It is obvious from the above that the role of CCO is very critical. Therefore, information called for by him must be furnished qualitatively and promptly. Similarly, communications sent, instructions issued, inputs provided and reservations expressed by him must be accorded due importance.  

 

IX.Please take suitable note of these instructions and bring the contents of this Office Order to the notice of all members of the staff concerned at your Branch/ Department.

 

 

P.S. Salian

Managing Director
