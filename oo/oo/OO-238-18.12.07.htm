Office Order No. 238 dated 18 December 2007

 

NPA Management & Recovery Policy

 

The bank had issued various circulars, office orders, manuals, IDCs, etc. from time to time in respect of recovery of NPAs, filing of cases etc. In order to streamline recovery procedure and for effective NPA management the bank has drafted policy & procedure for the NPA management. The Reserve Bank’s Inspector during the last inspection also insisted on a Recovery Policy. We have, therefore, drafted “NPA Management & Recovery Policy” of the bank, based on all relevant RBI circulars and our internal instructions & procedures.  A copy of the said document is enclosed herewith.

The main objective of the document is as under:

i)      To take all precautionary measures to prevent deterioration of Loan Assets, by constant follow up and monitoring.

ii)      To restrict slippage of Standard Account to NPA Account by effective post disbursement follow up and monitoring of Advances.

iii)     To implement time bound action plan based on perception of signs of likely or actual default by borrower.

iv)     To take prompt and effective steps for upgradation of identified NPA Accounts by using all persuasive, quasi-legal & legal measures viz. recovery through normal process, action under SARFAESI Act, Arbitration, u/s 138 of Negotiable Instrument Act, etc.

v)     To strengthen the existing system of monitoring and follow-up of borrowal accounts and to set uniform procedure at Branch & Departmental levels for adjustment of sale proceeds of prime & collateral securities, accounting of interest part, time bound action plan for initiating various types of Recovery action, filing cases, compromise settlement/OTS, etc.

vi)     To maximize recovery of NPA accounts and to minimize loss.  To bring down percentage of Gross NPA of the Bank to single digit.

In the enclosed documents detailed guidelines have been given for effective management & recovery of NPAs for achieving the major objectives of the Policy set out above.  The role of branch, Zonal Office & Legal & Recovery Dept is clearly demarcated.  The bank has decided to bring our gross NPA in absolute terms to Rs.140.00 crores by 31.03.2008 from Rs.203.48 crores as on 31.03.2007. However, the NPA positions as on 30.09.2007 is not at all satisfactory.  The NPAs at branches are increasing at alarming rate which needs to be restrained immediately.  In view of such disturbing trend observed at the branch level special emphasis has to be given on timely and effective post disbursement follow up and monitoring of big advances.

The compromise policy incorporated in this recovery policy has been forwarded to Reserve Bank of India for their approval.  The scheme will become operational only after receiving RBI confirmation/approval. 

All concerned are hereby advised to strictly implement the recovery policy and exert to bring down the NPAs of the bank as per projection given for the financial year 2007-08.

 

 

                                                                                        M. C. Shetty

                                                                                    Managing Director
