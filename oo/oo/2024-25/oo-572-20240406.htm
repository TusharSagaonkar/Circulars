Designation of Shri. Adhikrao Shripati Kumbhar as the Chief Risk Officer

I.  Introduction

1.  Please refer to Office Order No. 550 dated the 8th September 2022 on the subjects corresponding to those under caption in keeping with the situation and circumstances then obtaining.

 

2.  The philosophy of Risk Management, functions and responsibilities of the Risk Management Department, the three committees apart from the Asset Liability Committee (ALCO) constituted to manage the three major categories of risks in the Bank namely the Market, Credit and Operations and also those of various formations in the Bank starting from the top down to the level of branches (and the horizontal interactions across offices and departments), appointment of the CRO and his duties and responsibilities, etc., were given in detail in the said circular. The contents of the Office Order were majorly in compliance with the circular of the Reserve Bank of India applicable to UCBs, bearing reference no. RBI/2021-2022/62 DOR.CRE (DIR).REC.26/ 21.04.103/ 2021-22 dated the 25th June 2021 responsibilities so defined.

 

3. The CRO appointed through Office Order No. 550 dated the 8th September 2022 was subsequently placed in the position of General Manager (Legal and Recovery) and, in his place, Shri. Suhas Naik, the Deputy General Manager was appointed as the Chief Risk Officer through Office Order No. 558 dated 27th June 2023.

4. Later, owing to the exigencies of business, Shri. Suhas Naik was given another assignment. Upon approval by the competent authority, Shri. Adhikrao Kumbhar, a Special Officer, was designated as the Chief Risk Officer in place of Shri. Suhas Naik. Shri. Adhikrao Shripati Kumbhar took over as the Chief Risk Officer on/125th January 2024 and has been functioning in that capacity since then.

 

II.  Risk Management at the level of Board/Administrator’s Committee:

The Board/Administrator’s Committee is responsible for the Bank’s wider Risk Management. The details of the agenda and periodicity of meetings etc. of the Board/Administrator’s Committee on the subject of Risk Management are given in Annexure-I.

 

III. Risk Management Committee of Executives (RMCE):

A Risk Management Committee of Executives is constituted to guide and monitor the functioning of the Risk Management Department (RMD) headed by the Chief Risk Officer (CRO), the three subject-wise Risk Management Committees, and ALCO, and to act as a tier between them and the Board/Administrator’s Committee. The constitution of the Risk Management Committee of Executive (RMCE) and its functions are given in Annexure-II hereto.

 

IV.  Asset Liability Committee (ALCO)

The ALCO plays an important role in recommending measures for Risk Management in the spheres of capital augmentation and adequacy, working capital funds and their profitable deployment through maintenance of optimum Net Interest Margin, etc. Its revised constitution, functions, periodicity of meetings and other relevant matters are given in Annexure-III hereto.

 

V.  Three Risk Management Committees according to broad risk classification

The details of the three Risk Management Committees and their respective roles are as given in Annexure-IV - A, Annexure-IV - B and Annexure-IV - C hereto.

 

VI.  Initiation and process of identifying the risks and their mitigation

1.   The Risk Management Function in the Bank has a widely distributed ownership across major functional and specialist departments namely the Credit department, Integrated Treasury department and the Department of Human Resources Management etc.

2.   The Zonal Offices and branches also have a role in their respective domains regarding the identification of and reporting on the risk dynamics and the implementation of the measures to contain them, being the first line of defence.

 

VII. Role of major functional departments and offices

1.   The major functional and specialist departments, by conducting their own structured and other studies of the macroeconomic developments, indicators and regulatory policy formulations, shall assess the static and dynamic risks through projected scenarios.

2.   The above-named functional and specialist departments shall update the parameters for risk management in their respective areas. In case of a new business initiative, a change or the addition of a new dimension to an existing business or an operational procedure, appropriate new parameters will be evolved.

3.   The said departments may take the software support of the MIS department for financial and economic forecasting to compute dynamic risks and their parameters.

4.   The above departments shall report their findings to the Risk Management Department (RMD), which will carry out further studies/analyses, if needed, and place them before the respective Risk Management Committees.

5.   All branches and functional departments should report incidents of loss/ fraud or near-miss events to the RMD, immediately upon the occurrence of such events. This shall be apart from the prompt reporting of frauds etc. in accordance with the laid down procedure under the direction of the Reserve Bank of India and other extant internal instructions.

6.   The Secretarial department shall provide data on all customer complaints and frauds to the RMD every month.

7.   The Inspection Department shall submit copies of the executive summaries of all inspections/audits to the RMD.

8.   The Treasury Department shall send reports to the RMD on the position of liquidity, cut losses, exceptional transactions etc. daily.

 

VIII. Tenure of the CRO, expectations of his orientation, and constituents of his role and responsibilities

1.   Shri. Adhikrao Shripati Kumbhar continues and will continue to function in the said role without any change, except that the role may be changed by a decision of the Board/ Administrator’s Committee of the Bank under communication to the Reserve Bank of India.

2.   The CRO shall not be entrusted with any other duty or responsibility in the Bank other than the Risk Management function. The role however includes making an independent Risk Rating of High-Value Credit Proposals i.e. of value Rs. 1 Crore and more. However, to enable the CRO to do an independent Risk Rating, the process defined in Para IX.2.viii below must be followed. (It is clarified that no other requests like for change of surety, change of etc. are to be referred to the CRO).

3.   Compliance Risk constitutes a major risk in the Bank. However, there is a separate Chief Compliance Officer (CCO) in the Bank. Therefore, the CRO shall not get directly involved in the assessment, computation and management of the compliance risk but shall seek the relative data from the CCO for inclusion in his estimation and strategy for mitigation of the aggregate of all risks.

 

IX. Orientation, Role and Responsibilities of the CRO

The broad constituents of the orientation of the CRO, and the key components of his role and responsibilities in the said position, are listed hereunder. These should not however be interpreted narrowly but should be widely read in and inferred from the Risk Management Policy.

1.  Orientation

The CRO is expected to have

i.   sound grasp of the macroeconomic, financial and regulatory environment with particular regard to its implications over the foreseeable future,

ii. Knowledge and awareness of various business and operational verticals of the Bank with their current status as to their strengths and vulnerabilities, enabling an overall understanding of the risks involved in them.

 

2.  Role and responsibilities

i.     The CRO has been made the head of the Risk Management function. He will guide the Risk Management Department in its activities.

ii.   The CRO shall be a member of the three (Credit, Market and Operational risk Management) Committees. He shall also be an invitee for the ALCO committee meetings.

iii.  Primarily in the meetings but also outside thereof, he shall examine the components of the respective risks assessed and the measures for their mitigation placed before the committees for consideration.

iv. After the matters of risk assessment and their mitigation are deliberated upon in the three risk management committees (but not ALCO), the points involving/requiring decisions shall be communicated by the CRO to the Risk Management Committee of Executives (RMCE).

v.   The CRO shall periodically monitor the adequacy or relevance of the risk mitigation parameters. The indicators of adequacy/relevance, or otherwise, of the parameters could be defaults (credit), losses (market), frauds and complaints (operations), audit irregularities (breaches of delegated authority and other matters) and regulatory observations. Towards this, the Chief Risk Officer may call for synopsis of frauds and complaints, audit observations, etc. from the departments concerned.

vi. The CRO may commission studies in respect of risks in the areas in which he is of the opinion that such studies are warranted. He may, with the permission of the CEO, rope in such experts from other functional and specialist departments as he considers fit to work on the studies. Literature, software support and institutional/ online training (in or from CAB, IDRBT, NIBM etc.) may be arranged with prior permission of the CEO. Opportunities for participation in Webinars, seminars etc. on the appropriate subjects may be provided. In the case of all such studies and arrangements for training/webinars/seminars, a cost-benefit analysis with a timeline for completion must be given while submitting the proposal for approval.

vii.  The CRO shall vet, from the angle of Risk Management (other than Compliance Risk Management) (the policies of the Bank as they are framed and modified from time to time. It is to be recorded here that vetting of the policies from the angle of Compliance Risk Management is done by the CCO.

viii.      The CRO shall make an independent Risk Rating of High-Value Credit Proposals i.e. of value Rs. 1 Crore and more subject to the following process being followed.

a. Limit Criteria: The business credit proposals (i.e. excluding all personal loans) which are either new or those involving renewal/enhancement having/taking customer-wise total exposure to Rs.1.00 crore and above shall be referred to the CRO for risk rating.

b. Zonal Office reports: All proposals even if for renewal/ enhancement/ ad hoc limit or for non-funded facilities should be routed through Zonal offices with their comments and recommendations to approve/ disapprove the request.

c. Documents Required: The proposal file should contain certified copies of all necessary documents required as per the Bank's policy. However, the following information is a must for the risk rating of the proposal.

ü  'CIBIL reports with Score', of the borrower and its Proprietor/Partners and Directors

ü  All necessary Permissions / Valid licenses necessarily required for running the business.

ü  Viability Report of the Industry Finance Manager, wherever required.

ü  Details of Change in the Management during the last three years.

ü  Details of Change in the business profile during the last three years.

ü  IEC code/ negative list declaration, wherever required.

ü  Declaration for no pendency of statutory dues.

ü  Details of attachment orders if any, received in the last three years and their present status.

ü  Overall liability (Direct and indirect) report including outside borrowing/ obligation of the borrower and its Proprietor/ Partners and Directors.

ü  Audit Observations and Pending compliance of last statutory, regulatory and stock auditor.

ü  Details of all collaterals with their latest valuations, related documents of the property and Mortgage ability report and latest valuation reports from the Govt. approved valuers.

ü  List of major suppliers/ debtors and creditors.

ü  Details of Project implementation, in case of project loans.

ü  ZO report about business achievements as per projections

ü  Insurance details

ü  Technological Capabilities/ threats and major risks.

ü  Internal Rating as per the Credit Policy

ü  External Credit Rating (if exposure of Rs.10.00 Cr.)

d. Industry Report: To get in-depth information about the industry the borrower is dealing with, the department will refer to the reports published by the reputed credit rating agencies like CRISIL, Care, ICRA etc. The bank will have to subscribe to such credit rating agencies to get industry reports.

e. Time required at CRO's Office: It will be the responsibility of the CRO department to dispose of the proposal as early as possible. However, taking into account the wide coverage of the required information, a minimum of four working days are indicated for the risk rating of the proposal at the CRO's end.

f.  Comments by CRO: The CRO will comment on the overall risk in the proposal. He will not recommend any financial amount in the proposal.

g. After Risk rating, the CRO will forward the proposal to 'Credit Department-HO' for further necessary action./1Risk mitigation across the desirable parameters (5 Cs of credit, Risk Base Pricing, Contractual Debt Covenants, Post Disbursement monitoring, limiting sectoral exposure etc.) is the responsibility of the Credit Department./1

 

ix. The CRO is expected to place before the respective committee the findings resulting from his monitoring, the studies conducted or commissioned by him and from his overall grasp of the macro-economic and financial developments. He will ensure that the risk mitigation measures are appropriately modified or introduced in light of his inputs.

 

x.   The CRO shall place before the Risk Management Committee of Executives a consolidated report containing the following (apart from the specific proposals for revision of the Risk Mitigation Parameters).

a.   The number of the meetings of the three committees attended by him.

b.   The number of the meetings of the ALCO attended by him.

c.   The number of studies conducted.

d.   Specific findings of the study.

e.   Submission will be made regarding due weightage in determining/modifying the risk mitigation parameters as above.

f.    Results of the exercise of monitoring the adequacy or relevance of the risk mitigation parameters and a presentation on needs perceived to further modify or introduce new risk parameters. The needs so perceived will be spelt out.

g.   The list of new or modifications of existing products, particularly credit products, examined and cleared from the angle of Risk Management.

h.   The list of credit proposals of value Rs. 1 Crore and above referred to him and differences (with reasons) if any which he might have had in respect of credit rating assessed and recorded by the recommending department or the external agency.

i.     The list of policies vetted by him from the angle of Risk Management (other than Compliance Risk Management).

 

X.       The MIS department will now be headed by a Chief Manager. The Chief Manager of the MIS department will report to the CRO. However, departments other than the RMD will also continue to avail the services of the MIS department.

 

XI.     The CRO shall inter alia have the overall responsibility for coordinating the activities relating to the identification, management, and mitigation of the Bank's risk.

 

XII.   The CRO shall also guide the activities of the Risk Management Dept and also supervise the activities of its staff.

 

XIII. The CRO has responsibility for overseeing the development and implementation of the bank’s risk management function. This includes ongoing strengthening of staff skills and enhancing risk management systems, policies, processes, quantitative models, and reports as may be necessary to ensure that the bank’s risk management capabilities are effective to fully support its strategic objectives and all its risk-taking activities.

 

XIV. The CRO’s responsibilities also include managing/participating in key decision-making processes (e.g. strategic, capital and liquidity planning, new products/ services, compensation design/operation).

 

XV.   The CRO shall particularly examine the new credit products or modifications in the existing credit products from the Risk Management angle.

 

XVI. The CRO will function as the secretary to the Risk Management Committee of Executives (RMCE) and convenor of the meetings of the Board/Administrator’s Committee on the subject of Risk Management.

 

XVII.           He will arrange meetings of the RMCE as well as meetings of the Board/ Administrator’s Committee on Risk Management at least every quarter or at a shorter periodicity.

XVIII. The CRO, under the report to and guidance of the RMCE, shall be actively engaged in monitoring performance relative to risk-taking and risk-limit adherence.

 

XIX. CRO shall support the Board/ Administrator’s Committee in establishing an Integrated Risk Management System capable of identifying, measuring and monitoring of all types of risk on an ongoing basis.

XX.   He shall report to the CEO of the bank.

XXI. He will get an audience of the Board/Administrator’s Committee without the presence of the CEO or other members of the RMCE at least once in a quarter and on such other occasions as may be necessary. Notes of the discussion during such meetings shall be kept.

XXII.             He shall examine the components of the risks assessed and the measures for mitigation placed before the respective committees for consideration. The proposals after his due endorsement shall be placed by him for approval by the Risk Management Committee of Executives.

 

XXIII. The CRO shall periodically monitor the adequacy and relevance of risk mitigation parameters. He shall ensure that risk mitigation measures are appropriately modified or introduced in the light of his inputs.

 

XXIV. The CRO shall monitor and ensure the proper working of teams involved in credit, market and operational risk functions including review and upgradation of the respective risk management policies, risk identification, loss event management, development of risk library, business line mapping, Risk Control Self-Assessment (RCSA), and development and monitoring of KRI.

 

XXV.   He shall arrange for the training of bank employees in the area of risk management.

 

XXVI. He will contribute to the creation of risk culture, and risk awareness amongst the employees.

 

XXVII.         He shall procure/develop necessary software/tools to assess, identify, measure and mitigate risk.

 

XXVIII. While the Risk Management Department will collect and process the information internally generated and draw suitable inferences for policy formulation and/or action, one important aspect of the Risk Management Policy which captures the distributed and shared responsibility of all the formations in the bank in respect of Risk Management is reiterated hereunder for sensitisation of and action by all the branches, offices and departments.

        “The Risk Management Department will collect necessary information from the branches as well as various departments from time to time in this regard (i.e. in regard to the Risk Management). However, it may be borne in mind that the respective departments will continue to monitor and manage the risk as 1st line of Risk Management and the Risk Management Dept. (RMD) will be the 2nd line of defence and not a substitute for departmental efforts. The assessment of the risk by the RMD will be more macro-natured and comprehensive”.

XXIX.  A copy of the instant office order shall be placed by the HRM department in the personal files of Shri. Suhas Naik, Dy. Gen. Manager and Shri. Adhikrao Kumbhar, Special Officer, both.

 

XXX.   Please bring the contents of the instant circular to the notice of all the members of the staff for careful study and compliance.

 

 

P.S. Salian

Chief Executive Officer
