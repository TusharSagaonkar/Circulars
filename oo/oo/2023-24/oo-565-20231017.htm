Office Order No. 565 dated 17th October 2023

Operational Guidelines for Effective Functioning of Compliance Activities

I.  Background

1.   The RBI issued guidelines to the banking industry in general on the subject of framing the Compliance policy and the manner of its implementation.

2.   The RBI in terms of Circular No. RBI/2022-2023/118 Ref. No. DOS.CO.PPG/ SEC.04/11.01.005/2022-23 dated 19th September 2022 has issued specific instructions to the Urban Co-operative Banks on the subject of putting in place a compliance policy and the manner and method of implementing the same.

3.   The Compliance Policy in the Bank was reviewed and reframed in accordance with the RBI’s general guidelines and specific instructions. It was deliberated upon in the Board meeting dated 27th March 2023 in light of the Approach Paper on the subject. Upon approval therefor in the Board meeting, the Compliance Policy was circularised internally under cover of the OC Circular No.885 dated 1st April 2023.

4.   The instant Office Order incorporates operational guidelines for implementing the Compliance Policy in letter and spirit.

 

II.  Adoption and ownership of compliance by all departments, offices and branches of the Bank

1.   The Compliance Policy emphasises throughout that compliance is the responsibility not only of the Chief Compliance Officer or the department he heads. Compliance should be an integral part of the culture of the organization.

2.   The bank will be able to manage the Compliance Risk effectively only through participation from all the functionaries from Top to Bottom. The functionaries include all the Business Heads, the Heads of Departments and Offices providing support services, and the Specialist Functionaries. Last, but not the least, each Branch-In-Charge will lead the compliance function for the frontline formation which he/she heads.

 

III. Assignment of roles and responsibilities for better functioning of Compliance activity

Assignment of roles and responsibilities for ensuring compliance from all the layers of the management, from the Members of the Board of Directors down to Branches, is as under.

1.   The Bank’s board of directors is responsible for overseeing the management of the bank’s compliance risk. The board has examined the comprehensive Approach Paper on the subject and approved the bank’s policy which guides the compliance function in the Bank. The Board has fixed the periodicity for the review of Compliance policy and Compliance Risk as Yearly.

2.   Role of Senior Management

i.       The Bank’s Senior Management is responsible for the effective management of the Bank’s compliance risk.

ii.     As per compliance policy, Senior Management is comprised of the Managing Director and (then two and now) four General Managers.

iii.    The Senior Management will carry out an exercise, at least once a year, to identify and assess the major Compliance Risks facing the Bank and formulate plans to manage them.

iv.   Two General Managers tasked with spadework for identification, assessment, and suggestions for mitigation of risks

a. Within the Senior Management, two of the General Managers as amongst them decided by the Board, will carry out an exercise, at least once a year, to identify and assess the major compliance risks facing the Bank and formulate plans to manage it.

b. The report for particular FY will be completed within 3 months of submission of audited balance sheet and will be deliberated upon by the Senior Management. The CCO should normally be invited to the meeting of the Senior Management so that he may present his assessment of the current state of compliance risks and the efficacy of the means of mitigation.

c. In the Board meeting held on 16.05.2023 it was decided that General Manager – Legal & Recovery and General Manager – Accounts, Inspection & Treasury to place report for F. Y. 2022-23 through Managing Director for submission to the Board so that it leads to a review, and if need be, revision, of the Compliance Policy on the one hand and issuing of operational instructions on the other to mitigate the risks from the evolving compliance risk scenario encapsulated in the report for subsequent year.

 

IV.  Interactions which the Chief Compliance Officer shall have with other functionaries/ departments

1.   The Chief Compliance Officer has a direct reporting line to the Managing Director. This will generally be in all circumstances. However, such a direct reporting line is more important in respect of the prompt submission of reports of any material compliance failure.

2.   The CCO ascertains irregularities or lacuna potentially leading to breaches through sample testing (please see Para No. V below for more on this). The CCO is free to report to the Senior Management the irregularities or lacuna so ascertained, without fear of retaliation or disfavour from management or other staff members. While the normal reporting line is to the Managing Director, the CCO should also have the right of direct access to the Board of Directors or to the Audit Committee of the Board, by passing normal reporting lines, when this becomes necessary.

3.   The findings of test check reports & other correspondence for which Managing Director remark for actions to be followed up by Compliance Department & to be placed before Board of Management as agenda item for deliberation.

4.   While the responsibility for initiation, conduct and conclusion of an appropriate remedial action including administrative action shall continue to rest with the respective functional departments, CCO will be kept informed regarding the same as it will be his responsibility (parallelly with the department concerned) to apprise the Board in such matters.

5.   The matters placed before the Board for deliberation sometimes include matters of regulatory as well as statutory compliance. The Secretarial Department will advise the CCO regarding such matters coming up before the Board for deliberation. The Board may seek the view of the CCO in such matters. The seeking of views of the CCO by the Board shall be through notes or audio /video conferencing.

6.   The CCO shall report on matters concerning compliance to any of the committees at quarterly intervals on a one-to-one basis without the presence of members of the Senior Management including the Managing Director. Thus CCO shall be made a permanent invitee to the Audit Committee so that it will become possible to oversee the implementation of the compliance policy and review the compliance function by the Audit Committee on a quarterly basis. The review and oversight will also cover major irregularities pointed out in the compliance testing report (by the Compliance Department) and sharing of observations by the Inspection, Vigilance Department on major irregularities.

 

V.  Submission of certificates of compliance by departments and sections

1.   There is already a system in place for quarterly submission to the CCO of compliance certificates designed to cover activities typical of each department and section. Each department must submit the certificates truthfully and timely. This should not be treated as a routine and ritualistic affair.

2.   The mechanism of improving upon and updating the certificates to keep them relevant in the changing circumstances is also provided for in the Compliance Policy. The shared responsibility of improving upon, modifying and updating the compliance certificates is emphasised in the compliance policy. This exercise must be carried out by each Departmental Head on the one hand and the CCO on the other through incisive studies.

 

VI.    Monitoring and testing the compliance function

1.   The compliance department shall monitor and test compliance by the departments, sections and offices through sufficient and representative testing by the staff in the compliance department.

2.   The testing shall be on the basis of 10 to 50% samples chosen across the departments and offices and their activities.

3.   The actual percentage of samples shall be in accordance with the perceived gravity of risks associated with them.

4.   To begin with, the perceived gravity of risks and the percentage-testing to be undertaken in accordance with them will be on an impressionistic basis. Over a period, it will be gradually and progressively founded on the data to be gathered (such as performance indicators, number of identified breaches and deficiencies, corrective measures taken or required to be taken and so on). The following will also contribute to the data which will help determine the gravity and the proportionate sample size for testing:- (i) Information culled from the Audit Reports, Data / Reports submitted to regulatory authorities (ii) Minutes of the meetings (iii) Non-compliant parameters reported in the Quarterly Compliance Certificates (iv) Unreplied queries raised on earlier compliance testing reports (v) Persistent irregularities.

5.   The results of the compliance testing shall be reported on a quarterly basis to the Managing Director whereupon he will provide guidance in this regard and issue further instructions to the CCO for remedial action. The Managing Director may also put up the results of compliance testing with remarks before the Board if he so desires.

6.   Compliance testing must not remain a mere fault-finding exercise. It should result in appropriate guidance notes and instructions enabling rectification and prevention of recurrence of deviations from compliance.

 

VII.  Anticipatory and preventive function of the Compliance Department

1.   In tune with the Compliance Policy, the Compliance department under the leadership of the CCO shall, on a pro-active basis, identify, document and assess the compliance risks associated with the Bank’s

(i)          business activities including the development of new products and business practices,

and

(ii)          the proposed establishment of new types of business or customer relationships, or material changes in the nature of such relationships.

2.   In order to facilitate the aforesaid function of the Compliance Department, each department and office will first assess the risk profile of its products and activities (including the proposed ones) and ways of risk mitigation pertaining thereto and forward the relative document to the CCO.

 

VIII.    Wide ownership of compliance responsibility

1.  It is emphasised several times and it is yet not enough that the compliance department is not the sole owner of the compliance function.  Compliance responsibilities must be shouldered by staff in all the departments/branches and offices pertaining to their respective class of business and operations. The overall compliance function in the organization shall get performed across three lines. The details are as under.

2.  i. The  primary responsibility  for  the  Compliance  function,  consisting  mainly of

observance of rules/regulations set by the (i) Statutory Authorities in the form of circulars, (ii) Circulars including Master Circulars and Master Direction issued by RBI, (iii) guidelines issued by other regulators like IRDA, CCIL, UIDAI, GoI, IBA, CVC, FIU-IND etc., and (iv) internal guidelines framed by the Bank will be that of respective Departments /Branches. The Departmental/Office Heads and Branch-In-Charges will own the responsibility to ensure that the observance detailed above takes place in their respective units. This is to be called as first line of Business.

v.   Non-observance and violation of guidelines and penalties if any imposed by the authorities concerned shall be the subject matter for staff accountability.

 

3.  Compliance Department to be the Second Line

i.         a. The Compliance Department housed in the Head Office will be the second line

in as much as it will oversee the functioning of all the other departments and offices to ensure observance of proper rules through monitoring of compliance certificates and test checking for their truthfulness.

       b. It will review from compliance angle the products and services as and when

they are launched for the first time, or modified and repackaged,

       c. It will study synopses of audit/vigilance reports, exceptional detailed reports

of major incidents having wide and deep compliance implications, and statutory Audit Report & RBI-IRAR reports to suggest changes in systems and procedures, products and the compliance framework.

ii.     The Compliance Department is headed by Chief Compliance Officer (CCO).

iii.      Appointment, tenure and role of the CCO are defined in the Compliance Policy. Appraisal of the CCO is to be done by the Managing Director and assessed by the Board on a yearly Basis. Instructions are issued to the HRM Department to prepare the structure of the assessment report with descriptions and weights for performance parameters

iv.     In order to ensure the independence of the CCO, it is clarified that the members of the Senior management other than the Managing Director shall have no role in the assessment of the CCO.

4.  Inspection Department to be the third line of defence

i.       The inspection department will be the third line to guard against deviation from the compliance standards. The Inspection Department will carry out this task by working independently. It conducts inspection of all the branches, departments and offices of the bank internally as also by engaging firms from the outside i.e. Concurrent Audit. 

ii.     Compliance Department is also subject to audit. The audit is to be carried out by deputing staff of Internal Inspection / Concurrent Auditors

iii.    The compliance function and the audit function must be separate to ensure that the former is subject to independent review.

iv.   The CCO shall use the reports of Internal Audit, Concurrent, Statutory Audit and RBI-IRAR a feedback mechanism for strengthening the compliance system in the Bank.

v.     In order that this happens, the Inspection department should provide, on a monthly basis, synopses of the Inspection and Concurrent Audit Reports. The synopses should generally cover only systemic issues. Major issues commonly arising from audit/vigilance action should be highlighted so that they can be studied for improving upon the compliance framework. However, incidents having much wider and deeper implications should be shared in full detail. 

vi.   The RBI-IRAR report will be available to the Compliance Department from the moment it is finalised and delivered to the Bank as the CCO is the face of the Bank.

vii.  The Chief Compliance Officer, the Chief Risk Officer, and the Head of the Audit and Inspection/CISO/Head of Vigilance Department should work in close coordination to make the compliance function most efficacious.

5.  Summary of Compliance Function at three levels

Effective management of Compliance Function takes place at three levels.

First level: - Compliance activities at each department and office responsible for business and/or operations

Identification and reporting of non-compliance within the department/office.

Second level: - Functioning of the Compliance Department itself which involves ensuring compliance through

a.   receipt of quarterly reports by the departmental heads and their scrutiny followed by placing of the observations of non-compliances before the Audit Committee,

b.   test checking of actual compliance by the departments and offices and referring the cases of non-compliance to the Managing Director, and serious issues of non-compliance to the Board Members,

c.   review from compliance angle of the products and services as and when they are launched/modified,

and

d.   studying synopses of audit/vigilance reports, exceptional detailed reports of major incidents and RBI-IRAR report to suggest changes in systems and procedures, products and the compliance framework

Third level: - Inspection Department and Vigilance Department each of which reports and interacts with the Senior Management and Board members helps and assists them in overseeing the implementation of the overall Compliance Policy, among other things.

(The audit of the activities of the Compliance Department is to be organised through deputing staff of Internal Inspection / Concurrent Auditors).

IX.  Responsibility of Senior Management

1.   The bank’s Senior Management is responsible for the effective and efficient management of the bank’s compliance function. The Chief Compliance Offices has a direct reporting line to the Managing Director.

2.   In order that the compliance function in the Bank is managed effectively and efficiently, the Senior Management will engage with the CCO on an ongoing basis.

3.   Apart from the one-on-one engagement such as above, the heads of Accounts, Inspection, Vigilance, and Compliance shall meet on a structured basis at monthly intervals. The Chief Compliance Officer will be the convenor of such meetings. While the General Manager, Accounts, Inspection & Treasury will formally chair such meetings, he will treat all the participant role-holders as near-peers working toward the common cause of reaching compliance excellence. The participants will forward in advance to the CCO notes concerning major compliance issues which they come across during the period between two meetings so that such issues will be discussed resulting in concrete action points.

4.   A quarterly activity report will be submitted by the CCO to the Managing Director to enable review by the latter of the functioning of the Compliance Department. Suitability of changes if any suggested by the Managing Director in the manner of functioning may be implemented by the CCO. An annual report compiled by the CCO on the compliance function in the Bank, duly examined, commented upon and countersigned by the Managing Director, will be submitted to the Board. It will cover activities of the Compliance Department, observations on major compliance lapses and deviations including those from regulatory instructions, guidelines, SAF conditions, audited financial statements and others, and systemic measures taken towards mitigation of compliance issues. The report should also include support expected from the Board for better functioning of the compliance activities. The report should enable the Board to have a full grasp of the compliance function in the Bank so that remedial measures if any may be directed.

 

X.Support to and from the Compliance Department  -

1.   The Compliance Department will be supported by adequate staff.  Taking into consideration activities to be carried out by compliance department, it is the responsibilities of HRM department to provide desired staff strength. The staff of the Compliance Department shall preferably have a fair knowledge of law, accountancy, information technology, Forex and Treasury, and also have adequate practical experience in various lines such as Audit / Inspection functions. This will enable them to perform their duties effectively. Compliance testing to be undertaken by the department will also yield better results due to the knowledge and experience of the staff. In order to keep the compliance staff up-to-date with developments in the areas of banking laws, rules and standards, regular and systematic education and training shall be imparted to them wherever and whenever deemed necessary. The Compliance department also serves as a reference point for the staff from operational departments so that interpretations and clarifications in respect of various regulatory and statutory guidelines may be sought from the compliance department.

2.   The Compliance Department is required to point out the compliance deficiencies to the controlling authorities directly functioning under the HO. The accountability for deficiencies and deviations shall lie at the functional and operating levels directly responsible for breaches of norms/guidelines laid down by various regulators as also those laid down internally.

 

XI.  Compliance manual for each department

1.   Each department and office shall prepare a policies & procedure including compliance manual and operating guidelines covering its own activities. It shall be vetted by the CCO whereupon the same shall be placed by the department/office concerned for approval of the Board.

2.   This set of policies / procedure including compliance manual shall be reviewed, and renewed, on an annual basis by each department.

XII. Scope for the outsourcing compliance function

Compliance should be regarded as a core risk management activity within the bank. Specific tasks of the compliance function may be outsourced, but they must remain subject to appropriate oversight by the Head of the Compliance Department i.e. the CCO.

 

XIII. Responsibility for submitting structured and unstructured reports to RBI and other authorities

1.  Structured reports required by RBI

i.     As CCO is the face of the Bank before the RBI, CCO may be contacted at any time by the latter for want of, or delay, in the submission of structured reports. Therefore, the Accounts Department, Legal & Recovery Department, I.T Department, Secretarial Department, Treasury Department, KYC/AML Department, Inspection Department, Marketing Department, Credit Department, Credit Monitoring Department, and ATM Recon Department etc. should ensure from their side timely and accurate submission of the structured reports.

ii.   All reports to be sent to RBI are to be uploaded/reported through the “Daksh Portal” as well as reports submitted by respective departments in XBRL/OSS returns . The date and time of the reports/returns so uploaded shall be informed to the Compliance Department as a part of the monthly Certificate (Details of the report as per Annexure - Format of certificate is attached).

iii.  The above will also apply, mutatis mutandis, to the structured reports / returns required to be submitted to other regulators and other Govt./Quasi-Govt. & statutory authorities.

2.  Unstructured information and reports demanded, and queries raised, by RBI.

3.   The RBI and other regulators often demand unstructured information, reports and statistics, and also raise queries as to the functioning of the Bank. Such information, reports and statistics demanded, and queries raised, are received at a set of email addresses of the Bank. The Secretarial Department shall arrange to forward the said demands made and the queries raised from RBI and regulators to the CCO. The CCO shall be the focal point for studying and organising responses to the demands and queries. He will therefore forward the said demands & queries to the departments and offices concerned for the compilation of information reports and statistics, and formulation of responses to queries. The departments and offices concerned shall forward the compilation and formulation to the CCO who will thereafter transmit the same to the Secretarial Department so that the latter will arrange for their submission to the RBI or other regulators.

 

XIV.  In order to enable the fulfillment of compliance requirements, desired support is to be provided by I.T. Department and MIS Departments to the Compliance Department (just as it is provided to other departments, offices Branches and business verticals).

XV.  Any communication having compliance implications, including that in regard to Restrictions / SAF guidelines from RBI, other regulatory authorities, police authorities, EOW, CBI, UIDAI, etc., must be shared by the receiving department or office of the Bank with the Compliance Department immediately for record, reference, and action if necessary.

 

XVI. Annual Financial Inspection by RBI

1.   The CCO shall facilitate the Annual Financial Inspection of the Bank by RBI. He will be the point of contact between the bank and the regulators. The head of the Inspection Department is expected to coordinate with the RBI Officials during the AFI and ensure prompt submission of reports and compliance of queries raised and irregularities pointed out.

2.   The CCO shall coordinate for compliance of the AFI.

3.   Statutory Audit report & RBI Audit Compliance report is to be transmitted by Inspection Department to the RBI through the CCO / Secretarial Department.

 

XVII.  Monitoring RBI’s website for imposing of penalties on entities in the financial sector

RBI issues press releases on its website regarding penalties imposed on various Banks for non-compliance. These will also be among the guiding factors for the departments and offices in our Bank to ensure the avoidance of non-compliance.

 

XVIII. Complementarity of roles of CCO and CRO

The Chief Risk Officer is entrusted with the identification and mitigation of Credit, Market and Operational risks. The CCO is responsible for the management of the Compliance Risk.  However, many functions, businesses and operations in the Bank pose risks which cannot be sharply delineated.  Therefore, it is reiterated that the CCO and the CRO should work in close coordination.

 

The contents of the instant office order is to be brought to the notice of all the members of the staff concerned in Branches, Offices & Departments.
