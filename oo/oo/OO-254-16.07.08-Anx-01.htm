PART 1

PROCEDURE FOR HANDLING OF FOREIGN EXCHANGE TRANSACTIONS AT NON-DESIGNATED BRANCHES (‘C’ CATEGORY BRANCHES)

 

Foreign Exchange transactions mainly consist of Exports, Imports, Foreign Inward/Outward remittances, Purchase/Sale of Foreign Exchange like, Travellers Cheques & Foreign Currency Notes.

We give below the detailed procedure to be followed at the Non-Designated branches ('C' category branches i.e., all the branches other than Dadar, Ghatkopar, Andheri, Vashi and DG Pune). 

1.       SANCTION OF PACKING CREDIT (PRE-SHIPMENT FINANCE):

Pre-shipment finance is a working capital finance (inventory finance) extended to exporters at pre-shipment stage enabling them to procure/process/ produce/manufacture/pack the goods meant for export. This facility is commonly known to as Packing Credit.  Pre-shipment credit is governed by the regulations stipulated by the Reserve Bank of India and is extended at concessional rates as per RBI directives, which are subject to modifications.  Packing Credit can be granted either in Indian Rupees or in Foreign Currency.

Any exporter intending to avail Pre-shipment finance from the Bank, shall make necessary application to the Bank as per the Credit Policy and get the required limits sanctioned by the Head Office.  As per the current guidelines, after the HO sanctions the limits, completion of the loan Documentation, disbursal of the limits sanctioned and subsequent monitoring of the loan accounts have been attended to by the Foreign Exchange Department, Dadar.

i)     When the exporter  requests for disbursement of Packing Credit loan under the sanctioned limit, Application in FX(EXP) 04 – Packing Credit Drawal Application (format is available in the Exchange Server) is to be obtained from the exporter alongwith copy of the Export Order or Export Letter of Credit.  The details should be entered in a register by allotting a running serial number like EPC 001/08.  The portion “For the use of the branch” should be duly filled in by the branch in all aspects before forwarding the application to the Foreign Exchange Department for disbursal of the loan. The Account number of the customer is to be informed in the Application.  (As per the current guidelines, the portion “For the use of the branch” will be filled in by the FED until further instructions in the matter).                                                    

ii)    FED will verify the papers received from the branch and disburse the loan by Debiting Appropriate GL Head of the FED.  The proceeds will be credited to the customer’s account by FED through the Core Banking System under advice to the branch through the Exchange Server.                              

iii)   The Packing Credit Loan will be liquidated by extending post-shipment finance by way of Purchase/Discount/Negotiation of export documents. 

When the exporter tenders the documents against which EPC has been granted, the branch should follow the procedure stated in item (3) below.

       They should also inform the FED the fact of the outstanding EPC in the Branch Certificate without fail.

 

iv)    FED will extend the Post-shipment finance and liquidate the outstanding EPC liability in their records and credit the net proceeds to the customer’s account through Core Banking System under advice to the branch through Exchange Server.

v)     The branch should thereafter round off the outstanding EPC in their records after duly noting the Export bill reference number in the EPC register.

 

2.       EXPORT BILL ON COLLECTION BASIS:

i)     Bills should be submitted by the customer under cover of ‘Foreign Documentary Bill for Collection Covering Letter” in FX (EXP) 01. These forms should be supplied to the customer in sufficient number depending on their volume of business. This form is in set of 3 copies and the customer should present all the three copies with the documents and duplicate copy of GR/PP/SOFTEX forms, as the case may be.  The first copy will serve as covering schedule for forwarding documents to the Designated Branch (DB) concerned, the second copy is meant for the branch and the third copy should be returned to the customer duly acknowledging the documents submitted by him.

ii)    The Covering Letter is devised to facilitate the customer to give appropriate instructions to the bank and also includes terms and conditions for collection of documents. As such branches should ensure that all the documents as mentioned in the Covering Letter are invariably presented by the customer under cover of FX (EXP) 01 duly signed by the authorized signatory of the exporter firm/company.  In addition to this, the customer should mention at appropriate places on the Covering Letter the amount desired to be credited to their EEFC account upon realization of the bill and the name of the foreign bank through whom the bill is to be collected.

iii)   The bill should then be lodged in the Export Bill Register by allotting a running serial number, like, FBC 001/08,002/08.  The same should then be forwarded to the Designated Branch (D.B.) concerned retaining the second copy of the Covering Letter for the branch records.  Before forwarding the bill, the certificate portion in the Covering Letter is to be filled in by the branch. The account number of the exporter-customer should be furnished in the certificate portion so that DBs can credit the proceeds of the bill upon realisation direct to the customer’s account through Core Banking System under advice to the branch through Exchange Server.

iv)    After receiving the advice from the DB concerned as above, the branch should round off the outstanding bill in their records after duly noting the date of realization in the appropriate column in the Export Bill Register.

v)     DB concerned should pass the liability vouchers as below on the day of handling the documents:

Dr: Foreign Outward Bills for Collection

Cr: Bank’s Liability for Foreign Outward Bills for Collection

On realization of the bill amount, the above entry should be reversed.

 

3.       PURCHASE/DISCOUNT/NEGOTIATION OF EXPORT BILLS:

Financing of export bills takes place by way of Purchase/Discount/ Negotiation by the bank of the documents tendered by the customer. Sight export bills are purchased, Usance export bills are discounted and export bills drawn under Export Letter of Credit, both sight and usance, are negotiated.

Any exporter intending to avail post-shipment finance from the Bank, shall make necessary application to the Bank as per the Credit Policy and get the required limits sanctioned by the Head Office.  As per the current procedure, after the HO sanctions the limits, completion of the loan Documentation, disbursal of Loan sanctioned and post-disbursal monitoring is being attended by the Foreign Exchange Department, Dadar.

The procedure stated in paras 2 (i), 2(ii) and 2(iii) mentioned above is applicable for export bills meant for purchase/discount/negotiation of the export bills also, Except few changes/additions as under:

i)     The exporters should submit the documents under the cover of “Covering Letter for Foreign Documentary Bill for Purchase/Discount/ Negotiation” in FX (EXP) 02.

ii)    Appropriate running serial number with prefix of FBP, FBD, FBN is given.

iii)   The branch should tick appropriate column in the Branch Certificate without fail.

iv)    Documents should be forwarded to Foreign Exchange Department. FED will credit the net amount to the exporter's account through the Core Banking System by Debiting Appropriate GL Head of Foreign Exchange Department on the date of purchasing/discounting/negotiating the bill under advice to the branch through the Exchange Server.

Upon realization of the bill, FED will adjust the bill by reversing the original debit under advice to the branch. The branch should then round off the item in their records after duly noting the date of realisation in the Export Bill Register.

In the Export Bill Register supplied to the branches, few folios with separate heads may be allotted for recording the details of the bills which have been purchased/discounted/negotiated by the bank.

 

4.       OUTWARD REMITTANCES (OTHER THAN IMPORTS):

 

Banks are permitted to allow outward remittances (other than imports) for various transactions as permitted under FEMA which includes release of Foreign Exchange for undertaking travel abroad for various purposes viz., Private Visit (Basic Travel Quota–BTQ) Business Visit, Medical Treatment abroad, Education abroad  and other clean remittances like Gift, Donation ,Subscription to magazines etc.

Sometimes Outward Remittances take place by way of Issuance of Foreign Demand Drafts  also particularly in cases  where the amount to be remitted is not large like application/registration fee for  applying to foreign universities etc.

 

The Following Travel Related And Miscellaneous Remittances Facilities Are Available To Residents:

 

i) Private Visit:

Upto USD 10000 or its equivalents in other currencies in one financial year (April-March) for one or more private visits (Application in FX (REM) 02)

Foreign Exchange upto USD 10000 for private visit can also be released to a person who is availing of foreign exchange for travel outside India for any other purpose.

ii) Business Visit:

Up to USD 25000 or its equivalent thereof per business visit (Application in FX (REM) 01).

iii) International Conferences/Seminars/Trainings:

Up to USD 25000 or its equivalent thereof to a resident individual for participation in International Conferences/Seminars/Specialised Training irrespective of duration of the programme (Application FX (REM) 01)

iv) Employment:

Up to USD 100,000 or its equivalent thereof.  The amount also includes processing/assessment fees for overseas job applications (Application FX (REM) 01)

v) Emigration:

Foreign Exchange up to equivalent of USD 1,00,000 or amount prescribed by country of emigration.  This amount is only to meet the incidental expenses in the country of emigration.  No amount of foreign exchange can be remitted outside India to become eligible or for earning points or credits for immigration (Application FX (REM) 01)

vi) Medical Treatment:

Up to an amount of USD 100,000 or its equivalent or upto the estimate given by the doctor in India or hospital/doctor abroad.  A person who has fallen sick after proceeding abroad may also be released foreign exchange for medical treatment outside India (Application FX (REM) 01)

vii) Maintenance Expenses:

Up to equivalent of USD 25,000 for maintenance expenses of a patient going abroad for medical treatment/checkup abroad (Application FX (REM) 01)

If an attendant accompanies the patient going abroad for medical treatment/check up, foreign exchange upto equivalent of an amount of USD 25,000 for maintenance expenses of the attendant.

viii) Education Abroad:

Up to equivalent of USD 1,00,000 or the estimate from the institution abroad whichever is higher academic year (Application FX (REM) 01)

 

Note: Students going abroad for studies are treated as Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) and are eligible for all the facilities available to NRIs.  In addition, they can receive remittances upto USD 100,000 from close relative (as defined in Section 6 of the Companies Act, 1956) from India on self-declaration towards maintenance which could include remittance towards their studies also.

ix) Cultural Tours:

Dance troupes, artist etc. who wish to undertake cultural tours abroad, are required to obtain prior approval from the Ministry of Human Resources Development, Government of India, New Delhi (Application FX (REM) 01)

Foreign Exchange will be released on the strength of the sanction from the Ministry of Human Resources and Development (Department of Education and Culture) to be extended subject to the conditions prescribed therein.

 

LIBERALISED REMITTANCE SCHEME (LRS):

Under this facility resident individuals can remit upto USD 2,00,000 (USD Two Lacs) per financial year (April-March) for any permitted current or capital account transactions or a combination of both.  Remittances under this scheme can also be made towards gift to a person residing outside India or as donation to a charitable/ educational/religious/cultural organization outside India.  Remittances can also be made for certain other permitted purposes (Application FX (REM) 04 – format is available in Exchange Server).

 

Note:

  1. In addition to the above mentioned Applications, branches have to obtain FEMA declaration, Control Form A2 (formats for both are available in Exchange Server) and the required documentary evidence from the customers.

 

  1. For release of Foreign Currency Traveller’s Cheques and Currency Notes, branches have to send all the documents to the respective Designated Branches.  Documents related to other modes of outward remittances should be sent to Foreign Exchange Dept. directly. 

 

  1. Before forwarding the documents to Designated Branch (DB) concerned/ Foreign Exchange Dept, branches should enter the details in the Foreign Outward Remittance Register and ensure that adequate balance is available in the SB/CA account of the remitter.  After effecting the remittance or releasing the FC TC/CN or issuing the FDD, DB or FED will send advice to the branch through the Exchange Server.

 

  1. Any request from students for issuance of Foreign DDs for the purpose of applying to the foreign universities/educational institutions for pursuing higher studies abroad may be entertained even though they do not maintain any account with us.

 

  1. Requests from the customers for any outward remittances other than mentioned above should be referred to Foreign Exchange Dept. 

 

GENERAL PROVISIONS APPLICABLE TO ALL TRAVEL RELATED/ MISCELLANEOUS REMITTANCES:

 

  1. Payment in Rupees against sale of foreign exchange, cash upto Rupee equivalent of Rs. 50,000/- is acceptable.  When Rupee equivalent exceeds Rs. 50,000/- it should be paid by a crossed Cheque drawn on the applicant’s bank account, or crossed Cheque drawn on the bank account of the firm/company sponsoring the visit of the applicant, or Banker’s Cheque/pay Order/Demand Draft/NEFT/RTGS.
  2. Release of exchange in the form of currency notes is restricted upto USD 2000 or its equivalent in other currencies.  For travel to Iraq or Libya, currency notes upto USD 5000 may be released.  For travel to Iran, Russia and CIS countries (Former Soviet Union Republics) full exchange may be released in the form of currency notes.  
  3. In case the traveller does not undertake the journey, foreign exchange drawn is to be surrendered within 180 days from the date of the drawl of the foreign exchange.
  4. Unspent Foreign Exchange to be surrendered within 180 days from the date of returns.  However, a traveller can retain upto USD 2000 or its equivalent.  The entire unspent Foreign Exchange can be retained in the traveller’s Residents Foreign Currency (Domestic) Account.  There is no restriction residents holding foreign coins.
  5. If the remittance is made out of Exchange Earners’ Foreign Currency (EEFC)/Resident Foreign Currency (RFC) account, then remittance exceeding the Maximum Stipulated is allowed.
  6. No release of foreign exchange is admissible for any kind of travel to Nepal and Bhutan or any transaction with persons resident in Nepal and Bhutan.

 

5.       ENCASHMENT OF FOREIGN CURRENCY/TRAVELLERS CHEQUES, CURRENCY NOTES:

          A detailed booklet on “Operational Guidelines on Encashment of Foreign Currency Travellers Cheques/Currency Notes” has been supplied to all the branches. All the guidelines furnished in the said booklet should be followed scrupulously by the branches.

 

6.       HANDLING OF FOREIGN CURRENCY CLEAN INSTRUMENTS LIKE CHEQUES, BANKER’S CHEQUE ETC.:

i.     Whenever any instruments like Cheques, Banker’s Cheque etc drawn in foreign currency and payable abroad are tendered by the customers for collection, the same should be entered in “Foreign Currency Cheques etc. Collection Register" and forwarded to Foreign Exchange Department (FED), Dadar alongwith the branch covering letter (OBC Advice).  The name and account number of the customer are to be informed in the OBC Advice.

Upon realization, after the waiting period, FED will adjust the collection and credit the net proceeds after deducting commission to the account of the customer under advice to the branch through the Exchange Server. 

ii)    If the instrument is to be purchased, the branch should mention the same in the covering letter. They have to confirm that the purchase of the instrument is within the delegated powers of the branch. FED will debit appropriate GL Head at their end and credit the net proceeds to the account of the customer through Core Banking System after deducting commission and applicable interest for 15 days under advice to the branch through the Exchange Server. Upon realization, after the waiting period, the item will be adjusted by the FED under Exchange Server advice to the branch.

iii)   If the purchased instrument is returned by the foreign bank for any reason, the matter will be informed to the branch by the FED.  FED will debit the customer’s account with the amount paid in advance together with exchange difference between the Purchase Rate and the Sale Rate and out of pocket expenses, if any. Branches should ensure availability of adequate balance in the account of the customer to enable the FED to debit.

iv)    Few folios in the "Foreign Currency Cheque etc. Collection Register" may be allotted for noting down details of cheques which are to be purchased.

v)     In order to enable the FED to show the purpose of remittance in the R-Return, branches should obtain FX (REM) 04 “Purpose of Foreign Inward Remittance" (format is available in the Exchange Server) from the customer at the time of accepting the instrument for collection/purchase and should be sent to FED along with the instrument.

 vi)   Branches are advised to refer the PART 2 handout for complete details.

 

7.       OPENING OF IMPORT LETTER OF CREDIT:

          Letter of Credit mechanism is a widely used device to finance international trade.  It safeguards the interest of both the importer (buyer) and the overseas supplier (seller).  L/Cs are governed by provisions of Uniform Customs and Practice of Documentary Credits (UCPDC).

          For importing goods which are not appearing in the Negative List of the Import Export Policy of the Govt. of India, no separate Import Licence is required.  Branches need to get sanctioned required limits for their importer customers to open Foreign Letter of Credit. It is a non-fund based limit. In the event of the customer not paying in time the documents drawn under the L/Cs opened, it becomes a funded liability.

 

          Branches are to follow the following guidelines for opening Import Letter of Credit:

i)     Prescribed application in FX (IMP) 01 is to be obtained alongwith the Purchase Order/Proforma Invoice from the importer-customer. It should be appropriately stamped under the Stamp Duty Act. (Current stamp duty is Rs.100/-).  Additionally, Form A1 (printed format), FEMA Declaration,

Non-Negative List Declaration (both the formats are available in the Exchange Server) are to be obtained. 

In case the L/C is to be opened against Import Licence, Exchange Control Copy of the same to be obtained.

ii)    Before forwarding the  Application and other documents to the Designated Branch (DB) concerned for opening the L/C, details should be entered in the “Foreign Letter of Credit Opening Register" by allotting a running serial  number. The branches should certify in the “Branch Certificate” portion of the application that the L/C is being opened within the sanctioned limit. The account number of the customer is to be given in the certificate portion to enable the DB to debit Commission and other Out-of-Pocket expenses through the Core Banking System.

iii)   A photo copy of the Application is to be retained at the branch for its records. DB will forward a copy of the L/C after opening which should be kept alongwith the copy of the Application.

 

8.       HANDLING OF BILLS DRAWN UNDER IMPORT LETTER OF CREDIT:

i)     In our set up, Import Letter of Credits are opened by the Designated branches including the Foreign Exchange Department. Hence, documents drawn under the L/C will be received by the FED/DB concerned. After scrutinizing the same as per the terms and conditions of the L/C, FED/DBs will forward them to the branches concerned under their Bill Covering Schedule with suitable instructions.

ii)    Normally, our L/Cs provide for payment to the negotiating bank after the receipt of documents drawn as per the L/C terms.  Subject to receipt of documents drawn as per L/C terms, FED/DBs will be making the payment to the negotiating bank immediately in respect of sight bills which are payable on demand.  In the case of usance bills, FED/DBs will be making the payment on their due dates. 

iii)   The date of payment by the FED/DB will be indicated in the covering schedule with instructions to collect interest at the rate applicable to the category of the importer from indicated date till the date of payment by the importer customer or till the date of delinking/crystallization of the bill (explained in para (v) below) in the case of sight bills whichever is earlier.

iv)    In respect of those sight L/Cs where we are providing simultaneous reimbursement to the Negotiating Bank upon negotiation of documents, our Nostro account (Foreign Currency account maintained by the Foreign Exchange Department abroad) would have already been debited by the negotiating bank at the time of their negotiation.  FED/DBs will indicate this date of debit in covering schedule with instructions to collect interest from that date till the date of payment by the importer or till date of delinking/ crystallization of the bill whichever is earlier.

v)     Any undue delay in payment of the bill by the importer, will expose the bank to the exchange risk in addition to credit risk.  In order to avoid the exchange risk, the liability of the importer is crystallized in Indian Rupees by delinking the foreign currency element in the bill.  This procedure is called Delinking or Crystallization of unpaid import bills.

vi)    In the case of sight bills 10th day from the date of relative Bill Covering Schedule date is the date of delinking.  In the case of usance bill, the due date for the payment of the bill is the date of delinking.  This date will be indicated by the FED/DBs in the Bill Covering Schedule.

vii)   On receipt of documents from the FED/DB, branches should enter the bill in a Register and advise the customer about the receipt of documents.  The L/C openers are expected to make the payment upon presentation in the case of sight bills.  Documents drawn under L/Cs with usance terms are to be necessarily accepted by the openers and paid on the due date.  There is no need for our branches to affix stamp duty on such drafts, irrespective of the usance period.  When the customer approach branch for the payment of the bill, the branch should inform the FED/DB who will complete all the formalities about the payment and will send advice to the branch through Exchange Server.

viii)  If the bill is not paid by the importer on or before the 10th day in the case of sight bills or on the due date in the case of usance bills, FED/DBs will delink the bill by debiting appropriate GL head (Forced Loan) of the branch concerned.

ix)    Unpaid delinked bills should be handled by the branches from the credit angle.  Loans and Advances Department, Head Office should be kept informed about the matter.  In case the documents are not retired or remain unaccepted even upon arrival of the goods (the arrival dates of the carrying vessels could be ascertained from the Clearing Agents), in the interest of the Bank, the branches have to arrange for clearance of the goods and store them under their custody duly insuring for appropriate value.  If the goods are not cleared immediately, the same may get damaged, lost, pilfered, etc.  The demurrage charges on the goods will reduce the value of the security.  Further, the insurance cover may also get expired on the 30th day after the arrival of the goods.  Custom Authorities may also auction the goods if not cleared with in the time limit prescribed by them.

x)     Applicable overdue rate of interest should be charged on the unpaid delinked overdue bill till the date of recovery of our dues from the importer. 

 

9.       HANDLING OF IMPORT DOCUMENTS RECEIVED ON COLLECTION BASIS:

In an import transaction, where there is a good established relationship between the exporter and importer of the goods, documents are sometimes received by the bank or importer directly from the overseas banker or the exporter of the goods.

 

A)  DOCUMENTS RECEIVED BY THE BANK:

i)     In such cases, branches should forward the entire set of documents to the DB concerned/FED for processing at their end.  DBs/FED would enter the documents in their system and send them back to the branch concerned. In the case of usance documents, Stamp Duty payable on the usance draft (Bill of Exchange) will also be indicated in the covering schedule by the DB/FED.  

ii)    Branches should enter the same in a Register and advise the importer-customer about the receipt of the documents. At the time of retirement of the bill, the branch should inform the DB about the same who will complete all the formalities in connection with the retirement like debiting the customer’s account with the bill amount and other charges. After receiving confirmation in this regard from the DB, branches can release the documents to the customer by discharging/endorsing on the reverse of the original Bill of Lading/Air Way Bill.

B)  DOCUMENTS RECEIVED BY THE CUSTOMER:

i)     At times the importer clients may receive the import documents directly from their overseas suppliers covering goods consigned directly to them. In such cases, the importer may approach the branch only for effecting the remittance to the overseas supplier towards settlement of the transactions. All Import Trade Control and Exchange Control Regulations are applicable to these transactions also. Though such remittances appear to be simple clean remittances, as an Authorised Dealer, it is the responsibility of the bank to ensure compliance of the said Regulations.

ii)    Application in FX(REM) 01 alongwith copy of the Invoice, Bill of Lading or Air Way Bill, Payment Instructions, Control Form A1, FEMA Declaration, OGL Declaration or  Exchange Control copy of the Import Licence if the import of goods is covered under licence should be sent to the DB concerned for effecting the remittance.

iii)   As per the current Regulations, the value of the import bill should not be more than USD 1,00,000.

iv)    All the Know Your Customer (KYC) guidelines with regard to the importer-customer viz., the genuineness of the address furnished by him, line of activity etc should be strictly followed by the branch.

 10.    BOOKING OF FORWARD PURCHASE/SALE CONTRACTS:

Forward Contract is used by both exporters and importers as a method of covering exchange risk due to adverse movements in the exchange rates over a period of time. Forward Contracts are governed by FEMA regulations, FEDAI rules and also bank’s internal credit and procedural guidelines.

Forward Purchase Contracts are booked by the exporters and the importers book Forward Sale Contracts. Branches need to get sanctioned suitable limits for the customers who may like to book Forward Contracts.

Branches should obtain prescribed "Application for booking forward Purchase Contract – Export" in FX (MISC) 01 or "Application for booking forward Sale Contract – Imports" in FX (MISC) 02 alongwith the documentary evidence.  Certificate portion of the application should be invariably signed by the branch official before forwarding the papers to Foreign Exchange Department, Dadar.

PART 2

 

OPERATIONAL GUIDELINES ON HANDLING OF FOREIGN CURRENCY CLEAN INSTRUMENTS PURCHASE & COLLECTION

 

Clean Instruments:                                   

 

AN OVERVIEW:

1.    In Foreign Exchange Business, clean instruments are Demand Drafts, International Money Orders, Banker’s Cheques drawn by the foreign banks or Indian banks on their correspondent banks abroad, Travellers Cheques in foreign currency, personal cheques of NRIs drawn on their foreign currency account abroad, etc.

 

2.    The clean instruments segment constitutes a substantial portion of foreign inward remittance.  Proper and effective handling of such clean instruments by the branches will naturally augment the foreign exchange business turnover of the bank and eventually lead to increase in the deposit figures of the branches.

 

3.    Any mishandling of such instruments, though having small face value, is generally a complaint prone area and may lead to loss of valuable clientele in the long run. Branches, should, therefore, pay due attention to proper and effective handling of clean instruments.

 

4.    Clean instruments may broadly be classified as those denominated in foreign currencies and denominated in Indian Rupees.

 

5.    Foreign currency clean instruments may be further classified as those:

a)  payable outside India

b)  payable in India at other banks

 

6.    Indian Rupee instruments are those drawn by Exchange Houses/ Companies from the Middle East countries and banks abroad on other banks in India.

 

7.    In domestic business, all clean instruments undergo similar treatment, procedure and accounting system.  Whereas in respect of clean instruments comprising foreign inward remittances, the treatment and procedures to be adopted vary under each category which is enumerated below.

 

8.    FCP and FCC:

Depending upon whether the clean instruments are purchased or handled on collection basis, these are referred as Foreign Cheques Purchased (FCP) or foreign Cheques for Collection (FCC).

 

Section - A

 

FOREIGN CURRENCY INSTRUMENTS PAYABLE ABROAD

 

9.    Instruments of this category are in the form of:

a.  Travellers Cheques

b.  Drafts

c.  International Money Orders

d.  Personal cheques or any other similar instruments.

 

10.   Such instruments may be handled on collection basis or purchase.  Purchase is subject to the powers delegated to branches and other sanctioning authorities as communicated by the Head Office from time to time.

 

HANDLING INSTRUMENTS ON COLLECTION BASIS:

11.   Instruments received from the customers should be entered in the “Foreign Currency Cheques Collection Register” and forwarded to the Foreign Exchange Department (FED) under cover of OBC Advice (Code 106).  The name and the account number of the customer has to be informed in the OBC Advice.

 

12.   If the instruments received are for foreign currency deposit accounts, like FCNR, EEFC, RFC, RFCD etc., and the currency of the instrument is not other than the permitted currencies for such deposit schemes, same should be mentioned in the OBC Advice (Code 106) in bold letters without fail. Similarly, if any portion of the instrument if contemplated to be credited to EEFC account of the customer after realisation, branches should mention about the same in the OBC Advice without fail.  (All Foreign Currency accounts viz. FCNR, RFC, RFCD, & EEFC accounts will be maintained only at the Designated branch concerned for operational reasons).

 

13.   Upon realization of the instrument, FED, subject to waiting period concept, will adjust the collection item at TT Buying rate and credit the net amount to the customer’s account after deducting commission and postage through the Core Banking System under advice to the branch through the Exchange server.

 

14.   Where the amount is contemplated to be invested in foreign currency deposits like FCNR/RFC/RFCD/EEFC, FED will credit the same to the DB concerned at notional rate   under Exchange Server advice to the branch concerned.

 

PURCHASE:

15.   Instruments drawn in  our Position Currencies (i.e. currencies in which our Bank is maintaining Nostro accounts abroad) viz., US Dollars (USD),Great Britain Pound (GBP) & Euro (EUR) only can be purchased. In case of TCs guidelines applicable for encashment of TCs should be strictly adhered to. 

 

16.   In the OBC Advice branches should mention that the instrument is to be purchased and the purchase is as per the sanction from appropriate authority where a portion of the amount is contemplated to be credited to the EEFC account of the customer, the same should be mentioned in the OBC Advice.

 

17.   FED will credit the net proceeds after deducting commission and applicable interest for 15 days to the customer’s account through the Core Banking System under advice to the branch through the Exchange server.

Where a portion of the amount is not converted into Indian Rupees for the purpose of crediting to EEFC account, collection of interest should be for the funds converted into INR and not for EEFC component.  However for EEFC component, commission should be collected as applicable to collection of instruments.

 

Notes:

1.  As interest component is loaded in TC Buying Rate, no interest is to be collected while encashing foreign currency TCs.

2.  Interest as per this sub-paragraph is to be collected for purchase of all foreign currency clean instruments payable abroad (other than Travellers Cheques).

 

18.   Upon realisation, subject to waiting period concept as detailed in Para 26, FED will adjust the realised item in their system under Advise to the branch concerned.

Where a part of the cheque amount is to be kept in the EEFC account, FED will credit the same to the DB concerned at Notional Rate under Exchange Server advice to the branch concerned.

 

HANDLING OF INSTRUMENTS DENOMINATED IN A CURRENCY OTHER THAN THE HOME CURRENCY OF THE DRAWEE CENTRE:

19.   Under Exchange Control Regulations normally Authorised Dealers are permitted to maintain NOSTRO accounts in various countries in the home currency of the centre only i.e., U.S. Dollar account in USA, Sterling Pound account in UK, etc.  As per the clearing systems prevailing in various countries abroad the instruments drawn in home currency of the country undergo the process of clearing and full amount is credited to the NOSTRO Account of FED.

 

20.   If the instrument is denominated in a currency other than the home currency of the drawee centre [i.e. like US Dollar instrument payable in UK], the same undergoes the process of presentation and collection by the correspondent bank at the drawee centre and remittance of proceeds to the NOSTRO Account at another centre.  The same are therefore subject to collection and remittance charges which are substantially high with minimum around USD 25 to USD 50 per instrument.  Branches are advised to take into account such charges while handling such clean instruments payable abroad and recover the same upon realisation.  The same should be brought to the notice of the customers before handling such instruments and with their consent in writing to bear such collection charges.

 

21.   Purpose of Remittance:

Clean instruments in foreign currencies are similar to Foreign Inward Remittances into India.  Purpose of remittance represented by the instrument is to be ascertained from the customers who are tendering the same to the branch for collection/purchase in IF (REM) 05 without fail and forward the same to FED alongwith the instruments.

 

22.   Foreign Inward Remittance Certificates [FIRCs]:

Upon realisation of the clean instruments, beneficiaries are entitled for Foreign Inward Remittance Certificates [FIRCs] or Encashment Certificate [in the case of TCs].  FIRCs will be issued by the FED upon receipt of a request letter from them in this regard. Where the amounts are credited to NRE accounts, FED will NOT be issuing FIRCs.

 

Note: If the inward remittance or realisation of foreign exchange exceeds Rs.15,000 in value, FIRC should be issued on security paper.  Upto Rs.15,000 certificates may be issued on our Bank’s letter head.  A commission of Rs.50 for FIRCs issued on Security paper and Rs.25 for FIRCs issued on Bank’s letter head is to be collected from the beneficiary.

 

WAITING PERIOD:

23.   Under the cases explained in para 19 & 20, the correspondent bank will remit the proceeds to the Nostro account of FED with another bank in some other country only after the realisation of proceeds after deducting their charges for collection.  Whereas if the instrument is denominated in the currency of the drawee centre/country, the Nostro correspondent will instantly credit the amount as per our collection order to the FED’s Nostro account and present the instruments simultaneously in the clearing (local/national).  Though, the Nostro account gets credited so, the paying bank has a time limit for returning the instrument for reasons like insufficient of funds in the drawer’s account etc.

 

24.   In some countries like USA, the paying bank will make available the paid instrument to the drawer [the account holder] of the instrument who has the statutory right to claim the amount paid for reason like the instrument bearing forged signature etc.

 

25.   In such circumstances, the Nostro correspondent will debit the account of FED with back date value [i.e., the same value date on which the account was originally credited] and additional charges for returning the instrument unpaid.  In order to safe guard the interest of the bank in avoiding parting with the funds in the case of collection or not reversing the liability in the case of purchase, the waiting period concept is followed.  Accordingly, FED shall treat the instrument realised only after the expiry of the waiting period after the date of credit to our Nostro account (value date) and adjust the realisation after this waiting period.  This period varies from country to country and with various correspondent banks.   

 

26.  Waiting Period – Number of Days:

a. USD instruments payable in New York, New Jersey and Connecticut.

 

SEVEN U.S. working days from value date.

b. USD instruments drawn outside New York, New Jersey, Connecti- cut but within USA.

 

ELEVEN U.S. working days from value date.

c. GBP instruments payable in United Kingdom.

 

NINE U.K. working days from value date.

d. Other Foreign Currency instruments Payable in respective countries.

 

FIFTEEN working days from the value date.

 

Important Note: As per U S Laws, in respect of fraudulent encashments in USA, the U S Federal law is very clear that the time limitation for claim is between 12 to 36 months from the date of encashment depending upon the nature of forgery/ fraud.  In other words, for cheques with fraud on the front of the instrument, the paying Bank can claim the amount upto 12 months [even after our Nostro account getting credited on realisation] & for fraudulent endorsement on the reverse of the cheque, they claim upto 36 months from the date of credit to our account.

 

CHECK LIST FOR HANDLING INSTRUMENTS DRAWN ON USA

27.  Drawn on USA:

a.  The depositor of the foreign instrument should be well known to the branch, preferably an established customer and not a walk in customer.

b.  Branches should not handle third party/endorsed instruments drawn in any foreign currency either on collection basis or for purchasing/ discounting.

c.  The instrument should be examined under ultra-violet lamp for any alteration or erasures.

d.  Normally, cheques/drafts to be handled for collection/purchase should be for smaller amount as it involves fairly longer time to realise the proceeds.  Therefore, branches should be on guard when cheques are presented for large value especially towards advance remittance for exports, which should normally be received through TT channel.  (Remittances received through SWIFT).  The beneficiary has to be advised about the above US Laws.  Wherever cheques for huge amounts [say USD 5000 and above] are handled on collection/purchase, the beneficiary has to be explained the contents of US Laws.

e.  Exporters receiving huge amounts by cheques/drafts may be advised to insist for TT payments from their clients instead of cheques/drafts in view of the above US Laws regarding materially altered/forged/bogus cheques.

 

28.   Immediate Credit Facility:

In order to avoid complaints from our customers, specially from NRIs and Exporters about the delay in realisation and crediting of US Dollar clean instruments tendered by them for collection, the following system with regard to handling of clean instruments denominated in US Dollar and payable in USA, is to be followed.

 

29.   In the case of clean instruments payable in USA for amounts upto USD 5,000 or its equivalent in other currencies handled on collection basis for the well known and creditworthy customers, branches may request the FED to extend the facility of Immediate Credit upon the proceeds of the instruments are credited in our Nostro account, without waiting till the expiry of the normal waiting period stated at para 26 above.  This facility should be made available judiciously to our well known and credit worthy customers, especially Exporters and NRIs, after obtaining a suitable request letter from them in this regard.  However, they have to undertake in this letter that in the event of the Cheque returned unpaid, they will be refunding the amount with interest and also the exchange loss, if any, on account of the difference between the Purchase and Sale.

 

30.   Branches forwarding such collections to FED should request our FED in the Cheque forwarding letter itself, to release the proceeds immediately on sighting the pass sheet credit, alongwith a letter obtained from the beneficiary in this regard [as per Annexure I].  In case where the beneficiary requests for the immediate Credit Facility after the instrument has already been forwarded to the FED, the branch should obtain the prescribed letter and forward the same to our FED requesting to release the proceeds upon sighting the pass sheet credit.

 

 

Section - B

 

FOREIGN CURRENCY INSTRUMENTS DRAWN ON AND PAYABLE AT ANY OTHER BANK IN INDIA:

31.   These are DDs drawn by private Exchange Houses or banks abroad expressed in foreign currency and payable at other bank in India.  Cover funds for these instruments are received and/or will be received by the drawee banks and payment is effected by them ultimately.  Branches should therefore handle such instruments on collection basis without applying any firm exchange rates.

 

32.   The drafts for which payment is desired in Indian Rupees for the full value should be presented to the drawee bank across the counter if it is in the same centre and payment in Rupees to be obtained.  If the drawee bank is an upcountry bank the same should be forwarded to that centre as per the existing procedure and payment in Rupees should be obtained. 

 

33.   If a part of the proceeds of the draft is desired to be credited to EEFC account, the branches should clearly mention the same on the covering schedule indicating the total amount in foreign currency contemplated to be credited on EEFC account and request the drawee bank to issue draft in foreign currency favouring our bank.  If the instrument is sent to the drawee centre, suitable instructions are to be given to them by the branch concerned. 

 

34.   General:

a.  Purpose of remittance is to be ascertained from beneficiaries in IF (REM) 05 and the same should be forwarded along with the instrument.

b.  FIRC: Since the foreign currency cover funds are being accrued to the drawee bank, request for FIRC should be obtained and forwarded to the drawee bank requesting them to issue FIRC. 

Such FIRC requests should be sent simultaneously since retrieving the transactions at a later date may pose problems to the paying banks and eventually lead to delay, complaint etc.

c.  In view of urgency if the branch has decided to extend immediate credit against such instruments, the same should be at provisional rate leaving sufficient margin and subject to compliance with credit norms, delegated powers etc.  No foreign currency purchase transaction should be put through or reported to FED in such cases.  Interest and commission as applicable to the domestic instruments should be collected in such cases.  The immediate credit so extended should be liquidated from Rupee proceeds received from drawee bank or our offices. 

 

35.   FCNR/RFC/RFCD: Where the instruments are meant for FCNR/RFC Schemes, branches should send them to FED for collection and FED in turn will present the instrument to the drawee bank and arrange to obtain either a counter DD in foreign currency in favour of our bank drawn abroad or get the foreign currency funds credited to its NOSTRO account by TT.  The drawee bank’s charges for acceding to such request will be debited to the branch concerned.  Designated Branch should part the FCNR Deposit Receipt only upon receipt of Credit from FED.

 

 

Section – C

 

RUPEE INSTRUMENTS DRAWN ON OTHER BANKS IN INDIA:

36.   These are instruments drawn by Exchange Companies or correspondent banks on other banks in India favouring customers of our bank.

 

37.   Rupee draft drawn by Exchange Companies on other banks in India should not be purchased under any circumstances and handled on collection basis only.

 

38.   Handling of Rupee drafts will be similar to other inland instruments payable by other banks in India.

Purchase of Rupee drafts, drawn by banks is subject to the discretionary powers of the branches/sanctioned limits and collection of charges and interest as applicable to the inland instruments.  However, collection of charges may be waived if the proceeds are to be deposited in our Bank's term deposits.

 

39.   In these cases, FIRCs are to be issued by drawee banks only.  Where FIRC is required, requests are to be sent to drawee banks simultaneously since retrieving the transactions at a later date may pose problems to the paying banks and eventually delay, complaint, etc.

 

 

Section – D

 

GENERAL INSTRUCTIONS

40.  Crossing/Endorsements:

The procedures for affixing special crossing stamp as well as endorsements on the instruments are similar as in the case of domestic instruments.  Branch stamps for noting FCC/FCP numbers are to be affixed on the back side of the instrument in order to avoid defacing the contents.  In the case of instruments drawn on USA, such endorsement stamps and number stamps are to be affixed on the middle of the backside leaving the end portions for the use of New York correspondents.

 

41.   There may be occasions when some customers tender foreign currency instruments for  very small amounts, say, USD 7/- or Singapore Dollars 5/- or GBP 5/- etc. In such cases, branches need to inform them that after realization of such instruments, net amount that will be credited to their accounts may be insignificant because the minimum charges for collection is about Rs.250/- per instrument.

 

RETURNED UNPAID INSTRUMENTS:

42.   When any instrument is returned unpaid, branches should proceed as follows: If the transaction was handled on collection basis the instrument may be returned to the payee after recovering charges as applicable to collection of the instrument, actual out of packet expenses, and foreign bank’s return charges, if any.

 

a.  If the instrument was purchased, after collecting interest and charges, the unpaid instrument may be returned to payee after recovering the amount paid in advance as well as actual out of pocket expenses/foreign bank’s returning charges, if any.  Where, purchase of foreign Currency was reported earlier, the recovery of amount paid in advance should be at TT selling rate of the respective foreign currency.

b.  If no withdrawal was allowed or the proceeds have been deposited in term deposit account [i.e., instrument was purchased at PAR], branches should recover the advance amount paid and actual out of pocket expenses, charges as applicable to collection of the instrument and/or foreign bank’s return charges by debiting payee’s account or encashing term deposit before maturity as the case may be.

c.  If proceeds were earlier invested in deposits under FCNR/RFC/RFCD Schemes, branches should close the respective deposit accounts before maturity and reverse the entries passed [at the time of opening of account] and recover charges as applicable to collection of the instrument.

 

 

Section - E

 

EEFC:

43.   Where the TCs were released earlier by the banks to the debit of EEFC account and the treveller surrenders unspent TCs, the entire amount is eligible for credit to EEFC account.

 

44.   At times the client may have received foreign inward remittances through other banks.  Such paying banks will issue their DD representing EEFC component of such remittances with their covering letter stating that entire proceeds of the DD is eligible for credit to EEFC account of the beneficiary.

 

45.   In both the above cases entire proceeds of the instruments are eligible for credit to the EEFC Account concerned.  In such cases TCs/DDs can be handled only on collection basis and should not be purchased.

 

Branches while forwarding such TCs/DDs alongwith cheque covering schedule to FED should note to mark thereon that entire proceeds to be credited to EEFC account on realisation.

 

Note:

NRIs, Foreign Nationals and Foreign Tourists are not eligible to maintain EEFC account.
