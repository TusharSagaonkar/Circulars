Forex Circular No 123 dated 27th May, 2019

 

1)  Opening of Seafarers Non- Resident Deposit Account

2)  Opening of NRO Accounts by Long Term Visa (LTV) holders

3)  Export & Import of Indian Currency

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

I.   Opening of Seafarer’s Non-Resident Deposit Account:

1.     Attention of branches is invited to Forex Circular No 115 dated 18th October 2016 wherein we have furnished salient features of Non-Resident Deposit accounts (NR accounts) of various types. These accounts are normally opened by Non-Resident Indians (NRI) or Persons of Indian Origin (PIO).

2.     There are occasions when seafarers approach us for opening NR accounts. A seafarer is a person sailing or working on a ship. They will be on ship for a considerable period of the year on account of their job requirements. As per the Reserve Bank of India guidelines, such persons are also eligible for opening NR accounts.

3.     We furnish below the KYC documents to be obtained from such persons for opening the NR deposit accounts:

             i.    Self-attested copy of valid passport. Photocopies of the passport must include the pages indicating last date of arrival in India;

           ii.    Self-attested copy of valid visa OR Continuous Discharge Certificate (CDC). The Certificate must indicate last date of arrival in India;

         iii.    Seafarer's Declaration provided in our Account Opening Form for NR accounts should be signed by the seafarer;

          iv.    Initial Work contract;

            v.    Last wage slip (should not be more than 6 months old).

 

II.    Opening of NRO Accounts by Long Term Visa (LTV) holders:

1.     Govt of India is issuing Long Term Visa (LTV) to Pakistan, Bangladesh and Afghanistan nationals coming to India on valid travel documents i.e. valid passport and valid visa, and seeking permanent settlement in India with a view to acquiring Indian citizenship.

 The following categories of persons are eligible for grant of LTV:

             i.    Members of minority communities in Pakistan/ Bangladesh/ Afghanistan, namely, Hindus, Sikhs, Buddhists, Jains, Parsis and Christians.

           ii.    Women from Pakistan /Bangladesh married to Indian Nationals and staying in India, and Afghanistan nationals married to Indian nationals in India & staying in India.

         iii.    Women of Indian origin holding Pakistan/ Bangladesh/ Afghanistan nationality married to Pakistan/ Bangladesh/ Afghanistan nationals and returning to India due to widowhood/divorce and having no male members to support them in Pakistan/ Bangladesh/ Afghanistan.

          iv.    Cases involving extreme compassion.

2.     Reserve Bank of India vide their A.P. (DIR Series) Circular No 24 dated 20.03.2019 has advised that banks may allow only one Non-Resident Ordinary (NRO) Account for a citizen of Bangladesh or Pakistan, belonging to minority communities in those countries residing in India and who has been granted a Long Term Visa (LTV) by the Central Government. The account will be converted to a resident account once such a person becomes a citizen of India within the meaning of the Citizenship Act, 1955.

3.     The NRO account can also be opened if such a person has applied for LTV which is under consideration of the Central Government. In such cases, the account will be opened for a period of six months and may be renewed at six monthly intervals subject to the condition that the individual holds a valid visa and valid residential permit issued by Foreigner Registration Office (FRO)/ Foreigner Regional Registration Office (FRRO) concerned.

4.     Branches which are opening such NRO accounts should report their details to the Foreign Exchange Department, Dadar to enable the latter to report them in turn to the Ministry of Home Affairs (MEA) on a quarterly basis.

III.  Export & Import of Foreign Currency:

1.     In terms of Forex Circular No. 88 dated 05.02.2015, a person may take or send out of India to Nepal or Bhutan and bring into India from Nepal and Bhutan , currency notes of Government of India and Reserve Bank of India for any amount in denominations up to Rs 100/-. Further, an individual may carry to Nepal or Bhutan, currency notes of Reserve Bank of India denominations above Rs 100/-. i.e., currency notes of Rs 500 and Rs 1000 denominations, subject to a limit of Rs 25,000/.

2.     Reserve Bank of India vide their A.P.( DIR Series) Circular No 24 dated 20.03.2019 has advised that an individual travelling from India to Nepal or Bhutan may carry Reserve Bank of India currency notes in Mahatma Gandhi (New) Series of denominations Rs 200 and /or Rs 500/ subject to a total limit of Rs 25,000/-. Instructions regarding currency notes of Governmental of India and Reserve Bank of India for any amount in denominations up to Rs 100/- remain unaltered.

IV.   All the Branch in-charges are advised to note the contents of this circular and also bring them to the notice of the staff members and customers. Clarifications, if any, may be obtained from Foreign Exchange Department, Dadar.
