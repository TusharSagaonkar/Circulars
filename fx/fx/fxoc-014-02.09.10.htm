Forex Circular No. 14 dated 2nd September, 2010

 

Import Finance in Foreign Currency

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 

 

Import transactions are normally carried out by our importer-customers in the following manner:

1) Opening of Letters of Credit subject to our normal terms & conditions.

2) Receiving import documents on collection basis.

Upon receipt of documents either drawn under L/C or on collection basis, the importers provide funds in their account for meeting payment of the bill depending upon its tenor. In the case of imports involving capital goods such as machinery etc, bank sanctions need based Term Loans at the request of the customers.

In order to avail finance at the internationally competitive rates, some importers avail the Buyer’s Credit facility instead of utilizing their own funds or avail the Term Loan. Buyer’s Credit facility is a part of the Trade Credits which are governed by Reserve Bank of India guidelines with regard to the tenor of the credit and the cost of arranging for the same. Under this facility, funds are borrowed from an overseas financial institution or bank against the guarantee (Letter of Undertaking) given by the importer’s bank or any other well known bank in India on behalf of the importer.

As our bank is not having any direct tie –up with any overseas bank as of now, we are arranging the Buyer’s Credit facility for our customers through Bank of India, Mumbai against their guarantee. The total costs of any Buyer’s Credit will comprise the interest charged by the overseas lender, which shall not exceed LIBOR +2% as per present RBI guidelines, plus Bank of India charges and our Bank's charges.

For about a year, we have been arranging Buyer’s Credit to our customers   through Bank of India, Mumbai. For arranging the same, a portion of the L/C limit sanctioned to us by them has been earmarked.

Of late, on account of the competitive Rates of Interest, many importer-customers are desirous of availing the Buyer’s Credit to meet their import commitments. But we are unable to provide the same for want of adequate availability of limit with Bank of India for this purpose. Moreover, some of the importers for whom we have arranged this facility are of the opinion that the total costs are on the higher side.

Keeping this in view, we were of the opinion that we ourselves can extend finance in foreign currency to our import clients at competitive rates without resorting to Buyer’s Credit facility.

We are glad to inform all the branches that in terms of Board’s approval in its meeting held on 18.05.2010 and 22.07.2010, it has been decided to extend the facility of Import Finance in Foreign Currency to our Importer-customers subject to a maximum exposure of Rs. 25 Crore (Rupees Twenty Five Crore Only) for the bank.

The pre-requisite for extending the import finance by us is availability of adequate foreign currency resources with the bank. Presently, we do not have a good number of Foreign Currency accounts such as FCNR, EEFC RFC etc in our fold. Hence, our Forex Treasury Department will arrange for the required foreign currency funds using a combination of certain Money & Forex markets operations.  

       

We furnish below the details of this new loan product:    

 

1)    Name of the Product: The product will be known as FOREIGN CURRENCY CREDIT TO IMPORTERS (FCCI)

 

2)    Purpose: To provide Short Term finance to importers against their import commitments either against L/Cs opened by us on their behalf or against import documents drawn on them received on collection basis subject to fulfillment of Reserve Bank of India guidelines.

 

3)    Eligible customers: Since funds required for extending the facility of this product will be generated from the Money & Forex market operations which are associated with the normal market risks, the product, therefore, will be made available only to credit worthy customers.

 

4)    Eligible Currencies: USD, EUR, GBP

 

5)    Period : Maximum 180  days or as per the  prevailing RBI guidelines  

 

6)    Rate of Interest: Maximum LIBOR (London Inter Bank Offered Rate) PLUS 2% p.a or as per the prevailing RBI guidelines.

 

7)    Charges: Charges levied by the Forex Treasury Department for arranging the foreign   Currency funds PLUS our charges as applicable to opening of Import L/Cs for the period of the loan and SWIFT charges.

 

8)    Repayment:

 i)    The loan amount in Foreign Currency shall be repaid by the importer on the due date.

ii)    If it is not repaid on the due date, the loan amount will be crystallized into Indian Rupees at the Bill Selling rate /Forward Sale Contract rate. Higher rate of interest depending upon the category of the borrower will be charged from the date of crystallization till the recovery of the loan amount. 

       In cases where crystallization has taken place, opening of further L/Cs or sanction of fresh loans will be on obtaining specific permission from the higher authorities.  

9)    Other Conditions:

 

i)             This facility will be extended only to those importer-customers who have been sanctioned with Import L/C limit by the bank with the permission to avail Import Finance in Foreign Currency. Requests from the existing L/C limit holders desirous of  availing this facility may be referred by the branches to the Head Office for consideration. All the required formalities for availing the limit viz., Obtaining of collaterals, Creation of mortgage (if any), Documentation etc should have been completed. The import finance extended shall be within the sanctioned limit.

ii)           The currency exposure of the importer on account of the import finance shall be fully hedged by booking Forward Sale Contract. For this purpose, required permission shall be obtained from the higher authorities if the L/C limit sanctioned has no provision for booking of Forward Sale contracts.

iii)         The foreign currency loan will be sanctioned by the Foreign Exchange Department upon receipt of discrepant-free import documents drawn under our L/Cs, sight or usance, for making payment to the overseas negotiating banker. Importers may avail this loan for retirement of import bills received on collection basis also.

iv)          The maximum period of the loan is 180 days at the first instance. It can be subsequently extended to further periods subject to prevailing RBI guidelines on Trade Credits.

v)           The importer-customer needs to inform the bank in writing for availing this loan preferably at the time of opening of the L/C itself. The amount required and the period of finance shall be indicated.

vi)          On being referred by the Foreign Exchange Department, Forex Treasury shall convey the Rate of Interest, charges etc.

vii)        Presently, the disbursement of the loans under this new product is restricted only to our Dadar branch for operational reasons. The accounting for the transaction for the purpose of Trial Balance shall be at the Bill Selling Rate/Forward Sale Contract rate on the date of availing. The newly created Sub-head “Foreign Currency Credit to Importers” shall be debited and the credit shall be given to the “Mirror Nostro Account “of the currency concerned.

viii)      Forex Treasury shall maintain required Forex accounting for these transactions at their end.

                                                                                    

All the branches are advised to bring the contents of this Circular to their Importer- customers and make popular this new loan product of our bank.
