I.     GOODS AND SERVICE TAX (GST):

 

Goods & Services Tax (GST) has been implemented w.e.f. 01st July, 2017, under the provisions of the Central Goods and Service Tax Act, 2017, the Integrated Goods and Service Tax Act, 2017 and corresponding State GST Acts. GST is a comprehensive indirect tax levied on the supply of goods and services, subsuming multiple erstwhile indirect taxes.

In the case of banks and financial institutions, the supply of service constitutes the predominant taxable activity. The applicable GST rate on such services is 18%, comprising CGST @9% and SGST @ 9% (or IGST @ 18% in case of inter-state supplies).

Certain transactions, including sale of dead stock, sale of gold, or disposal of repossessed or recovered assets, attract GST at rates applicable to the respective goods or services as per the relevant rate notifications under the GST law.

B.  Concept of CGST, SGST and IGST:

i)   Unlike Service Tax, GST is a tax which is collected by Central and State Government simultaneously. It is Income for both the Governments. Therefore, GST is charged separately under three heads viz.

·        CGST/Central Goods and Services Tax/Central Tax – which goes to the Central Government

·        SGST/State Goods and Services Tax/State Tax – which goes to the State Government

·        IGST/Integrated Goods and Services Tax/Integrated Tax – which initially goes to the Central Government and later on certain portion is given to the State Government

ii) Intra and Inter State Supply

For Intra-State Supply (supply within the state) CGST and SGST are levied. For Inter-State Supply (supply in other state) IGST is charged. The IGST rate is equivalent to the combined rate of CGST and SGST as it consolidates both the Central and State components of tax into a single levy collected by the Central Government.

iii)      Place of Supply of Service

Whether CGST and SGST or IGST is to be levied is decided on the basis of Location of Supplier of service and Place of Supply of Service.

In case of banking,

a.   Location of Supplier is Branch from where the service is provided.

b.   Place of Supply of service is,

i.             Address of recipient of service (Customer) on the records of the bank.

ii.           If the address of recipient of service (Customer) is not on the records then the place of supply shall be location of the supplier of service i.e. the location of the branch.

 

If Location of Supplier (branch) and Address of Customer on records are in same state then it is Intra-State Supply and hence CGST (9%) and SGST (9%) are charged.

If Location of Supplier (branch) and Address of Customer on records are in different states then it is Inter-State Supply and hence IGST (18%) is charged.

 

iv)      Case Scenarios for understanding

Case 1: A Customer staying in Vashi, Maharashtra, having account with Vashi branch, goes on a trip to Gujarat and approaches our Vadodara Branch for RTGS/NEFT services.

In this case, Location of Supplier of Service is Vadodara Branch, Gujarat and

Address of Customer on record is Vashi, Maharashtra. Since both are in two different states i.e. Gujarat and Maharashtra, Vadodara Branch shall charge IGST on such service.

 

Case 2: In above case, if customer has no account with any branch of the bank that means bank has no address on records for that person. In that case location of supplier of service is Vadodara branch and since no address of customer available on record his location shall be Vadodara, Gujarat. In such case, branch shall charge CGST and SGST on such service.

 

Case 3: A customer staying in the same state avails any service from branch in the same state then CGST and SGST shall be charged by the branch. This is the most common scenario in case of banks.

 

For Individual customers, address shall be their residential address. And for others, address shall be the place of registration or incorporation.

 

C.  Concept of Input Tax Credit (ITC):

i)            The Bank provides various services to customers which are liable to GST. In the course of providing such services, the bank procures several input goods and services, on which it incurs expenditure and GST applicable on it. So, GST is Payable on Bank’s income (i.e. outward supply) and GST paid by the bank on expenditure is Receivable (i.e. inward supply).

 

E.g. Bank provides Passbook to customers at Rs. 200 plus GST @ 18%. i.e. Rs. 236/-

 

Bank’s Liability: Income

200

GST Payable

36

 

 

Bank purchases and prints Passbook from ABC Co. and Cost to the bank for making Passbook is Rs. 100. ABC Co. raises bill to bank of Rs. 100 plus GST @18% i.e. Rs.118/-

 

Bank’s Asset: Expense

100

GST Receivable (ITC)

18

Bank’s Final Liability to pay Government is,

GST Payable

36

Less: GST Receivable

18

Final payable in Cash

18

 

Therefore, expenses incurred by bank for providing output service to the customer, bank is eligible for Input Tax credit.

 

ii) Bank incurs GST on various Expenses such as Rent, Computer software/hardware, furniture and fixtures, Printing and Stationery, ATM related charges and other operational expenses and claims Input tax credit of the same which is then utilised against the bank’s output tax liabilities.

 

iii) There are certain Expenses which are specifically restricted from ITC eligibility and are known as “blocked credit”. GST paid on such expenses cannot be claimed as credit and therefore, GST paid on such expenses forms part of the cost which shall be fully debited to the Profit and Loss account.

List of Expenses which are ineligible for ITC:

·        Motor Vehicles having seating Capacity of Less than 13 used for other than business purpose

·        Insurance, Servicing, repairs and maintenance of such vehicle

·        Foods, beverages, outdoor catering for consumption of employees

·        Membership of Club, health and fitness centre

·        Travel benefits extended to employees on vacation

·        Works contract service in relation to Immovable property including construction, repairs, renovation, addition, alteration, and re-construction.

·        Construction, repairs, renovation, addition, alteration, re-construction of Immovable Property on his own account.

·        Tax paid under composition scheme

·        Goods or Services used for personal consumption (may be of employees, directors or others)

·        Goods Lost, stolen, destroyed, written off or given as gift or free samples

 

iv)      To claim Input tax credit, there should be a taxable income. In case of Banks, all the Income is not taxable (refer 2(iii) below). Interest earned by the bank on loans given to the customers is not taxable. This type of interest is major part of the bank’s Income. Thus, bank cannot claim the Input tax credit available fully but only proportionately to taxable income. However, an option that an entity can claim the input tax credit up to 50% of the all eligible (except ineligible ITC) input tax credit has been provided to banks only.

 

v) Our bank has opted for the option given by the law of availing 50% ITC and charging balance 50% to the profit and loss. It means out of all the GST paid on expenses, we use only 50% for setting off the GST liability.

 Normal Charge and Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM):

i) When a supplier of service charges GST on such service and pays to the government it is called as Normal or Forward Charge.

ii) When GST registered person is receiver of service as per the list declared and amended from time to time, then receiver has to pay GST to the government under Reverse Charge Mechanism.

iii) Under GST, for certain transaction with unregistered vendors, the bank is required to pay GST under RCM at 18%.

Accordingly, all Departments/Branches are encouraged to prefer procurement of goods or services from registered vendors to ensure seamless tax compliance and credit availability. In cases where procurement from unregistered vendors (URD) becomes unavoidable due to operational requirements, the concerned department sanctioning the expenditure should factor in the applicable tax impact under RCM while evaluating quotations and finalizing vendors.

iv)  Bank is required to pay GST under Reverse Charge mechanism (RCM) on following items:

1. Rent of branches/premises paid to Unregistered Owners

2. Payments to Advocates

3. Payment to Arbitrators

4. Commission to DSA/DDS agents, Business facilitator, Recovery agent

5. Payment for Non-corporate Security Services

6. Premium paid on PSLC

7. Foreign Bank charges/remittance

 

D.  INPUT SERVICE DISTRIBUTOR (ISD):

i)      Our Bank is also registered as ISD w.e.f. 01st April, 2025 having GST number “27AAAAA0300L2ZB”. The ISD registration is mandatory for organization having multiple GST numbers under same PAN to transfer Input Tax Credit amongst them.

ii)    As per the provisions of the Act, input tax credit availed on Services billed to Head office but used by all the branches shall be transferred to other branches\states in certain ratio through ISD mechanism. ISD

iii)   In case of Common Services, the departments are required to take invoices from Vendors for such Common Services on ISD number.

iv)  The departments procuring services from vendors need to ensure that whether the services provided by such vendors are common for more than one state or confined to only one state. If the service is common among two or more states then it is mandatory to raise invoices on this ISD GSTIN on ongoing basis.

v)    The department shall take the following actions:

a.  Check the vendors related to their specific departments.

b.  Confirm the nature of service provided by the vendor and whether the vendor is providing service to all the three states or specific to one state and accordingly identify common vendors.

c.  Inform to the identified common vendors about our bank’s ISD GSTIN 27AAAAA0300L2ZB and ask them to raise further invoices on this ISD GSTIN.

 

II.   INSTRUCTIONS TO BRANCHES:

 

1.  Collection and Feeding of GST Number of Customers:

 

i)   GST registration is mandatory if turnover of a business/profession exceeds Rs.20 Lakh in a financial year. However, a person having turnover of less than 20 lakhs can register for GST voluntarily where his turnover does not exceed the limit prescribed.

GST number is issued only to a person carrying on any business/profession etc. Others will not have GST number. E.g. if a salary earner has account with us, he will not be having a GST number.

 

ii)  If customer is registered under GST, obtain his GST number along with a photocopy of GST registration certificate. GST number is required for the purpose of issuance of invoices and filing of GST returns.

 

iii)  The option to update the GST registration number is provided in OMNI 3.0 under Master>>GST Module>> Customer GST Mapping.

 

iv)  If entered GST number is invalid or cancelled, the same shall be updated or deleted in OMNI 3.0 through Manager Code under Master>>GST Module>> Customer GST Mapping.

 

v) GST number is a 15 digit number, E.g. 27AAAAA0300L1ZC, which is explained as below,

         27                           AAAAA0300L                     1ZC

State Code                        PAN                 Check code

 Branches should check accordingly the GST number before accepting from customers and also on government site at www.gst.gov.in>search taxpayer>by PAN or by GST number if the GSTIN is valid and active.

 

vi)                GST number will be in the name of the business i.e. in case of a Partnership Firm, it will be in the name of Firm and not in the name of a Partner. For a Company, it will be in the name of the Company and for Proprietorship Firm, in the name of the Proprietor.

 

vii)              The GST number in OMNI is linked to Customer Master. If a proprietor is having 2 proprietorship firms, then he may have 2 GST numbers or 1 GST number (it is at the option of proprietor). In such a case, while updating GST number in OMNI, branch should take care and update GST number to proper customer ID.

 

viii)             Wherever customer provides GST number, the same shall be immediately and correctly updated in OMNI. Non-feeding or wrong feeding of GST number of customer in OMNI will attract penalty under GST Act, as all the returns will be uploaded by placing reliance on GST numbers entered in the customer master.

 

ix)                Branches are requested to take specific care if GST number contains O (letter ‘O’) or 0 (number ‘0’), I (letter ‘I’) or 1 (number ‘1) etc.

 

x)  Before collecting GST number, Branches should ensure that, the state code in GST number and the address of the customer is matching. E.g. customer having address in Maharashtra should have GST number starting from 27 etc.

 

2.  GST on Income:

i)   GST is applicable to various charges collected from customers including amount collected towards reimbursement of actual expenses incurred.

ii) Refer latest Service Charges Manual issued by the bank for GST applicable to different charges.

iii)                GST is not applicable to following items:

·    Amount collected towards Share Capital

·    Interest Income on all kinds of loans and advances

·    Income from investment (Interest / Discount / Dividend etc)

·    Insurance claim received (if any) for loss of dead stock

·    Exchange gain on foreign currencies between Bank to Bank transactions

·    Bad Debt written off recovered

·    Reversal of any provisions

iv)                Please note that GST is applicable for sale of Gold or other Assets during the course of Recovery of loans, sale of Dead stock, sale of scrap, money conversion, Nominal Fees and Entrance Fees (including PNCPS fees) also.

v) The staff/officer concerned will be held responsible for non-collection or short collection of GST on charges levied to customers.

 

3.  Income Module:

i)      Various charges collected by branches should be entered in a proper manner in OMNI to ensure generation of invoice and reporting of GST transactions in GST return.

ii)    There are certain charges which are run by I.T. Dept periodically (e.g. minimum balance, SMS charges etc) some charges are run by system automatically at the time when transaction takes place (charges for withdrawal from ATM) or some charges are run by branches through specific programme (e.g. TOD charges etc) provided in the system. Some charges are to be collected by selecting specific activity (e.g. payorder, BGISSUE etc). In all such cases GST is automatically collected by system.

iii)   Apart from above, all other charges collected are entered manually (e.g. service charges for loans, Duplicate payorder, e-payment of taxes etc) by branches. For collecting such charges branches should use Income Module provided in OMNI 3.0 under Master>>GST Module>>Income Module. Branches can collect charges by entering the account number of concerned customer from whom charges are to be collected.

Please note that concerned staff/officer will be held responsible for any loss to the Bank due to the same such as GST credit is not passed on to customer, any fine / penalty for non reporting etc.

v)    No Module is provided till date for charges collected in cash by I.T. Dept. Hence charges collected in cash has to be entered manually in the system. Invoice will not be generated for such transactions. As far as possible branches should avoid collection of charges in cash.

6.  Reversal/Waiver of service charges collected:

Our Bank collects various charges from customers. Sometimes, Branches reverse the charges collected due to various reasons such as waiver on customer's request, wrongly collected or excess collected etc.

It may be noted that, Branches shall reverse GST along with the charges if the reversal of charges is on account of waiver within same month.

If charges recovered in a month are subsequently waived, reversal of GST thereon will not be permissible.

In cases other than waiver, such as charges wrongly collected or collected in excess etc., Branches can reverse the charges along with the GST amount thereon.

Branches should reverse the charges collected (either with GST or without GST) through the reversal module only. The option is provided in the OMNI on following path:

GST Module > Reversal of Income (Credit Note).

 

Instruction to be followed while passing entry in reversal module:

·        Charges collected date: Branch should enter original date of collection of charges which now Branch wants to reverse.

·        Invoice no. : Enter the original invoice no. Branch can get the invoice no. from Report> GST Report > Branch wise GST Report or Report> GST Report> Cust. Invoice Report.

After entering the above details, the fields of Customer Name, GST No., Address will be auto populated.

·        PL Account ID: Branch shall enter the PL code where the charges shall be debited (the field is generally auto populated).

·        Cr Account ID: Branch shall enter Br. Code and Account No. of the customer where the charges are to be credited (the field is generally auto populated).

·        Charges Amount: In this column, amount of charges originally collected by the Branch against the invoice no. specified above will be reflected directly after entering original Invoice number and date.

·        Amount to be reversed: Branch shall specify the amount of charges to be reversed to the customer. e.g. if Branch has collected Rs. 1000/- towards Loan Service charges and borrower is granted 40% waiver in such case Branch will enter Rs. 400 as amount to be reversed. Please note that the amount to be reversed cannot exceed the charges originally collected.

·        CGST/SGST Amount: The CGST and SGST or IGST amount will be auto calculated. Here, Branches shall note that GST will be reversed in case of waiver of charges to customer if within same month, GST shall be reversed only in cases where charges were wrongly collected or excess collected. In case of reversal due to waiver, branch shall make CGST/SGST/IGST as "0" (zero) if not reversed in same month.

·        Reason Code: Pick the correct reason carefully from the options available.

·        Narration: Mention narration briefly containing reason of reversal.

 

Branches should carefully check Charges amount, PL Code & Cr. Account ID from GST Report before passing reversal entry.

Use TAB while passing entries.

7.  GST paid on expenses :

i)      Vendors charge GST to the Bank on various goods and services procured by the bank. The bank is eligible to claim Input Tax Credit (ITC) of GST paid, subject to compliance with GST provisions.

ITC can be claimed only when vendor files its returns and invoice issued to the bank is reflected in GSTR-2B. To ensure availability of ITC step in point ii below shall be followed. For expenses which are released/paid by Bills Dept centrally, required compliance for such expenditure will be done by Bills Dept. centrally.

ii)    For expenses such as telephone bill, electricity bill, all petty cash expenses, cleaning charges, rent of premises etc. incurred by branches themselves, branches / departments are required to make the following compliances:

a. Monthly fixed expenses (like Rent) shall be paid on or before the End of the month.

b. Obtain Tax Invoice for every expense incurred.

c.  Ensure that the name of our Bank and its appropriate GST Number is mentioned on the invoice correctly.

d. Ensure that GST of the vendor is shown separately in the invoice if the shopkeeper/vendor is registered under GST. If vendor’s GST registration number is printed on the invoice, it indicates that the owner is registered under GST. In such a case, branches should insist that GST is shown separately in the invoice (for goods/services which are exempt from GST, GST will be NIL even if vendor is registered and if the vendor is registered under composition scheme then GST will not be charged by the vendor). If the shopkeeper/vendor has charged GST in invoice but has not printed his GST number, then insist that the GST number be mentioned in invoice.

e.  I.T. Department has provided Expenses Module in OMNI. The entry for any kind of expenses is to be made through “Expenses Module” only. In case of any deviation, the staff/official concerned will be held personally responsible for any loss/penalty to the Bank.

f.   While making entry, separate entry for each expense is to be made on daily basis. E.g. If rent is paid to 4 persons who are joint owners of the premises, then 4 separate entries will be made for rent.

g.  While making entries, branches should ensure following.

i.       Select GST Y/N properly. If the vendor has GST number then “YES” should be selected even if vendor has not levied GST in the invoice. Branches/Departments can mention GST amount as ‘0’ in such cases.

ii.     Mention vendor name i.e. the name of vendor from whom goods are purchased or services are obtained.

iii.   Mention GST number of the vendor in the GST number field (some branches are mentioning our Bank’s GST number in this field which is incorrect).

iv.   Mention Invoice number and Date correctly (some branches mention entry date in the invoice date field, some branches are mentioning voucher number in the invoice number column or entering 0 or 1 etc.)

v.     Mention HSN/SAC code in “HSN Code” field. (Many branches are entering either 0 or 1 in that field). This field is applicable only if GST is levied in bill.

vi.   Mention Invoice value, GST amount as mentioned in the invoice. Branches can adjust rounding off difference in Invoice value. GST amount should be exactly tallied with the bill/invoice. Where vendor is having GST number but has not charged GST in bill Branches should mention GST as ‘0’.

vii.  If GST is levied in the invoice then branches has to show the GST amount. We have come across certain instances where branches have not entered GST amount in “Expenses Module” even if GST is mentioned in the bill/invoice.

viii.          If branches are booking any expenses where invoice is not raised in our Bank’s name e.g. few branches are paying insurance premium of case file accounts etc. wherein the invoices are raised on the name of borrower etc. In such cases branches should mention GST as ‘0’ even if the same is charged in the invoice. In such cases total amount including GST will be debited to concerned PL head and ITC will not be available to the bank.

ix.   If branches are paying the expense directly by debiting to customers account (e.g. stock audit bill) and not by debiting to our P&L then branches should not use “Expenses Module”.

x.     Branches have to ensure that entries for all expenses irrespective of GST levied or not (except interest on deposit, reversal of service charges collected from customers, quarterly provisions) are passed through “Expenses Module” only. Repeated instructions were issued by Accounts Department from time to time, inspite of the same we have come across few instances where branches had passed the entries manually.

iii.   The amount entered in CGST and SGST field will be debited to separate GL Head created for the same. GL head created for CGST amount is “CGSTREC1”, and for SGST, it is “SGSTREC1”.

iv.   Once branches have entered the required data in fields of Expenses Module, following entry/voucher will be passed in OMNI.

2.  Obtain Tax Invoice from Vendors:

i)   To claim input tax credit under GST, we should have a TAX INVOICE issued and signed by vendor containing the following details:

a) The word “TAX INVOICE”.

b) Name, Complete address and GST No. of vendor.

3.  Release of Bills within stipulated time:

i.     All Departments releasing the payments of various bills through Bills Dept are requested to ensure that bills pertaining to a month are released on or before 10th day of next month.

ii.   Departments are also required to obtain MSME certificate from every vendor and where vendor is not registered as MSME a clarification to this respect shall be obtained mandatorily on letterhead of the vendor.

iii. The same is required to ensure smooth filing of GST return and to take input credit of the same along with compliances under GST Act, Income Tax Act, MSME act and various other statutes.

iv. For MSME vendors, time limit for making payment is 15days (45days if agreed by the parties) as per MSME Act. Such vendors shall be paid within the time limit otherwise penalty is imposed under the relevant statutes.

v.   Please note that delay in release of Bills beyond the stipulated time will result in mismatch in GST returns of our Bank and vendor which may result in disallowance of input tax credit.

 

4.  Loss to the Bank if the tax is not deposited by the vendor:

                            i. If the vendor who has charged and collected GST from us but has not paid to the Government, Bank will not be able to claim the input tax credit (set off) of the same. It will result in increased cost (i.e. loss) to our Bank.

                           ii. In cases where vendor is unable to deposit the GST to Government or fails to update GST returns, the responsible department shall collect back the GST amount paid to such Vendor or withhold further payments to such vendors.

                         iii.  In order to avoid such instances, it is suggested that, in case of regular parties, a clause in agreement should preferably be added that Bank will retain some amount if it observes that vendor is not depositing, or depositing late, the amount of GST collected by him to Government, & further if a vendor continues to make such default, Bank can terminate the contract.

 

5.  Advance payment of expenses:

 

i)   The input credit is available only at the time of completion of work. Hence if the advance payment is made to any vendor, Bank can claim input credit only when final invoice is raised by the vendor even though Bank has paid GST at the time of making advance payment. Therefore, if any payment is released in advance, obtain receipt voucher at the time of payment and when the work is completed, obtain tax invoice from Vendor for total amount and send the same to Bills Dept.

ii) Till the final tax invoice is not received, payment will be kept under ‘Advance for Expenses A/c’ by Bills Department.

 

6.  Refund of payments:

Obtain credit note from vendor in case of Refund of payments / deduction from invoice amount. Credit note will contain all the details of tax invoice except that the heading will be “Credit Note” and not “tax invoice” and the reference of original invoice no. will also be there.

 

7.  Lapse of input tax credit:

It may be noted that input tax credit of GST charged in invoice will lapse if not claimed by 30th October from the end of Financial Year. Our bank avails ITC only after making payment to the vendor. Hence departments are required to make sure the payment is released within the period.

E.g. if an invoice dated 01.08.2024 is received for payment after filing the return for October 2025 (Date mentioned under the Act is 30/11/2025, but can be claimed only till the last date for filing return for October i.e. 20th Nov), then Bank will not be able to claim input tax credit of GST levied in invoice and the same will be a loss to the bank.

Hence before obtaining invoice, ensure that the date of invoice is not beyond the period as above. If the same is beyond the period as above, special approval from Board will be required as GST levied will form additional cost for the Bank.

 

8.  Separate Bill for each state and separate bill for Departments:

 

In case of a common service vendor for all branches and/or departments, Invoice on Bank’s ISD number is required to be obtained. However if vendor is common but services are specific to branches or department invoice shall be obtained on GST number of the concerned state of branch/department.

9.  Expenditure which are common in nature:

In case of expenditure which are common in nature and cannot be allotted to a branch or apportioned among branches, it is necessary to distribute the input tax credit of GST charged on such invoice to 3 states in the ratio of turnover in each state. In such cases, invoice will be obtained in the name of Head Office on ISD number. Invoices on ISD can only be issued in respect of services. For goods, invoices shall be issued on respective GSTN only. Head Office will play the role of Input Service Distributor for the branches located in the state of Gujarat and Karnataka.

 

10.      Expense Module for entries of expenses:

Departments and regional offices are required to record expenses in OMNI only through “Expenses Module” provided by the I.T. Department. In case of any deviation, the staff/official concerned will be held personally responsible for any loss/penalty to the Bank.

 

B.  FOR INCURRING PETTY CASH EXPENSES:

1.    Certain expenses such as telephone bill, electricity bill, petty cash expenses, sweeper’s salary etc. are incurred by departments themselves. In such cases, all the instructions relating to entry of expenses to Branches as per point no. II(7)(ii) above will also be applicable to Departments while making petty cash expenses.
