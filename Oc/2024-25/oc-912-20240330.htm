Circular OC No. 912  dated 30th March, 2024

Management of Internal/Office Accounts

Policy and Standard Operating Procedure

__________________________________________________________________

 

 

I.    Background

1.  The Reserve Bank of India has been continuously emphasizing the need for establishing a sound and effective control mechanism for opening and operating internal/office accounts.

2.  A few examples of internal Accounting Heads are Sundry Creditors, Sundry Debtors, REM-SUS, POADJ, LNCLGCTS and Suspense Account. There are many more such accounts.

3.  Some of the prescriptions of the RBI are the need for a Board-approved policy in UCBs (such as ours) in respect of the internal/office accounts governing their opening, operations and management, and inclusion of the area of reconciliation pertaining to them in the audit policy.

4.   The areas of concern generally pointed out by the RBI in the management of Internal/office accounts are as under,

(i)      Internal accounts are being misused to bypass internal/external guidelines.

(ii)     There is a lack of periodic review of user roles assigned to internal accounts.

(iii)   There is an absence of periodic reconciliation of long outstanding entries in internal/office accounts which results in fraudulent transactions.

II.  An illustrative list of RBI’s concerns concerning internal/office accounts which need to be addressed is given below.

1.  Need for a Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

(i)      There should be an SOP/ Framework for such accounts.

(ii)     Authority for opening internal/ office accounts and user roles should be defined.

(iii)   The policy on Risk Based Internal Audit (RBIA) should cover the reconciliation of internal accounts.

2.  Need for robust systems and controls

(iv)   There should be system-based checks and controls to prevent the routing of unauthorised or unrelated entries in the internal/office accounts.

(v)    There should be a system of exception report generation in CBS to monitor and report abnormal transactions in internal accounts and to generate a list of internal accounts and their balances.

3.  Adequate monitoring and review

(i)      Status and operations of internal/ office accounts should be reviewed periodically and subjected to audit.

(ii)     Reconciliation of long outstanding entries in inter-branch, suspense and sundry accounts must be done periodically. (The RBI has observed that there are some entries which are pending for several years which cannot be tolerated).

(iii)   The Board/Audit Committee of the Board should provide oversight regarding the operations of internal accounts.

(iv)   User roles assigned for operating internal accounts should be reviewed periodically.

4.  There should be no bypassing of AML/CFT guidelines under any circumstances

The following wrong practices must be avoided

(i)    Cash repayment of a loan is first credited to the bank's internal account (sundry creditors) and then transferred to the borrower's loan account, thereby bypassing identification and reporting of cash transactions (CTR) to FIU.

(ii)   Parking the balances of KYC non-compliant inactive accounts in a separate internal account "Account closed under non-complete KYC" and not providing any interest to such balances.

5.  There should be no evergreening of accounts

The following practices must be avoided.

(i)    In the case of a loan against fixed deposit (FD) accounts, shortfall of collateral security vis-à-vis outstanding loan is met by debiting sundry creditors account.

(ii)   NPA accounts are regularised by using one or more internal accounts.

(iii) Bypassing the system-based identification of NPA accounts by using internal accounts.

6.  Avoidance of Fraudulent transactions.

Non-reconciliation of Digital channel GL, an internal GL account, for a significant period leading to fraudulent transactions wherein the account is debited for crediting failed UPI transactions.

7.  The RBI has mentioned some more practices of Unauthorised usage which must be avoided

(i)      Internal accounts debited for disbursal of loans.

(ii)     Internal accounts used for parking funds and routing customer transactions.

(iii)   The debit balances in internal accounts are mapped as liabilities in GL, netted against the credit balance of other liabilities GLs while finalising the accounts.

(iv)   P&L accounts inherently in the nature of only receipts have neither debit freeze nor any restriction of keying in reference ID while being used for reversing transactions.

III.   Framing and adoption by our Bank of a framework and an SOP on Management of Internal/Office Accounts under “Policy on Accounting Principles, Accounting Codes & procedure”

1.      In deference to the observations and prescriptions of the RBI, our Bank has incorporated the framework on the Management of Internal /Office Accounts in the “Policy on Accounting Principles, Accounting Codes & procedure” and it has been approved by the Administrators Committee.

2.      The SOP has been approved by the Chief Executive Officer.    

3.      As per the policy, the authority for the creation of any new accounting head in the General Ledger is vested only with the Accounts Dept.     The General Manager, Accounts Dept, will approve the opening of new account heads and necessary instructions will be given to the IT Dept for opening new account heads.

4.      Role of Branches/Departments - 

(i)    Branches/ Departments will do reconciliation of Internal Accounts every month w.e.f. April 2024 and shall submit a Monthly Certificate (as per Annexure-A) to the Accounts Dept. on or before the 10th of the subsequent month confirming that all the Internal Accounts have been reconciled and reviewed by them and no irregular transactions have been observed. Branches/Departments to note that format of “Annexure-B” is to be kept on record with them for Audit purpose only.

(ii)  Branch/Department Incharges are advised to avoid unauthorised operations of internal accounts. It shall be their responsibility to reconcile, monitor, and prevent unauthorised operations on internal accounts maintained at their respective branch/department.

(iii)In case of the internal accounts used as Parking Heads such as LNCLGCTS-1, SRYCR, TDCLGCTS-1 etc., Branches/Department should ensure in case of entries in parking heads that if they remain outstanding for more than 3 working days, then the branches/departments should immediately inform this position to Accounts Dept. with justification. Further Branches/Departments are advised to follow below mentioned guidelines

a.    The GL head LNCLGCTS-1 should be used only to realize the instrument, when the recovery in the loan account is by way of cheque issued in favour of the Bank. For e.g. Abhyudaya Co-op. Bank Ltd. A/c.___________________ (Name of the Borrower).

 

b.    The GL Head TDCLGCTS-1 should be used, only when customer wishes to keep deposit in the Bank and do not have Saving/Current account in the Bank. The cheque should be drawn from his own account with other Bank and should be in favour of our Bank.

c.     The GL head Sundry Creditors (SRYCR) should be used, only when name of customer and account number differs or name or account number not mentioned in the pay-in-slip.

(iv)             Reconciliation of GST-related internal accounts is not separately required as these are tallied with the GST portal. The TDS-related Internal Accounts are also tallied every month; hence separate reconciliation of the same is also not required. Reconciliation in the case of Cash and Petty Cash are also not required as they are tallied daily.

(v)  A list of branch-wise internal accounts will be furnished to all branches/departments by the Accounts Dept.

(vi)             Branches should generate the exceptional reports of internal accounts on daily basis and Branch Incharge should check and ascertain the genuineness of transaction in internal accounts and certify the same and keep it ready for record and audit.

5.      Role of Accounts Dept.

(i)    The Incharge of the Accounts Dept. will be responsible to

a.    reconcile all Internal Accounts including Parking Heads maintained at the Accounts Dept.,

b.    reconcile the count of total Internal Accounts on a monthly basis,

c.     review the Inoperative Internal Accounts with zero balances on a Quarterly basis,

d.    ensure closure of Inoperative Internal Accounts which are not required, and

e.    reconcile long outstanding entries and their settlement periodically.

(ii)  The Accounts Dept. will also ensure the monitoring of all Internal Accounts regularly.

(iii)The Accounts Dept. will review the monthly certificate received from Branches/Departments and will take necessary steps for corrections in case of observance of irregularities.

(iv)             The list of exceptions to disable cash transactions in Internal Accounts will be reviewed by the Accounts Dept., on a periodic basis.

6.       Role of Information Technology Dept.

(i)  The I.T. Dept. shall make arrangements for the generation of Branch/Dept.- wise exception reports, daily, of all Internal Accounts excluding Accounts where manual intervention is not permitted. This report shall be made available to all the Branches/Departments.

 

(ii) Further, the I.T. Dept. shall make arrangements for dual authorisation for all types of transactions of Rs. 50,000/- and above in all the Internal Accounts. The authority to change the threshold limit of Rs. 50,000/- will remain only with the Accounts Dept.

(iii)             I.T. dept. shall disable cash transactions in all the Internal Accounts except Petty Cash, Cash shortage in ATMs, Current Accounts with PSBs & Pvt. Sector Banks (only for CCD), Cash Dispenser, Advance Locker Rent, Cash Excess, Cash-in-hand, Sundry Deposits, REM-SUS, and PO-ADJ.

(iv)             I.T. Dept. shall also debit freeze all the P&L accounts in the nature of Income/Receipts. Any request for debiting any Income account, if at all, should be scrutinised by the Accounts Dept. A debit entry to an income/receipt account will be permitted by the I.T. Dept. only after receipt of instructions in that behalf from the Accounts Dept.

(v)The I.T. Dept. shall submit data of all Internal Accounts every month to the Accounts Dept. w.e.f. April 2024 on or before the 5th of each subsequent month.

7.       Role of Inspection Department

(i)  The checking of the above-mentioned processes i.e. reconciliation, monitoring and rationalisation shall be part of the scope of Concurrent Audit and it should be reviewed by the Audit Committee every quarter. At the same time, the internal audit shall also review the need and utility of the existing types and the number of internal accounts regularly. A Risk-Based Internal Audit shall cover the reconciliation of these Accounting heads.

(ii) The findings of the periodic review of the position pertaining to opening of internal/office accounts, the purpose for which used and their reconciliation should be placed before the Audit Committee and deviations or instances of malpractices if any must be dealt with strictly.

IV.  Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) duly approved is placed in the public folder of Accounts.

 

V.   All the branches/departments for whom Internal Accounts are necessary for their day-to-day transactions should also review their continuing need and the standards of their operations, and report to the Accounts Dept. As per their findings or recommendations, Accounts Dept. will take necessary action thereon.

 

VI.  All the branches, offices and departments and the members of the staff concerned are hereby advised to follow the instructions herein scrupulously.
