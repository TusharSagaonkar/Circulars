I.  PAN/Form 97 applicability

A.   Section 262, read with rule 159 and 161, prescribes that every person shall intimate PAN while entering into following transactions (Rule 159 and Rule 161):

 

1.   Cash deposits/withdrawals aggregating to Rs. 10 Lakhs or more in one or more account of a person

2.   Opening of an account other than Time deposit and Basic Saving Bank Deposit Account

3.   Time deposit exceeding Rs. 50,000 or aggregating to more than Rs. 5,00,000 in a FY.

4.   Opening of a current Account or a Cash Credit Account.

 

In absence of PAN, declaration in Form 97 can be submitted, limited to the following transactions:

 

a.     Opening of Bank Accounts: All accounts other than Basic Saving Bank Deposit Accounts

 

b.     Opening of new Time Deposits (Fixed Deposits): Time Deposits exceeding Rs. 50,000 or aggregating to more than Rs. 5,00,000 during a financial year where the person doesn’t have a PAN.

 

The use of Form 97 is strictly disallowed and a PAN is mandatory for point 1 and 4.

 

B.   Key Changes in the declaration/form 97 as compared to form 60 is as follows:

a.    The declaration cannot be accepted if declarant’s estimated income for the Tax Year exceeds maximum amount not chargeable to tax (for TY 2026-27, the amount is Rs. 4,00,000 as per the default new regime).

 

b.    If a transaction is undertaken in joint names (saving account in joint name or FD in joint name), part D of the form requires the total number of persons and the specific percentage share of the declarants (equally if nothing specified by the customer).

 

c.    The branches shall retain the Form 97 for a period of six years from the end of the financial year in which the transaction was undertaken.

II.        TAX DEDUCTION AT SOURCE

The bank has obtained a Centralized TAN for the Bank as a whole i.e. MUMA01872D from 01.04.2016 for payment of TDS, filing of TDS Return, issue of form 131(TDS Certificates, erstwhile Form 16A), obtaining form 121 (erstwhile Form 15G/15H), Lower deduction certificate under Sec 395 etc.

 

Section 397(2) prescribes that tax deduction shall be made on valid (correct and should not be inoperative) PAN and where valid PAN is not available:

a.   Tax shall be deducted at 20% or higher rate as per section

b.   Tax shall be collected at higher of twice of rate (cannot exceed 20%) as per section or 5%.

 

Inoperative PAN i.e. PAN not linked with Aadhar: Compliance with Sec. 397 read with Sec. 262 -

A PAN is considered inoperative if the PAN is not linked with Aadhar number in case of an Individual assessee. An inoperative PAN is similar to not having PAN and TDS provisions will apply to an inoperative PAN as if the assessee has no PAN. In such cases TDS shall be deducted at 20% as if PAN is not available.

The accounts department will provide a list of Inoperative PANs to branches. Branches are required to check the list and deduct tax at 20% on payment to such customers. Status of PAN can also be checked on the Income tax portal.

An inoperative PAN can be made operative again by linking PAN with Aadhar on Income Tax portal. If any customer links PAN to Aadhar number then tax shall be deducted at normal rates from payments made after the PAN is linked.

 

Form 121 cannot be accepted if the customer has in-operative PAN.

3.  Deduction at Rates in Force (Special Rates)

Deduction of tax is required to be done in special cases i.e. where customer files Form 125 with the bank.

Form No. 125 is a declaration furnished by an eligible senior citizen account holder (More than 75 years of age) to the Bank containing details of income and deductions, if any.

To identify the eligibility of customer providing Form 125, all of the following conditions must be satisfied:

a)   is a resident of India for the relevant year;

b)  has attained the age of 75 years or more at any time during the relevant year;

c)   has income consisting solely of pension and interest, with no other source of income whatsoever, including rental income, capital gains, or business income; and

d)  receives both the pension and the interest income through an account or accounts maintained with this Bank

 

When the form 125 is filed by the eligible Senior Citizen to the bank, the bank is obliged to:

(a) Compute the account holder's total income by aggregating the pension and interest income;

(b) Apply the deductions available under Chapter VI-A and the rebate available under Section 87A, in accordance with the tax regime selected by the account holder, and obtain documentary evidence of deductions claimed where the old regime is opted for;

(c) Deduct tax at source on the total income so computed, at the rates in force;

(d) Issue the account holder a certificate of tax deducted at source;

(e) Include the deduction in the Bank's periodic TDS return filed with the Income-tax Department; and

(f)  Retain the declaration, together with supporting documentation, and produce the same to the Income-tax Department upon request.

 

Where all of the conditions are satisfied and bank completes its responsibility the customer need not file a regular Income Tax Return (ITR).

The declaration is required to be furnished afresh for every Tax Year in respect of which the exemption from filing an Income-tax Return is sought. Where there is any change in the account holder's income or particulars during the year, a revised declaration must be obtained; and where the account holder derives any income other than pension or interest during the year, the declaration must be withdrawn, following which the account holder is required to file a regular Income-tax Return.

5 Reversal of TDS of Customers submitting form 121 after application of TDS –

Please note that, TDS once deducted and paid cannot be reversed. However, we may allow reversal of TDS as a special case to those Customers who submit form 121 before the end of that particular month, provided the same has not been remitted to Govt. In such cases, following procedures should be followed by branches:

i.       Customer can submit form 121 till the end of the month concerned to avail benefit of reversal of TDS already deducted during the month. In such case, Branches should modify 121 Master to make TDS “No” with appropriate reason code (as mentioned in point no. 2) immediately after submission of form 121.

ii.     TDS will not be reversed for those customers who submit form 121 after the end of the month concerned. Please take note that once TDS is remitted to Government, it will not be reversed.

iii.    TDS will be reversed directly through Auto reversal programme (provided by I.T. Department) centrally for all Branches by Accounts Department. Branches are not required to reverse TDS manually in such cases. The reversal will be made on the immediate next working day of the following month.

iv.   Transfer of TDS reversed to Customer In case of MID/QID Deposit – TDS entries pertaining to MID/QID Deposits and reversed through auto reversal programme by Accounts Department as mentioned in point (iii) above will be transferred to INTPAY account. Branches are advised to credit the same to the Account of concerned Customer; or issue Pay Order/DD or effect transfer through NEFT/RTGS as the case may be.

v.     Reversal of TDS in case of Closed Deposits – If the Deposit from which the TDS is deducted is Closed or Auto Renewed & the Customers submit the form 121 towards the end of the month, then, in such case, TDS will be reversed by Auto Reversal Programme as stated above and the amount of TDS will be parked in separate General Ledger Head i.e. TDSREV. Branches are required to transfer such reversed TDS to the Account of the Customer, issue Pay Order or remit the TDS by NEFT as the case may be.

If the customer does not maintain an account with our bank, then the branch will be required to follow up with the customer and obtain required cash from the customer.

 

·        Then following entry will appear

TDS                      --- CR

 

e. For reversal of TDS deducted on FDR / RD

·        Under Transfer Entry enter Acct ID (e.g. RD/21942/21942) and select activity TDTDSCR (Tax Deducted Credit). Enter the TDS amount to be reversed on the said account.

In case of reversal of TDS in the month of March (for e.g. if customer has submitted Form No. 121 after deduction of TDS, excess deduction of TDS, etc), reversal should be completed before 31st of March itself. No entries for reversal of TDS will be passed in the next financial year.

8. Payment of TDS.

Ø      Due Dates for deposit of TDS in Government account:

a)   All sums deducted as TDS by branches shall be paid to the credit of the Government on or before 30th day of April where interest, commission, fee , rent etc. are credited to beneficiaries’ accounts or paid otherwise in the month of March: and

b) In any other case, within seven days from the end of the month in which the deduction is made, i.e. on or before 7th of next month.

 

Ø  Branches should originate the amount of TDS on Interest on Deposits, NRO Deposit, Rent, DDS Commission and Payment made, to Contractors to Accounts Department on or before 5th day of the month following concerning month. The amount originated should match with the corresponding GL balance of TDS of the respective branch. If the branch is unable to provide the TDS sheet within the prescribed date given by Accounts Dept, then branch in charge and the staff concerned will be held responsible for any interest on delayed payment of TDS for that month.

Ø  Accounts Department consolidates the amount of TDS received from Branches and makes payment of TDS on Interest on Deposits ( erstwhile Sec 194A), TDS on NRO Deposits (erstwhile sec 195), TDS on Rent (erstwhile sec 194I), TDS on Contractors (erstwhile sec 194 C) and TDS on Commission to DDS Agent (erstwhile sec 194H). Branches are not to send/fax challan for payment of TDS of branches.

Ø  Payment of TDS will be made under single TAN for all Branches by Accounts Department.

Ø  Please note than once TDS is deducted and paid to the Government, the same will not be reversed/refunded under any circumstances.

Ø  Premature closure of deposit:- If as a result of premature closure of term deposits, revised interest projected from all branches for balance deposits plus interest already provided (from all branches) does not exceed Rs. 50,000/- (or Rs.100,000/-in case of a senior citizen), then TDS already deducted and remitted to Govt. Account will not be reversed. Branches should direct the customer to claim refund of TDS in his Income Tax Return. If TDS is deducted but not yet remitted to Govt. Account, the same can be reversed by branches.

 

Ø  Interest levied for failure to Deduct Tax or failure to pay after deduction of Tax.

·     Interest levied for failure to Deduct Tax: Interest is payable @ 1% for every month or part thereof from the date on which tax was deductible to the date of deduction of such tax. E.g. if TDS on interest on deposits for the month of December is deducted on 15th Feb (due date for which is 31st Dec), then interest will be paid for 3 months i.e. from Dec to Feb since the due date of deduction falls in the month of December and date of actual deduction falls in the month of February.

·     Interest levied for failure to pay after deduction of tax: Interest is payable @ 1.5% for every month or part thereof for failure to pay or delay in the payment of the tax deducted at source. Interest is payable from the date of deduction of tax to the date of payment to the Government i.e. month in which TDS is deducted to the month in which TDS is paid.

The Staff-members /Officers concerned, who handle TDS work shall be held responsible and accountable for delay in deduction, deduction at wrong rate and or/payment of TDS liability to Government Account. The penalty imposed/ amount of interest levied shall be recovered from the staff/officer concerned, for delay in deduction / payment.

10. Checking of TDS deduction – Role of the Branches

i.       To check deduction of TDS (i.e. short/excess deduction of TDS) at their end before originating entry to Accounts Department. Any mistakes identified should be rectified immediately. The activity to be used by branches for deducting or reversing TDS is mentioned in point no. 6 above.

ii.     If error identified after entry is originated, intimate Accounts Department immediately to incorporate the correct data in TDS Return. Manager and staff concerned will be held responsible for late deduction of tax and interest on delayed payment of TDS.

iii.    TDS on Provision: If Branches / Departments have made any provision towards expenses liable to TDS such as rent, commission, professional fees or any other expenses, on account of quarterly/yearly closing, Branches / Departments shall deduct TDS on the same at the time of making provision itself and include the same in the TDS sheet provided to Accounts Dept.

In case of a customer with NO PAN or Inoperative PAN, TDS will be deducted @ 20% through system. If such customer immediately submits PAN after deduction of TDS but before remittance to Govt. A/c, branches can reverse half of the TDS amount (i.e. 10%) and pay the balance. But, if PAN is submitted after payment of TDS to Govt. A/c, no reversal will be made, but TDS will be deducted @10% from further interest payments.

Even though system will deduct TDS @ 20% in case of No PAN customer, branches should check deduction of TDS at their end. In case of Inoperative PAN branches shall check the Inoperative PAN list sent by the accounts department and deduct the tax at 20% on such inoperative PAN. Also, if customer links the PAN to Aadhar and informs the branch, branches should change TDS rate to 10% in the customer master.

 

iv.   Checking of TDS work: TDS workings should be checked by a responsible officer i.e. Officer, Asst Manager or Manager to ensure the correctness of the data, and to ensure compliance with TDS provisions mentioned in this circular. The soft copy of the working of TDS should have the name and contact number of the person preparing the same, checking the same, and also that of the branch in-charge.

 

11. Mistakes due to Wrong Data Entry in OMNI

Deduction of TDS is based on information entered in OMNI. Following Wrong Data Entry affects deduction of tax-

a.     Wrong PAN – TDS will get deducted at higher rate or TDS defaults will be generated or credit of TDS will go against a wrong PAN number.

b.     Wrong Date of Birth – In case of Senior Citizen, TDS will get deducted even if eligible for form 121 or interest limit of Rs.100,000/- is not breached.

c.     Wrong Customer Type – In case of non-individual customers, when customer type is selected as “individual” instead of “others/business”, then Form 121 is generated. As already mentioned previously, Form 121 can be given only by individuals (except proprietorship firm where, the proprietor submits the Form). Therefore, entering the customer type for non-individual customers as individual results in wrong application of interest limit for the purpose of deduction of TDS in case of Form 121.

d.     Wrong TDS Reason Code –

i.     Customer has submitted form 121 but appropriate reason code has not been entered by the Branch in the System,

ii.   Submission of form 121 wrongly noted in the Customer Master,

In above cases, TDS will be wrongly deducted. Hence, branches are advised to take precaution.

e.     Grouping of Customer Numbers – Sometimes there is wrong Grouping of Customers (e.g. grouping of Husband and Wife’s Customer Number) or grouping is not removed even if Minor becomes Major. Similar other cases are seen in respect of Wrongly Grouped Customers. In such cases, interest will get clubbed and TDS will get deducted under the name of Main Customer. Hence, there should be correct Grouping of Customer IDs, as also TDS Reason Code should be updated in Main Customer and not in Sub-Customer. It may be noted that if details are updated in Sub- Customer, the same will not get considered while application of TDS.

 

Example: A Customer No. 123 is grouped under Customer No. 345, then PAN and TDS Reason Code is to be updated to Customer No. 345 and not in 123.

f.      More than 1 Customer ID for the same customer – In such cases, there is a possibility of Short Deduction of TDS.

 

For any mistakes due to wrong data entry in OMNI, branches (i.e. the staff concerned and the branch-in-charge) will be held responsible and they will be liable to make good the losses if any.
