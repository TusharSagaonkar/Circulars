Circular OC No. 853 dated 11th May 2021

 

FRAUDS IN DEPOSIT ACCOUNTS AND TRANSACTIONS

CAUSED DUE TO DEVIATION FROM INSTRUCTIONS

 

I.  Recently, there were two frauds, one of which was perpetrated by a staff-member in a dormant account by organising impersonation of the depositor who later turned out to have been deceased. The other related to a merchant transaction rendered possible because the customer compromised the security details of his account/Debit Card. There has also been a spate of frauds through Mobile Banking perpetrated by fraudsters who induce gullible customers to click at fake social media pages promising some benefit thereby leading them to compromise security of account/Debit Card.

 

II.  Separate instructions have been issued through the IDC Ref. No. MD-920/1441/2020-21 dated 5th January, 2021 towards preventing frauds by delinquent elements among staff. However, it is a cause of acute anxiety to us that, in many cases, frauds are becoming possible due to flouting by the operating staff of instructions. Simple & basic rules, which have been laid down and reiterated several times, are given no importance at all.

 

III.    We summarise below the broad areas of the systems and procedures, with further elaboration wherever required, which if followed, would have averted the frauds. Some systems and procedures have been widened through additional rules with a view to strengthening them. Such rules are marked accordingly. They will be incorporated in the subject-wise manuals & other materials in due course.

 

1. Dormant accounts

i.         Dormant (and inoperative) accounts are easy targets of fraudsters because customers or their successors either do not have time or inclination to keep track of balances in their accounts, or may possibly have lost their track altogether. Therefore, all our efforts must be made to contact or trace the customers and/or their heirs so that the accounts are made active.

ii.       There is an elaborate system of imposing restrictions on dormant accounts so long as they are not made active by inviting KYC documents afresh. The system needs to be followed strictly.

iii.   Additional rule :- Whenever a request is received for activating a dormant or inoperative account, the customer must be visited at his place of residence by a staff-member not below the rank of officer. The staff-member shall, during such visit, carry with him copies of Proof of Identity (POI) and Proof of Address (POA) which are already on the

record of the branch, for cross-verification. A visit report certifying genuineness or otherwise of the request shall be submitted by the staff-member for enabling decision by an official in the branch in the rank of Manager and above (see sub-Para v. below)

iv.    Current system requires attendance by the customer at the branch along with latest KYC documents. Photocopies of the documents which are to be retained at the branch need to be verified from the original and a certificate to this effect must be recorded by the official concerned thereon. This system shall invariably be followed regardless of visit by a staff-member to the place of the customer as mentioned in sub-Para iii. above.

v.     Additional rule :- Decision to activate a dormant or an inoperative account shall not be taken by an official at branch below the rank of Manager.

vi.      Further, identity of the customer has to be established by comparing his facial appearance with the photograph on previous record as also in the POI document to be obtained at the time of reactivation of the account.

vii.    Additional rule:- The official concerned should certify on application for activation of Dormant/inoperative account by branding a rubber stamp and signing it with own name and staff no. as under.

       “In person verification of the customer carried out and he/she has signed in my presence”

-Signed-

Name:-----------------------

 

Staff No.:- ----------------

Date:---------------------- "

2.  Issue of cheque book

i.         Application for issue of cheque book (continuation, or because the previous one has been lost) should not be entertained in respect of a dormant account.

Additional rule :- Currently, OMNI system does not bar registration of such application in dormant accounts. IT department is hereby instructed to introduce such a bar.

ii.       Application for new cheque book must be made by using the requisition slip from the previous cheque book.

iii.     If an application for new cheque book is not made by using a requisition slip from the previous cheque book as above but by way of a letter, the reason for such an application should be ascertained and asked to be recorded therein.

iv.     Unused cheques from the previous cheque book should be asked to be surrendered. If they are stated to have been lost or destroyed, a statement to that effect should be obtained in the application. Such unused cheques, whether surrendered or otherwise, should be stopped for payment in OMNI system.

v.       Signature of the customer on the requisition slip or on the separate application, as the case may be, must be compared with that on record and the request for the cheque book should be registered only thereafter.

vi.     The work of delivering cheque books to customers must be entrusted only to staff-members in clerical cadre and above.

 

3.  Cheque books must be issued either to the customer himself or to his authorised representative. (It is reiterated that cheque books should not be issued on dormant accounts at all; thus, question of its delivery in such case to customer’s representative does not arise). Identification of the customer should be done by comparing his signature with the one on record and also that of the authorised representative (the latter from the signature attested by the customer, and also from the representative’s own POI such as PAN). A copy of POI should be obtained and the official concerned should verify the same from the original. A certificate towards having “Verified from the original” should be recorded thereon under signature, name and staff number.

 

4.  Payment of cheques

i.         Drawer’s signature must be verified by comparing it with the specimen signature on record.

ii.       Ceilings for cash payment under Any Branch Banking must be adhered to.

iii.     In the matter of payment of cheques of high value requiring authorisation by more than one official, all the officials must apply their mind independent of others and decide upon appropriateness of payment.

iv.   As a prudent banker, enquiries must be made as to the purpose of heavy payment in cash considering that there are several provisions in Income Tax Act meant to discourage high value cash transactions.

v.       The transaction, or the series of transactions of which the said transaction appears to be a part, must be consistent with the Bank’s knowledge of the customer, his business, and the history of transactions being conducted through the account.

vi.     Ongoing due diligence of existing clients should be carried out in order to ensure that their transactions are consistent with the bank’s knowledge of the client, his business and risk profile and, where necessary, the source of funds.

 

5.  Mobile banking facility

i.         Registration of mobile number or change in the registered mobile number should not be done so long as an account is dormant or inoperative.

Additional rule: - Currently, OMNI system does not bar such registration of mobile number in respect of dormant or inoperative accounts whether for the first time or subsequently by way of change. IT department is hereby instructed to introduce such a bar.

ii.     Additional rule: - The mobile number, whether being registered for the first time or under a change from the previous one, must be in the name of the customer himself/herself. A bill from the service provider in respect of such mobile number, not more than two months old, must accompany the application for mobile banking facility to establish the fact of the mobile number being in the name of the customer himself/herself.

iii.   Application for mobile banking facility should be accepted directly from the customer himself by insisting on his personal visit to the branch. On such occasion, the customer must be identified on the basis of his photo on record. KYC details should also be confirmed in person on the basis of proof submitted by the customer for activation of mobile banking. (In all the cases where POI is obtained, record of In Person Verification having been carried out under rubber stamp, signature, name and staff number as mentioned in Para III. 1. vii. must be created). In rare cases (such as those of old or infirm customers), an application may be received through an authorised representative. In both the cases, signature of the customer must be verified from the same on record. Additionally, in case of an application received through an authorised representative, identity of such representative must be verified from the signature attested by the customer on the application (or separately) as well as on the basis of the POI of such representative which must be demanded, verified photocopy thereof being kept on record.

 

6.  Issue of duplicate FDR

i.         Application for issue of a duplicate FDR must be submitted only by the depositor himself in person. In rare and justifiable circumstances (to be ascertained and recorded, such as the depositor being old or infirm), application received through an authorised representative of the depositor may be entertained.

ii.       Full details of the FDR, the circumstances in which it was lost/misplaced, and efforts made by the holder to trace the same, should be mentioned in the application.

iii.      Applicant’s signature must be compared with the one on record. His appearance should be compared with the photograph on record. In Person Verification by the official concerned should be certified as prescribed in Para III.1. vii.

iv.      If the application is received through an authorised representative, signature of the authorised representative should be compared with the one attested by the customer on the application, apart from comparing signature of the customer himself with that on record. Further, POI of the authorised representative should be asked to be produced with a photocopy thereof; identity should also be established on its basis and the photocopy with verification recorded to be retained at the branch with due certification as prescribed in Para III.1.vii.

v.       An enquiry must be made in the OMNI system to ascertain whether there is lien on the said FDR. The enquiry should be made against the name of the customer as also the FDR number. The possibility of the FDR being under lien must be eliminated.

vi.      POI & latest POA should be called for afresh, customer’s signature should be obtained on copies of KYC documents in the presence of staff with due certification thereof, and name and address should be compared with those on record as per the application obtained at the time of the original deposit.

vii.  Additional rule: - In case of each application for issue of a duplicate FDR, a call must be made by the staff-member concerned on the registered mobile number of the customer to seek confirmation of the application. This will be regardless of the amount. The fact of having made the call and having received a positive confirmation (or otherwise) of the application must be recorded on the application with name & signature of the staff-member concerned. In case the customer denies during the call having applied for the duplicate FDR, matter should be handled judging seriousness thereof (keeping in view the amount involved) and under guidance from the Regional Office/Zonal Office. In case of any ambiguity in respect of an FDR involving a face value of Rs. 1 Lakh and above, a visit by a staff-member to the place of the customer may be made & genuineness of the application should be confirmed & recorded through a report.

viii. When e-KYC resumes and becomes available at branches, and customer agrees in writing to get e-KYC (Biometric identification) done, the same can be done in branch (or at the customer’s place at the discretion of the Branch-(In-Charge).

ix.      The documents such as indemnity etc. which will be obtained before issue of the FDR must conform to the standard set.

x.       The duplicate FDR should be marked as such, i. e. “Duplicate”. Necessary noting should be made in the OMNI system.

 

7.  Encashment/premature payment of FDR/duplicate FDR

i.         The mere fact that a person holds an FDR (whether original or duplicate) does not mean that he owns the same or he is himself the customer named in the FDR. Identity of the customer who presents the FDR for payment must be established by comparing his signature with that on record apart from identification through comparison of appearance with the photograph on record.

ii.       It should be checked in the OMNI system that there is no lien against the number of the FDR and also the customer named therein. Both the checks should be carried out independently. The mere fact that the person demanding encashment holds the FDR should not lead to an automatic conclusion that there is no lien against the FDR.

iii.   Instances of a duplicate FDR being presented for premature payment should be subjected to thorough scrutiny. All such cases must be referred to the Branch-In-Charge for decision.

iv.      POI & latest POA should be called for afresh and name and address should be compared with those on record as per the application obtained at the time of the original deposit.

v.     Additional rule: - Instances of duplicate FDR being presented for premature payment in a short time after their issue should be viewed with healthy scepticism.

vi.    Additional rule: - The cases of duplicate FDRs being presented for payment, whether prematurely or upon maturity, must be dealt with by comparing the signatures and photographs as obtained at the time of placement of the original deposit and not only by comparing them with the ones obtained as part of the procedure for obtaining the duplicate FDR. This is to preclude the possibility that the documents submitted at the time of obtaining the duplicate FDR were forged.

vii.  Additional rule: - In all the cases of premature payment of a duplicate FDR of face value above Rs. 5 Lakh, a visit to the place of residence of the customer and his interview thereat must be organised to ascertain genuineness of the request. A report of the visit & interview must be prepared, the report being kept on record. Further, in all such cases

with face value above Rs. 25 Lakh, possibility of e-KYC of the applicant-customer must be explored.

 

8.  Frauds in Mobile Banking, use of debit card in POS/merchant transactions, Payment through ATMs etc.

i.         Acknowledgement of complaint lodged by customer, and noting date, time and name of receiving staff member under signature on the received complaint : - Complaints tendered by customer (in respect of all the matters, but particularly in respect of mobile banking, ATM related or merchant transactions, against the backdrop of a recent incident) tendered at the branch must be acknowledged by the staff-member concerned with recording of date as well as time of receipt thereof under full signature with his/her name and staff number. The particulars (of date and time, receiver’s name and staff number) should also be recorded under signature on the original complaint lodged with Bank so that further preventive action or slackness in respect thereof can be determined, followed up and tracked. This is very important for purposes of investigation and accountability, whether to be determined internally or externally.

ii.        Ascertaining from the complainant details of the incident of fraud and circumstances and situation around the same- Need to be circumspect and discreet.

a.  When a complaint of fraud in respect of ATM transaction, POS transaction or a Mobile Banking transaction is received, branches are under instructions, vide Circular OC no. 799 dated 14th August, 2018

and particularly its Revised Annexure-V, to seek details of the circumstances of the incident, actions of the complainant at or around the material time, and compromises if any with the security of the account or the Debit card which the customer may have made. The said Revised Annexure V is confidential and provides guidance on the types of questions which are not to be uniformly asked to the customers in a routine manner.

b.  The sample questions indicated therein do not cover instances of mobile banking frauds; however, they can be thought through and asked. Likely questions which may be put to the customer lodging a complaint on Mobile Banking Fraud may be as per following samples.

·        Whether you received any telephone call in which you were offered some prize or gift or facility of credit, withdrawal limit, additional limit etc.?

·        If yes, whether you responded to the said call?

·        Were you asked any information in the said call?

·        Did you give any information to the caller during the said call? What was it?

·        Did you receive any e-mail or visit a social media post or respond to any message which offered you some prize or gift of facility of loan/credit, withdrawal limit, additional limit etc.?

·        Did you click at any attachment to or link received in any e-mail, any social media post or a social media page which called for any information?

·        If yes, did you provide any information? What was it?

(The sample questions as above have now been added to those in the form, which stood until now, of the “Revised Annexure-V of the Circular OC no. 799 dated 14th August, 2018”. The amended text of the “Revised Annexure-V of the Circular OC no. 799 dated 14th August, 2018”, after inclusion of the above sample questions, is attached hereto. Henceforth, while referring to and acting in accordance with the Circular OC no. 799 dated 14th August, 2018, the instant amended annexure alone shall be considered and the one previously attached to the said circular should be ignored).

c.  It is observed that branches take a printout of the said annexure and seek responses to the printed questions. This defeats the purpose of our probing.

d.  While recording the responses to questions under the signature of the complainant is necessary, the questions should be carefully thought through by the official concerned and framed as suited to the circumstances.

e.  The exercise of seeking responses must be skilful. It should not put the complainant on the defensive. It should be remembered that at the time of the initial complaint, the customer in anxious to get

back the money and is likely to be most truthful. Comforting and assuring behaviour of the official concerned will enable the customer to remember all the facts and will help us elicit useful responses which will be mutually useful.

 

iii.   Debit freeze/hold on balances not to be reversed without due analysis: -When a customer reports an incident of loss of debit card, compromise of security details or some unusual happening which may have caused or likely to cause a loss, there is a possibility of fraud, either a further one or a new fraud altogether. In such cases, officials in branch take preventive measures such as debit freeze, putting a hold on balance etc. Such a freeze, hold or another similar preventive action should not be reversed without adequate analysis. Further, it should not generally be done without reference to or permission from the official who had taken the preventive action.

 

IV.  It should be noted that the instructions as above are not in substitution of the current ones on the relative subjects but in further elaboration and, as indicated at appropriate places, in addition to or as extensions of them. Considering the grave threat to which Bank has been exposed due to non-adherence of the systems and procedures, branches are being instructed to follow them strictly in future failing which stern administrative action shall be taken against the erring staff-members.

 

V.    Please bring the contents of the instant circular to the notice of all the staff-members and ensure strict compliance.

 

 

P. S. Salian

Managing Director
