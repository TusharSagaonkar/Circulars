Circular OC No. 758 dated 4th January, 2017

Master Direction by RBI on Know Your Customer as Updated upto 8th December, 2016

 

Strict Adherence to Instructions Contained in Sections 8(d), 8(e), 23 & Section 67 thereof

 

 

        I. It has been brought to the notice of RBI that compliance with provisions of Sections 8(d), 8(e), 23 and 67 of Master Direction on Know Your Customer (KYC) are not being strictly adhered to. In this regard RBI has issued the Circular No. RBI/2016-17/183 DBR.AML.BC.48/14.01.01/2016-17 dated December 15, 2016. Operative portions of the RBI circular, with appropriate paraphrasing for applicability to branches, are as under:

 

1. Section 23 of the Master Direction provides instructions for opening & operations of small accounts

 &

2. Section 67 thereof quotes provisions of Income Tax (I. T.)Rule 114B/1(as applicable to banks) that Permanent Account Number (PAN) of customers needs to be obtained and verified while undertaking transactions mentioned therein. The circular also clarifies that opening of an account with a Bank itself is a transaction requiring compliance with the I. T. Rule 114B.

 

(i) RBI’s Circular under reference further dwells on the subject as under:

In respect of small accounts (i.e. (a) PMJDY-BSB and (b) PMJDY-SSB in our Bank), the prescribed limits/conditions shall not be breached and compliance thereof shall be strictly monitored. If any customer desires to have operations beyond the stipulated limits, the same shall be allowed only after complying with requirement for opening a normal account including completion of Customer Due Diligence (CDD)/Know Your Customer (KYC) procedures detailed in section 16/17 & provisions of section 67 of the Master Direction which include quoting of PAN/obtaining of Form 60 while opening an account with a Bank (among other entities). If any account is rendered ineligible for being classified as a small account due to credits/balance in the account exceeding the permissible limits, withdrawals may be allowed within the limit prescribed for small accounts where the limits thereof have not been breached./1

 

/1(ii) Basic Savings Bank Deposit(BSBD) accounts (PMJDY-BSB & PMJDY-SSB in our Bank) which are not KYC compliant accounts are to be treated as “Small Accounts” and are subjected to the limitations/1applicable to such accounts.

Hence, for allowing normal operations in such accounts, the procedures explained at (i) above are to be complied with. If any account is rendered ineligible for being classified as a small account due to credits/balance in the account exceeding the permissible limits, withdrawals may be allowed within the limit prescribed for small accounts where the limits thereof have not been breached.

 

(iii) In respect of KYC compliant accounts where the required CDD procedure has been complied with, Banks (including our bank as a Regulated Entity) shall ensure compliance regarding quoting of PAN/obtaining of Form 60 for all transactions in terms of I.T. Rule 114B which includes opening of accounts with banks (including our bank as a Regulated Entity). No debit transaction, transfer or otherwise shall be allowed in accounts which do not comply with the above mentioned requirements to begin with. This rule shall be strictly applied in accounts where both the thresholds listed below are reached:

a.  Balance of Rupees 5 lakh or more

and

b. The total deposits (including credits by electronic or other means) made after November 9, 2016, exceed Rupees two lakh.

        II. In this connection, we reiterate extant instructions in simplified manner with suitable examples, as under :

i] Our Bank has following four (4) types of accounts belonging to the category of “Small Accounts” as defined in Section 23 of Master Direction.

·      Small Savings Bank Account (SSB A/c)

·      Basic Savings Bank Account (BSB A/c)

·      Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana –BSB A/c (PMJDYBSB A/c)

·      Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana –SSB A/c (PMJDYSSB A/c)

 

i-A] There are following four basic restrictions, apart from a few others, with regard to operations in SSB A/c and PMJDYSSB A/c which are to be strictly adhered to by the branches.

 

a) The aggregate of all credits in a financial year does not exceed Rs. 1.00 lakh.

b) The aggregate of all withdrawals & transfers in a calendar month does not exceed Rs. 10,000/-.

c)  The balance in the account at any point of time does not exceed Rs. 50,000/-.

d) Only local clearing cheques not exceeding Rs. 5000/- drawn in favour of Account Holders will be accepted for credit of these accounts./1

 

i-B] Further, the four basic restrictions, apart from a few others, with regard to operations in BSB A/c and PMJDYBSB A/c which are to be strictly adhered to by the branches, are as under.

 

a)  Only up to 4 (four) withdrawals including ATM withdrawals shall be allowed in a month.

b) No cheque book will be issued to the account holder and withdrawals will be allowed using withdrawal slips only.

c)  No foreign remittances in any form will be accepted for credit to this account.

d) Local clearing cheques exceeding Rs. 5,000/- in favour of account holder drawn by other than Central/State Government Agencies and Departments, will not be accepted for credit to Account.

 

ii-A] If any customer desires to have operations beyond the stipulated limits in Small/Basic Saving Bank accounts of the above mentioned types, such account holder should be persuaded & given to understand that operations beyond stipulated limits are not possible in SSB accounts and could be possible only in Normal Accounts. Such customer should further be requested to open a normal account in lieu of SSB/BSB A/c in compliance with requirements for completion of CDD/KYC procedures detailed in Sections 16/17 and after obtaining PAN/declaration in Form 60 while opening such an account under provisions of Section 67 of the above referred Master Direction.

 

ii-B] Further, if any customer’s account is rendered ineligible for being classified as a small account (SSB A/c) due to credits/balance in the said account exceeding the permissible limits and further if the said A/c has not been replaced by a Normal A/c, the withdrawals in the said SSB A/c may be allowed within the limit prescribed for small accounts (referred to above in point no. (i-A] b)) in case of SSB type of accounts and referred to above in point no. (i-B] a)) in case of BSB type of accounts) so that the limits therefor are not breached.

iii]   RBI has, now, directed us that we must comply regarding quoting of PAN/obtaining declaration in Form 60 for opening of accounts with us/1(i.e. by implication, replacement of those accounts which have been rendered ineligible for being classified as a small account due/1to credits/balance in them exceeding the permissible limits, into a/1regular account)./1

      RBI has strictly mandated that no debit transaction, transfer or otherwise, shall be allowed in accounts “without obtaining/1PAN/Form 60” in such cases where both the thresholds limits listed below have been reached:

 

a) Balance in the Account of Rupees Five Lakh (Rs. 5.00 Lakh) or more

               and

b)The total deposits (including credits by electronic or other means) made after November 9, 2016, have exceeded Rupees Two Lakh/1(Rs. 2.00 Lakh).

 

        III. Instructions for Inspection Department: -

In terms of Section 8(d) and (e) of the Master Direction, it is necessary that Bank’s concurrent/internal audit systems verify compliance with KYC/AML policies and procedures and quarterly audit notes and compliance reports are submitted to Audit Committee of the board. Therefore, the department shall ensure strict compliance of Section 8(d) & (e) as mentioned herein.

 

IV. Please take suitable note of these instructions and bring the contents of this circular to the notice of all staff/officials concerned at your Branch/Department and ensure strict compliance.
