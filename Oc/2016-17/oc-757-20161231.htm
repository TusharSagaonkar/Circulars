Circular OC No. 757 dated  31st December, 2016

 

Central KYC Registry [CKYCR] (for Individuals)

- Operating Guidelines

·     On-Boarding to Central KYC Registry (for Individuals)

·     Revised Customer Information Form (CIF-I)

·     Discontinuation of obtaining the existing Addendum-I form from individuals for FATCA & CRS compliance

___________________________________________________________________

 

 

I. 1. The Government of India vide their Notification dated November 26, 2015 authorized the Central Registry of Securitization Asset Reconstruction and Security Interest of India (CERSAI), set up under sub-section (1) of Section 20 of the Securitization and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act, 2002 (54 of 2002), to act as and to perform the functions of the Central KYC Records Registry under the said rules, including receiving, storing, safeguarding and retrieving the KYC records in digital form of a “client” of a Bank, as defined in clause (ha) of sub-section (1) of Section 2 of the Prevention of Money-Laundering Act, 2002. The Central Government has also amended the Prevention of Money-laundering (Maintenance of Records) Rules, 2005 vide Notification dated 7th July, 2015 for the purpose of establishment of Central KYC Registry. As per Prevention of Money-laundering (Maintenance of Records) Amendment Rules, 2015, Rule 9 (1A), every reporting entity shall, within three days after the commencement of an account-based relationship with a client, file the electronic copy of the client’s KYC records with the Central KYC Registry. Our Bank falls in the category of Reporting Entities.

 

2. RBI vide their Circular Ref. No. RBI/2016-17/177 DBR.AML.BC.47/14.01.01/ 2016-17 dated December 8, 2016 has advised that all Scheduled Commercial Banks (SCBs) are required to invariably upload the KYC data pertaining to all new individual accounts opened on or after January 1, 2017, with Central KYC Records Registry. SCBs are, however, allowed time up to February 1, 2017 for uploading data in respect of accounts opened during January 2017. Every Co-operative Bank including our Bank is required to upload the KYC data pertaining to all new individual accounts which are opened on or after April 1, 2017 to Central KYC Registry [CKYCR].

 

1. II. In the Board Meeting held on 29/7/2016, it was decided to implement CKYCR in our Bank. It was also decided that the setup for CKYCR would be done at the Centralized Data Entry Cell (CDEC) at Vashi as : 

2.the centralized account opening of all deposit accounts of the Bank is already being  done by the said department,

3.the said department follows electronic Document Management System (DMS),

4.DMS provides a facility to store scanned copies of Account Opening Forms (AOFs), Customer Information Forms (CIFs) & KYC documents provided by customers while opening accounts, and

5.uploading some of these scanned images is an essential part of overall uploading of customer information to CERSAI for CKYCR registration.

6.A brief on “Central KYC Registry (CKYCR)”

7.“Central KYC Registry (CKYCR) is a centralized repository of KYC records of customers in the financial sector with uniform KYC norms and inter-usability of the KYC records across the sector with an objective of reducing the burden of producing KYC documents and getting those verified every time when the customer creates a new relationship with a financial entity.”

2. The timeline for the KYC record to be uploaded onto the Central KYC Registry

As per Prevention of Money-laundering (Maintenance of Records) Amendment Rules, 2015, Rule 9 (I) (1A), every reporting entity shall within three days after the commencement of an account-based relationship with a client, file the electronic copy of the client’s KYC records with the Central KYC Registry. ‘Client” will mean “Customer” for the Bank.

 

3. The documents which are required to be uploaded on to the Central KYC  Registry

(i) The Central KYC Registry requires data as per the common KYC template to be captured along with the scanned copies of the certified supporting documents (PoI-Proof of Identity/PoA-Proof of Address), cropped signature and photograph.

(ii) While the data required as per the common template needs to be captured; the same can be captured on the common template, or the institution’s account opening forms can be modified to capture the required information.

 

4.  Salient features of Central KYC Registry are as under:

·     User friendly web portal

·      Unique KYC identifier linked with independent ID proofs

·     KYC data and documents stored in a digitally secure electronic format

·     Secure and advanced user authentication mechanisms for system access

·     Data de-duplication to ensure single KYC identifier per applicant

·     ID authentication with issuing authorities like Aadhaar/PAN etc.

·      Substantial cost reduction by avoiding multiplicity of registration and data upkeep

·     Real time notification to institutions on updating of KYC details

·     Regulatory reports to monitor compliance 

5.  The various account types for individuals in the Central KYC Registry

There are three account types in the Central KYC form – Normal, Simplified and Small. The account type can be gauged from the nomenclature of CKYC identifier issued to the customer.

(i)    For Normal Account, any of six officially valid documents (PAN, AADHAAR, Voter ID, Passport, Driving licence, NREGA Job Card) can be submitted for the ID of the customer.

 

(ii)  For Simplified Measures Account, there are additional OVDs that are allowed as per  RBI circular RBI/2015-16/42 dated July 1, 2015 - Point no. 2.3(i) & (ii) and point 3.2.2 I.A (iv) & (v). The KYC identifier for Simplified Measures Account will have a prefix “L”.

 

(iii)  For Small Account types, only personal details and photograph duly certified by the customer are required to be submitted. The KYC identifier for Small Account will have a prefix “S”.

               

6.   Bank’s Role in capturing data relevant for CKYCR, data uploading and further processing

(i) Common Templates to capture data

(a)         To begin with, CERSAI has given common templates only for “Individual Customers” for capturing relevant data of customer. The KYC data captured by the template also fulfills the reporting requirement under Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act (FATCA) and Common Reporting Standards (CRS)                i.e. FATCA declaration to be given by customer. Hence our Bank has modified and revised our Customer Information Form (CIF-I) based on CERSAI template. Copy of the  modified CIF-I form which includes the required templates provided by CERSAI and also takes care of FATCA and CRS compliance is enclosed herewith.

(b)        The revised modified CIF-I form is to be used from 10th January 2017 onwards from all new/prospective customers and existing customers approaching for KYC updating.  Development department has been advised to provide the revised modified CIF-I form to all the branches on an urgent basis. Since revised CIF-I form takes care of FATCA and CRS compliance, it has also been decided to discontinue obtaining of existing form of Addendum-I for FATCA compliance from an individual while opening an Account. However, obtaining Addendum-II along with entity (non-individual) account opening form will continue.

7. Upload of KYC Record

The data captured as per the common KYC template will be uploaded on the Central KYC portal along with the scanned copies of the supporting documents (PoI-Proof of Identity/PoA-Proof of Address). For an individual record, the signature and photograph is to be cropped separately and uploaded as per detailed specifications mentioned in operational guidelines, by the CDEC department. The IT department has been suitably advised to provide interface for uploading the record to CERSAI in desired format with the help of software vendor.

 

8.  Processing of Records at Central KYC Registry

The KYC data uploaded on the Central KYC Registry will go through de-duplication process on the basis of the demographics (i.e. customer name, maiden name, gender, date of birth, mother’s name, father/spouse name, addresses, mobile number, email id etc.) and identity details submitted by the customer to the Bank.

 

9. KYC Identifier - A unique CKYCR Id Number :

       i. A 14 digit unique KYC identifier will be generated for new customer record if not registered by any other linked entity and will be notified to the reporting entity  (a no-match found message).

     ii. For “Small Accounts”, the KYC identifier will additionally have a prefix “S”.

    iii. For “Simplified Measures Accounts”, the KYC identifier will additionally have a prefix “L”.

 

a.  Download of CKYCR Record

In cases where the uploaded details match with some details already existent in CKYCR registry, then in such cases our Bank (as the reporting entity) will be suitably notified about the match (a partial or complete match message). In such cases, our Bank will need to download information details provided by CKYCR registry for re-confirmation and if satisfied with the match details, allocate the relative CKYCR registry Id Number to the customer for whom request has been sent. 

 

b.  Charges for Upload / Download of CKYCR Record

The services of the Central KYC Registry will be available on payment of prescribed fee, in advance for data upload @ fee of Rs.0.80 (80 paisa) and download @ fee of Rs.1.10 approximately per record, which will be borne by the Bank.

 

c.  Update of KYC record

In case of change of existing information of a customer (including minor turning major) in the records of Central KYC Registry, Bank (as the reporting entity) will initiate an update request.

(i)  The updated data along with the scanned copy of the supporting document, where required, will be uploaded on the Central KYC Registry portal.

 

(ii) In order to initiate a request for updating, Bank (as the reporting entity) will need to have the latest KYC record of the customer.

 

(iii)     On updating of a KYC record at the Central KYC Registry, all linked entities (other Financial Institutions or agencies linked to CKYCR) that have either uploaded or downloaded the KYC record for that particular customer will receive an electronic update notification of KYC record. The entities can download the last updated record of the customer in all such cases.

10.     The reporting entity which performed the last KYC verification or sent updated information in respect of a client shall be responsible for verifying the authenticity of the identity or address of the client.

It is being reiterated that branches should use only the revised CIF-I form from 10th January, 2017 onwards and discontinue using Addendum-1 for FATCA / CRS declaration.

 

IV. Please bring the contents of this Circular to the notice of all staff members/officials working in your branch, for achieving the objectives of successful On-Boarding to Central KYC Registry [CKYCR].
