Circular CR No. 336 dated 30th September 2024

Retail and MSME sectors Providing information regarding pricing of term loans in a transparent manner Mandatory “Key Facts Statement (KFS)” to be given to applicants for loan with in 3 days’ validity period w.e.f. 1st October 2024

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

I.       Introduction

1.  It has been the concern of the Reserve Bank of India that different Regulated Entities (REs) in the financial sector (including banks) follow different methods of quoting as well as calculating interest rates and charges on loans and advances. This makes it difficult for the applicants of loans and advances to decide among the REs as to which one offers them the most competitive terms. In the past, there were three communications on the subject which, however, were not directly applicable to the UCBs (unless they were engaged in certain credit products which are currently not part of our portfolio).

2.  Recently, through its notification numbered RBI/2024-25/18 DOR.STR.REC.13/13.03.00/2024-25 dated the 15th April 2024, the RBI has issued guidelines to all the REs (including UCBs such as ours) to provide a Key Facts Statement (KFS) to each borrower in the retail and MSME segments to whom a term loan is sanctioned, and disclose to him/her/them the Annual Percentage Rate (APR) worked out through a defined methodology. Upon sanction, a few days’ time is to be given to the borrowers to decide whether to take the loan or not. (Some situations considered therein regarding digital lending etc. do not apply to us as of now).

3.  The guidelines given by the RBI and our internal operational instructions are detailed in the following paragraphs. The reference to an “RE” made by the RBI is taken as applicable to the “Bank” (ours) for the purpose of this circular.

II.     The terms used in the RBI’s circular (and adopted by us)

1.  Key Facts of a loan agreement between the Bank and a borrower are legally significant and deterministic facts that satisfy basic information required to assist the borrower in taking an informed financial decision.

2.  Key Facts Statement (KFS) is a statement of key facts of a loan agreement, in simple and easier to understand language, provided to the borrower in a standardised format.

3.  Annual Percentage Rate (APR) is the annual cost of credit to the borrower which includes interest rate and all other charges associated with the credit facility.

4.  Equated Periodic Instalment (EPI) is an equated or fixed amount of repayments, consisting of both the principal and interest components, to be paid by a borrower towards repayment of a loan at periodic intervals for a fixed number of such intervals; which result in complete amortisation of the loan. EPI at monthly intervals is called Equated Monthly Instalment (EMI).

(NOTE-1: - An EPI in our Bank is EMI because we do not stipulate instalments at bi-monthly, quarterly or yearly intervals etc. but only at monthly intervals).

III.    Specific instructions of RBI

1.  The Bank shall provide a KFS to all prospective borrowers to help them take an informed view before executing the loan contract. The RBI has provided a standardised format given in Annex - I.

2.  The RBI requires the KFS to be written in a language understood by borrowers.

3.  The contents of the KFS shall be explained to the borrower(s) and an acknowledgement shall be obtained from him/her/them that its contents have been understood.

4.  Each KFS shall be given a Unique Proposal Number and shall have a validity period of at least three working days for loans having tenor of seven days or more, and a validity period of one working day for loans having tenor of less than seven days.

(NOTE-2: - Validity period refers to the period available to the borrower, after being provided the KFS by the Bank, to agree to the terms of the loan. The Bank shall be bound by the terms of the loan indicated in the KFS, if agreed to by the borrower during the validity period. In our Bank, all the loans are of tenor more than seven days. It has been decided that the validity period for each KFS shall be uniformly 3 days).

5.  The KFS shall also include a computation sheet of Annual Percentage Rate (APR), and the amortisation schedule of the loan over the loan tenor. APR will include all the charges which are applied by the Bank. Illustrative examples of calculation of APR and disclosure of repayment schedule for a hypothetical loan are given in Annex. II & III respectively. Charges recovered from the borrowers by the Bank on behalf of third-party service providers on actual basis (such as insurance charges, legal charges etc.) shall also form part of the APR and shall also be disclosed separately. In all cases where the Bank is involved in recovering such charges, the receipts and related documents shall be provided to the borrower for each payment, within a reasonable time.

6.  Any fees, charges, etc. which are not mentioned in the KFS cannot be charged by the Bank to the borrower at any stage during the term of the loan, without explicit consent of the borrower.

7.  The KFS shall also be included in a summary box to be exhibited as part of the loan agreement.

8.  All new retail and MSME term loans including fresh loans to existing customers must be compliant of the guidelines as above in letter and spirit without any exception w.e.f. 1st October 2024.

 

IV.    Additional instructions for dealing with applications for Term Loans in Retail and MSME sector in light of the RBI’s circular under reference

1.  While handling the initial inquiry for a loan, the need for the loan shall be correctly ascertained through interaction with the applicants/inquirers and appropriate scheme in the Bank’s portfolio shall be suggested to him/her/them.

2.  If the applicant(s) find the suggested scheme suiting their need, approximate quantum of the loan to be applied for may be determined through discussions and the amount of processing charges (which the approximate size of the proposed loan shall attract) should be informed to him/her/them.

3.  After receiving an In-Principle-Clearance for the proposal including that for the draft KFS, its final printout will be taken, given the running Unique Proposal Number, and such KFS shall be issued to the applicant(s) against his/her/their acknowledgement. Essential contents of the KFS are briefly described in Para V below.

4.  The validity of the KFS is three days. If an applicant/applicants convey to the branch that he/she/they are agreeable to avail the loan at the interest rate, charges, moratorium (if any), amortisation schedule, EMI and the resultant API, the rest of the process of scrutiny (including sending the photocopies of the title deeds to the Bank’s empanelled advocate for confirming that they convey a clear, absolute and marketable title) and sanction should be initiated.

5.  If the applicant(s) convey(s) that he/she/they is/are agreeable to the KFS after three days, the Bank may or may not entertain the proposal. If the bank decides to entertain the proposal after the three days’ validity period has elapsed, the terms in the KFS may be reviewed and modified by the Bank due to changes if any in interest rate, charges, risk factors, exposure norms, etc. and a new KFS issued which shall carry its own validity period of three days.

6.  Application received for a Term Loan in Retail and MSME sectors shall be entered in register to be maintained in each branch bearing name “Applications received for Term Loans in Retail and MSME sectors”. The specimen folio of the register is given in Annexure IV hereto.

 

V.   Essential contents of KFS

1.   The KFS should convey to the applicant(s) in clear and transparent manner the terms and conditions of the loan to be sanctioned.

2.   It should particularly contain the effective APR in which various costs (over and above the quoted rate of interest) are built with their time-value. This is done by calculating an Internal Rate of Return of instalments and charges estimated for repayment over the entire amortisation schedule.

3.   The charges which are considered for calculation of APR shall include the Bank’s own charges as also those which shall be recovered by the Bank for other service providers [or paid directly by the borrower(s) to the outside service provider(s) at the behest of the Bank, being a requirement of the loan].

4.   Some charges leviable on account of third-party services (such as insurance premium) may be variable. Therefore, while the APR to be advised to the borrower through the KFS should include the charge of the said third-party obtaining at the time of sanction, a footnote should be given in the sanction letter as also at the bottom of the KFS sheet that it is subject to changes made from time to time by the third party concerned.

5.   The charges shall include those for loan processing, title search and certification, stamp duty, insurance, etc. The timing of their payment should be suitably projected to enable calculation of their time-value.

6.   The Bank shall not levy any charge which has not been shown in the KFS and accounted for in calculation of the Annual Percentage Rate.

7.   Additional charges which may be contingently recovered for breaches of credit discipline do not form part of the KFS for obvious reasons. A footnote to this effect shall be incorporated in the sanction letter as also the KFS.

VI.     Important points to be noted by the staff processing the loan proposal

1.   The loan quantum, the moratorium, the amortisation schedule and the rate of interest must be correctly worked out on the basis of information provided by the applicant(s) and discussions with them.

2.   All the charges which are recoverable from/payable by the prospective borrowers and the months of the amortisation schedule in which they become recoverable by the Bank/payable by the prospective borrowers must be anticipated as accurately as possible, indicated in the KFS separately as such, and also accounted for in calculation of the APR.

3.   The responsibility of calculation of the APR, accurate preparation of the KFS and seeking approval for it from the sanctioning authority, and issuing the same (the approved KFS) to the applicants under one’s own signature against acknowledgement shall be with the official who internally recommends the proposal to the authority vested with the powers for sanction.

 

VII.    Final sanction

1.   After the applicant(s) grant(s) consent to the KFS within three days’ validity period for proceeding with the loan availment, the photocopies of the title deeds shall be sent by the official to the Bank’s empanelled advocate processing the loan proposal for verification of mortgageability. If an acceptable report is received, the appraisal and scrutiny notes shall be put up to the authority for obtaining final sanction.

2.   Thereafter, the sanction letter which should again include the KFS in a summary box, should be prepared and issued against acknowledgement.

3.   The sanction letter shall also be accompanied by a statement of full amortisation schedule with EMIs broken up into Payment of Principal Component and Payment of Interest Component on reducing balance basis. This should be followed in the normal course with the signing of agreements, execution of mortgage (and other documentation if any), and disbursement.

VIII.   Mandatory nature on instructions

The instructions by the RBI regarding the KFS in respect of new or renewed loans in the retail and MSME sectors are mandatory. Therefore, the staff in branches, Zonal Offices and the Credit department of Head Office dealing with such loans much adhere to the instructions meticulously.
