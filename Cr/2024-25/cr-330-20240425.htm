Circular CR No.330 dated 25th April 2024

 

I.     Updating/rectifying of information by Credit Information Companies (CICs) and Credit Institutions (CIs)

II.   Compensation to customers for delays in updating or rectification and apportionment of the amount of compensation among CICs and CIs

III. Communications with customers regarding (1) access to their credit history by inquirers (2) irregularities affecting credit history and(3) payment of compensation

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 

I.   Background

 

1.    The Credit Information Companies (CICs) are regulated by the Reserve Bank of India under the Credit Information Companies (Regulation) Act, 2005 (CICRA, 2005). The major function of the CICs is to collect, process and collate information on trade, credit and financial standing of the borrowers of Credit Institutions [CIs, which include banks,NBFCs or institutions in the organised sector dispensing credit which are their (CICs’) members], and to provide credit information to their specified users or the specified users of any other credit information company or to any other credit information company being its member, and to make available credit score to its specified users or specified users of any other credit information company or to other credit information companies being its members, and to undertake research projects.

2.    Each CI is required to be a member of all four CIC’s and the information on borrowers is required to be shared by the former to the latter. There is a standard mechanism for seeking the consent of borrowers for such sharing of information. The CICs have facilitated the efficient distribution of credit by making available reliable databases to the user institutions.

3.    The extant Credit Policy of our Bank requires that branches obtain Credit Information Reports from Trans Union Credit Information Bureau of India Ltd. (CIBIL), Experian, or other Credit Information Agencies on all existing and prospective borrowers and their sureties, whether individual or corporate, and their partners, directors, trustees, guarantors and sister/associate concerns. Branches are also required to check www.cibil.com for the list of defaulters whenever new and takeover credit proposals are received and considered.

 

 

 

 

II. Existing and prospective borrowers constitute a major group of beneficiaries and recognised as “Customers” by RBI

 

 

 

1.    Existing and prospective borrowers constitute a major group of beneficiaries of the institutional credit information mechanism because their creditworthiness is established independently based on objective data accessible to member CIs and CICs.

They are recognised by the RBI as “Customers” of the Credit Information Mechanism.

2.    It came to the notice of the RBI that the customers are seriously disadvantaged due to sharing by CIAs of erroneous or obsolete data about their credit history. The CIs may also be contributing to such sharing of erroneous or obsolete data. The representations made and updation advised to, and correction sought from the CIs or CICs or both, by the customers (existing and prospective borrowers) in respect of erroneous or old/obsolete data are not attended to timely or at all.

 

III.  Introduction of a mechanism by the RBI for compensation to customers for

delayed updation/rectification of credit information by the CIs and CICs

1.    To redress the grievances of the existing and prospective borrowers, the RBI, in terms of its circular No. RBI/2023-24/72 DoR.FIN.REC.48/20.16.003/2023-24 dated the 26th October 2023, has put in place a compensation framework for delayed updation/rectification of credit information and directed the CIs and CICs to implement the same. There is a timeline of 30 days for updation/rectification. Delayed updation/rectification invites compensation at Rs. 100/- per day which will be shared by the erring CIs/CICs. There are detailed instructions in the said RBI circular on this subject

2.    Further, for the existing and prospective borrowers to receive information on inquiries about or access to their credit history maintained by the CICs, RBI has, through its circular no. RBI/2023-24/73 DoR.FIN.REC.49/20.16.003/ 2023-24 dated the 26th October 2023 issued instructions to alert them by text messages and emails.

3.    The compensation framework and the instructions for communicating messages to the existing and prospective borrowers have been put in place by the RBI in exercise of the powers conferred by sub-section (1) of section 11 of the CICRA, 2005.

4.    The instructions contained in the RBI’s two circulars are to come into effect six months after the date of the RBI circulars. The instant circular is issued consequent upon the RBI’s said two circulars. Thus, the instructions herein come into effect from the 26th April 2024.

5.    The details of the mechanism of alerts to the existing and prospective borrowers, the framework of the compensation for delays in updation/rectification and other operational guidelines in the two matters are given in the following paragraphs.

IV.     Framework for compensation to customers for delayed updation/ rectification of credit information

1.  The existing and prospective borrowers can complain about erroneous, incorrect or obsolete data captured and posted, supplied or reported by the CICs or the CIs.

2.  A complainant shall be entitled to a compensation of ₹100/- per calendar day in case his/her complaint is not resolved within thirty (30) calendar days from the date of the initial filing of the complaint by the complainant with a CI/ CIC.

3.  Explanations concerning Para 2. above: -

                     i.    A complainant may request a CIC or CI to update the credit information by making an appropriate correction, addition or otherwise, and on such request, the CI or CIC shall take steps to update the credit information within thirty (30) days after being requested to do so.

                    ii.      The CI shall forward the corrected particulars of the credit information to the CIC or complainant within twenty-one (21) days from the date when the CI was informed of the inaccuracy in the credit information.

                  iii.      A combined reading of the above two sub-paras (i. and ii.) implies that, collectively, there is an overall limit of thirty (30) days to resolve/ dispose of a complaint. In effect, this means that a CI will get twenty-one (21) days and CICs will get the remainder of nine (9) days for complete resolution of the complaint.

                   iv.      A CI shall pay compensation to the complainant if the CI fails to send updated credit information to the CICs by making an appropriate correction or addition or otherwise within twenty-one (21) calendar days of being informed by the complainant or a CIC.

                    v.      The CIC shall pay compensation to the complainant if the CIC fails to resolve the complaint within thirty (30) calendar days of being informed by the complainant or a CI, despite the CI having furnished the updated credit information to the CIC within twenty-one (21) calendar days of being informed by the complainant or the CIC.

                   vi.      The complainant shall be advised by the CI/ CIC of the action taken on the complaint in all cases, including the cases where the complaint has been rejected. In cases of rejection, the reasons for rejection shall also be provided by the CI and the CIC.

                 vii.      Compensation to be provided by the CICs/ CIs to the complainant (for delayed resolution beyond thirty (30) calendar days of filing the complaint) shall be apportioned among the CIs/ CICs concerned based on respective contribution to the period of delay. Illustrative examples of the same are given in Annexure-I of the instant circular.

4.    Where the grievance/ complaint involves inaccurate credit information provided by more than one CI, the complaint shall be registered by the complainant with the concerned CIC. The CIC shall coordinate with all the CIs concerned and furnish the complainant with a comprehensive resolution of the grievance.

5.    Where the complaint has been received and registered by a CIC and there has been a delay in the resolution of the complaint, the CIC shall inform the concerned CI(s) and the complainant after the final resolution, regarding the total delay (in calendar days) and the amount of compensation to be paid by the CI(s) and/ or CIC.

6.    Where the complaint has been received and registered by a CI and there has been a delay in the resolution of the complaint, the CI shall inform the concerned CIC(s) and the complainant after the final resolution, regarding the total delay (in calendar days) and the amount of compensation to be paid by the CI and/ or CIC(s).

7.    The date of the resolution of the grievance shall be the date when the rectified Credit Information Report (CIR) has been sent by the CIC or CI to the postal address or email ID provided by the complainant.

8.    The CICs/ CIs shall make an appropriate provision in their complaint submission format (both online and offline) for enabling a complainant to submit the contact details, email ID, and bank account details/ Unified Payment Interface (UPI) ID for crediting the compensation amount. The onus of providing accurate contact and bank account/UPI ID details will lie with the complainant and the CIs/ CICs will not be held responsible for any incorrect information provided by the complainant.

Internal operational instruction – A - for Stationery and IT departments: - Our current grievance redressal form on the Bank’s website does not provide a box for Bank/UPI details for non-account holders (say some guarantors) who may have a grievance about the credit history reported to a CIC. The Stationery Department shall modify the form to provide for these particulars. The IT department shall post it on the Bank’s website.

9.    The compensation amount shall be credited to the bank account of the complainant within five (5) working days of the resolution of the complaint.

10. The complainant can approach the RBI Ombudsman, under the Reserve Bank - Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2021, in case of wrongful denial of compensation by CIs or CICs.

11.  In case of wrongful denial of compensation by CIs which are yet to be covered under the Reserve Bank - Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2021, thecomplainant can approach the Consumer Education and Protection Cell (CEPC) functioning from Regional Offices (ROs) of Reserve Bank of India.

12.  Non-Maintainability: The compensation framework shall not be applicable in

 the following cases:

                     i.    Disputes for which remedy has been provided under Section 18 of CICRA, 2005. (Section 18 of CICRA, 2005 provides that for disputes arising amongst CICs, CIs, borrowers, and clients on matters relating to the business of credit information for which no remedy has been provided under CICRA, 2005, such disputes shall be settled by conciliation or arbitration as provided in the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996).

                    ii.    Complaints/ references relating to (a) internal administration, (b) human resources, (c) pay and emoluments of staff, and (d) references like suggestions and commercial decisions of the CIC/CI. (iii) complaints about disputes/ grievances regarding the computation of the credit score/ credit score model. (iv) complaints that have been decided by or are already pending in other fora such as Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission, Courts, Tribunals, etc.

13. CICs and CIs which contravene or default in adherence to the above directions shall be liable for penal action as per the provisions of CICRA, 2005.

V.   Intimation of accessing Credit Information Reports (by non-customers) and updation of credit information with CICs, with our Bank’s internal operational instructions wherever necessary.

The RBI has issued several instructions on the subject. The specific instructions from the RBI and internal operational instructions in respect of those applicable to our Bank as a CI are as under.

1.   When customers’ Credit Information Report (CIR) is accessed by the Specified Users (SUs), CICs shall send alerts through SMS/ email to the customers wherever the details of their mobile number or email IDs are available.

·         Explanation: - Customers means “borrower” or “client” defined in section 2. b. and c. of CICRA 2005 who are essentially existing and prospective borrowers of various CIs or their existing or proposed guarantors/sureties).

·         Explanation: -Specified Users are as defined in subsection (l) of section 2 of CICRA, 2005 (which is any credit institution, credit information company being a member under sub-section (3) of section 15, and includes such other person or institution as may be specified by regulations made, from time to time, by the Reserve Bank to obtain credit information from a credit information company.

2.   The alerts shall be sent by CICs only when the CIR enquiry is reflected (i. e. shown) in the CIR of the customer.

3.   CIs shall send alerts through SMS/ email to customers while submitting information to CICs regarding default/ Days Past Due (DPD) in existing credit facilities, wherever the mobile number/email ID details are available.

Internal Operational instruction-B - for IT department:- The IT department will put in place a system of automatically generating text messages to the customers advising submission by the Bank of information to the CICs about their default/Days Past Due status. The text of recommended by the TUCIBIL is as under.

“Dear customer, you have an amount of Rs. ----- overdue for past – days for your account number ending with xxxx (last four digits) with Abhyudaya Bank ----loan (the name of the type/scheme)”

4.   To enable the sending of alerts through SMS/ email, the Uniform Credit Reporting Format for reporting credit information by CIs to CICs has been modified as follows.

                     i.        The nomenclature of the field ‘Fax Number’ is changed to ‘Email ID’ in the Commercial Bureau reporting format.

                    ii.        The nomenclature of the field ‘dummy’ is changed to ‘Email ID’ under the MFI Bureau reporting format.

                  iii.        The above fields continue to be categorised as ‘when available’.

Internal Operational Instruction - C - for Nodal Officer (please see sub-Para 6 below): -The Nodal Officer shall make the necessary changes in the current format to comply with the above.

5.   The RBI has advised CIs to organise special awareness campaigns to sensitise their customers about the benefits of submitting their mobile numbers/ email IDs.

Internal Operational Instruction – D - for IT department: - In compliance of the above, the IT department is hereby advised to post an exhortation the text of which is given below on the Bank’s website for the customers to register their mobile numbers and email IDs with the Bank if not done already.

Internal Operational Instruction – E - for KYC-AML department: - The KYC-AML department is hereby advised to publish an advertisement half yearly giving a suitable message to the customers requesting them to register their mobile numbers and email IDs.

Internal Operational Instruction – F - to Marketing department: - The IT department. Is hereby advised to develop suitable materials exhorting customers to register their mobile numbers and email IDs and supply them to branches for display.

6.   Designation of a Nodal Officer by each CI

The RBI has asked CIs to set up Nodal points and designate Nodal officials with whom CICs can take up customer grievances for redressal. The telephone numbers and email IDs of such Nodal Officers need to be provided

to the CICs. Any changes in the nodal points/ officials shall be communicated by the CIs within five (5) calendar days of such a change.

Internal Operational Decision – G - In compliance of the above, the name of the Nodal Officer of our Bank to receive and send communications to the CICs regarding grievances of customers and his/her mobile no. and email ID shall be communicated to all the CICs. The changes if any made whether of the designated official or his/her mobile number and the email ID shall be communicated to the CIs within the stipulated 5 days.

7.   Root Cause Analysis (RCA) by CIs:-

The CIs are required to undertake an RCA of the complaints at least on a half-yearly basis. The information on data rejected by the CICs and the Data Quality Index (DQI) provided by CICs shall be used by the CIs as sources of information for carrying out the RCA.Analysis of the RCA shall be reviewed by the Top Management of CIs, at least, on an annual basis.

Internal Operational Decision - H - This function is hereby being entrusted to the Bank’s designated Nodal Officer.He/she shall submit the findings of the RCA to the Board on a half-yearly basis.

8.   Informing customers of reasons for rejection of requests for data correction:-

                     i.The CIs are required to inform the customers of the reasons for the rejection of their request for data correction, if any, to enable them to better understand the issues in the CIR.

                    ii.A list of reasons for rejection of requests is expected to be sent by the CICs to all the CIs. When received, the same shall be used by the CIs while communicating the rejections of the request for data correction made by customers/ CICs during the grievance redressal process.

 

Internal Operational Instruction – I - The Nodal Officer shall follow up with the CICs, and particularly TU CIBIL for the said list of reasons for rejection. After the same is received, it shall be made use of while advising the reasons for rejection.

9.   Logical processes and systems used and to be used by the CICs (Except Sr. No. vi. below, the other points under this sub-para are only for information; action in respect of them is expected on the part of CICs).

                     i.        CICs are to undertake at least on a half-yearly basis of the ‘Search & Match’ logic algorithm implemented by them to provide a Credit Information Report (CIR) of a borrower.

 

                    ii.        RCA of the complaints being undertaken by CICs shall be used to identify issues in the existing ‘Search & Match’ logic algorithm. The results will be internally reviewed by them.

                  iii.        CICs shall ingest credit information data received from the Credit Institutions (CIs) as per its data acceptance rules, into their databases within seven (7) calendar days of its receipt from the CIs.

                   iv.        In case of data rejection, CICs shall communicate to the concerned CI, regarding the rejection of the data with reasons, within seven (7) calendar days of receipt of the data.

                    v.        The CICs shall provide easy access to Free Full Credit Report (FFCR) including credit score, once a year (January- December), to individuals whose credit history is available with the CIC by displaying the link prominently on their website (on the Home page itself) so that individuals can access their FFCR conveniently.

                   vi.        The CICs shall disclose on their websites, details of complaints registered against them and CIs.

Internal Operational Instruction - J - The Nodal Officer shall monitor on at least a monthly basis the data displayed on the websites of the CICs regarding complaints received against our Bank, those that are disposed of, and those that remain pending.

 

VI.         Maintenance of two registers by the Nodal Officer

The Nodal Officer shall maintain

1.    one register (Register-1) as per the format given in Annexure-II to the instant circular to record, monitor, and redress the customer grievances and to calculate, determine and pay compensation if any,

and

2.    one more register(Register-2) as per the format given in Annexure-III to the instant circular to compile the information on customer complaints with the RCA exercise conducted by using inputs received from the CICs as part of the half-yearly exercise for submission before the Board.

VII.       Monitoring of complaints by customers pertaining to their credit information     under the internal reporting system on Customer Service

Internal operational instruction – K - to Credit department and Secretarial department: - The Credit department shall submit the status of complaints under this circular to the Secretarial department monthly. The Secretarial department shall include it in the internal reporting system designed to monitor the quality of customer service.

VIII.     The instructions in the instant circular shall be carefully noted by all the members of the staff concerned for meticulous compliance. Incidence of compensation if payable by the Bank to the customers shall be recovered from the staff members found accountable therefor.
