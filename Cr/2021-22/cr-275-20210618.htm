Circular CR No. 275 dated 18th June 2021

COVID-19 PANDEMIC

Resolution Framework 2.0 for Individuals and Small Businesses

________________________________________________________________________________________

 

I.      BACKGROUND

Resolution Framework 1.0

RBI vide its circular DOR.No.BP.BC/3/21.04.048/2020-21 dated 6th August 2020 on “Resolution Framework for COVID-19-related Stress” (“Resolution Framework/1.0”) had provided a window to enable lenders to implement a resolution plan in respect of eligible borrowers viz. individuals and some other categories which briefly included corporate exposures and MSME exposures of all Lending Institutions (LIs) together above Rs. 25 Crore (the term “Small businesses” did not find mention therein), without change in ownership, while classifying such exposures as Standard, subject to specified conditions. The borrowers targeted were those which were “Covid-19-First-Wave-affected”.

A policy framed in accordance with the guidelines contained in the “6th August, 2020 Circular” & within their contours was placed in the Board meeting dated/16th October 2020 which met with approval. Internal instructions in implementation of the said policy were issued vide Circular CR No. 260 dated 3rd November, 2020.

 

II.   Introduction to “RESOLUTION FRAMEWORK 2.0 for Individuals & Small Businesses”

Owing to the uncertainties created by the resurgence of the Covid-19 pandemic in India in the recent weeks [Covid-19 Pandemic (Second Wave)], RBI vide its Circular RBI/2021-22/32 DOR.STR.REC.12/21.04.048/2021-22 dated 5th May 2021 decided to extend the above facility for restructuring existing individual borrower loans and small businesses loans (whether run by an individual or run by an entity in non-individual category) without a downgrade in the asset classification. The said guidelines have been referred to by RBI as “Resolution framework-2.0 for Individuals and Small Businesses.”

The limit for exposure of all LIs together which was Rs. 25 Crore has since been enhanced to Rs. 50 Crore vide circular no. RBI/2021-22/47 DOR.STR.REC.21/ 21.04.048/2021-22 dated 4th June, 2021.

 

III.   MANDATORY CONDITIONS

A.  Borrower Eligibility

1.   The borrower should be an Individual who have availed themselves of loan for personal use such as housing, education or consumption. (No exposure limit is indicated).

The types of loans availed of by individual borrowers eligible under this dispensation are listed hereunder.

·     Consumer credit

·     Education loan

·     Loans given for creation/ enhancement of immovable assets (e.g., housing, house repairs etc.).

·     Loans given for investment in financial assets (shares, debentures, etc.).

2.   The borrower should be an Individual who has availed of loan for conducting Small Business. (Limit for aggregate exposure to LIs prescribed at Rs. 50 Crore)

3.   The borrowers covered are under non-individual category. Limit for aggregate exposure to LIs is prescribed at Rs. 50 Crore. Only those businesses which are not eligible to be classified as MSMEs should be considered under this category. (Reference by RBI to “LI” is henceforth replaced by “Bank” i. e. our Bank.)

(The borrower-accounts / credit facilities to certain categories mentioned in RBI’s instructions on Resolution Framework 1.0 (which essentially means that loans to agri. and allied activities, Governments and Government Companies, local Govt. bodies, financial service providers and some housing finance companies are not to be considered).

4.   The Resolution Framework 2.0 is not applicable for credit facilities provided by Bank against Fixed Deposits and also credit facilities to their own personnel/staff.

5.   Facility is to be extended only to the borrowers having stress on account of COVID-19.

6.   The credit facility(ies) / investment exposure to the borrower must have been classified as Standard as on 31st March 2021.

 

B.  Invocation & Implementation:

i.         The restructuring of the borrower’s account must be invoked by 30th September 2021. For this purpose, the restructuring will be treated as invoked when Bank and the borrower agree to proceed with the efforts towards finalising a restructuring plan to be implemented in respect of such borrower.

ii.       Decisions on applications received by Bank from customers for invoking restructuring under this facility must be communicated in writing to the applicant by Bank within 30 days of receipt of such applications. (In practice, date of communication of positive decision, if taken, will be the date of invocation).

iii.      If multiple LIs have credit exposure to the same borrower, the decision to invoke the restructuring under this facility should be taken by each LI independent of decisions taken by others.

iv.      Restructuring of the borrower account must be implemented within 90 days from the date of invocation.

v.       Compromise settlements are not permitted as a resolution plan.

vi.      The resolution plan shall be deemed to be implemented only if all the conditions as under are fulfilled (which are same as those under Resolution Framework 1.0).

·        Entire related documentation, including execution of necessary agreements between LI and borrower and collaterals provided, if any, is be completed by the LI concerned in consonance with the resolution plan being implemented.

·        Changes in the terms of conditions of the loans get duly reflected in the books of the LI.

vii.    In respect of accounts being considered under all the three categories i. e. A-1, 2 and 3. mentioned above, the resolution in terms of the “Resolution Framework – 1.0” should not have been extended to them; however, this will be subject to the special exemption mentioned in Paras VI-ii.[(adopted) features-Working capital support for small businesses where resolution plans were implemented previously and Convergence of the norms for reschedulement of/moratorium for loans resolved previously].

 

IV.   INTERNAL OPERATIONAL GUIDELINES

1.   Branch- In-Charge to identify the accounts which fulfills the criteria under “Resolution Framework 2.0” for Individuals and Small Business. Due diligence/ process of identification of stress including visits shall be followed by the branch to establish the necessity of implementing Resolution Plan.

2.   The units so identified should be contacted by branches and application as per Annexure 1-A and Annexure 1-B should be invited from individuals and small business respectively. It should be accompanied by supporting papers.

3.   Each application received together with supporting papers and projections if any must be acknowledged under signature, name and Staff No. of the employee receiving the same with date and Branch’s stamp. Application must be recorded in Loan Applications Received Register with full particulars of the receiving employee under signature and date.

4.   Individuals/entities falling under MSME category are not to be considered under “Resolution Framework 2.0 for Individuals and Small Businesses”.

V.     FEATURES UNDER PRUDENTIAL FRAMEWORK ADOPTED BY BANK:

Bank has decided to adopt following features permitted under Prudential Framework for providing relief to eligible customers based on their assessment of income streams/cash flows so as to enable them to address the ongoing stress caused by the current COVID-19 Pandemic:

i.     Re-scheduling of existing outstanding amount.

Such rescheduling of repayment of existing outstanding amount (including amount of interest accrued and to be accrued) shall be by a period not more than 2 years from the date of implementation. Such maximum rescheduling is to be inclusive of moratorium, extended or otherwise.

ii.   Extension of tenure including moratorium granted.

a.   Moratorium may be granted up to a maximum of 2 years from the date of implementation. It would be principal moratorium for the specified period in which customer would be servicing the interest on the outstanding amount.

b.   Under any Personal loan schemes of the Bank, if the total period of repayment or maximum age of the borrowers is getting breached due to Covid-19 moratorium already permitted under the existing RBI guidelines and / or reschedulement or moratorium now permitted under Resolution plan, the same will not be treated as breach beyond stipulated period.

 

iii.  Conversion of interest (Subject to clause VII-ii):

Interest accrued or to be accrued (maximum upto 31st March 2022) to be converted into another credit facility i.e. FITL to be repaid in 12 months. The repayment period will be reckoned / start from next month of the period upto which interest is considered for conversion into FITL.

Branches will follow the guidelines contained in the Annexure 4 in respect of FITL.

 

iv.  Only for Small/Businesses:     Overdrawn/ Irregular component of Cash Credit as on the date of implementation may be carved out and sanctioned as an ad hoc facility to be liquidated in maximum period of 24 months including moratorium period of 3 months in suitable installments in the form of WCTL on case to case basis.

 

VI.   Features for Resolution Plans implemented previously

As a one-time measure, to review sanctioned working capital limits and/or drawing power based on a reassessment of the working capital cycle without the same being treated as restructuring of accounts.

i.     Working capital support for small businesses

a.   In respect of borrowers for whom resolution plans had been implemented in terms of the “Resolution Framework – 1.0, Bank has decided, as a one-time measure, to review the working capital sanctioned limits and / or drawing power based on a reassessment of the working capital cycle without the same being treated as restructuring.

b.   The decision with regard to above is to be taken by bank by 30th September 2021, and the margins and working capital limits are to be restored to the levels as per the resolution plan implemented under Resolution Framework – 1.0, by 31st March 2022./

ii.   Convergence of the norms for reschedulement of/moratorium

In cases of loans to Individuals and Small Business units in respect of whom resolution plans had been implemented in terms of the “Resolution Framework1.0”, but such previous resolution plans did not contain any moratoria, or contained moratoria of less than two years, and / or extension of residual tenor in their cases was by a period of less than two years, instant “Resolution Framework 2.0 for Individuals and Small Businesses” may be made use of to modify such plans only to the extent of increasing the period of moratorium / extension of residual tenor subject to maximum two years.

a.   Consequent changes in the terms of the loan for implementing such extension shall be required to be made.

b.   Modification implemented by increase/extension of moratoria as per permission mentioned above shall also follow the timelines which are applicable for invocation and implementation.

c.    For loans where modifications are implemented in line with the sub Para ii above, instructions regarding asset classification and provisioning shall continue to be as per the “Resolution Framework 1.0”.

d.   The above measures will be contingent on the Bank satisfying themselves that the same are necessitated on account of the economic fallout from COVID-19. This is also subject to supervisory review.

 

VII.  IMPORTANT INSTRUCTIONS

i.         Out of the features listed under Para V above, only those which are appropriate for a particular individual/entity shall be considered for Resolution Framework 2.0.

ii.       It should be endeavor of the branches/ Zonal Offices to restructure or grant moratorium to the borrower’s accounts on the total outstanding in such identified cases, under Resolution Framework 2.0. Only when repaying capacity is not available by the above endeavor, branches/ zonal offices may consider FITL for funding the interest portion.

iii.      The instructions contained in circular CR 260 dated 3rd November 2020 in the matter of “Resolution Framework for COVID-19-related Stress – Financial Parameters” (prescribed by the “Kamath Committee)/shall not be applicable to resolution plans implemented under this window.

iv.      It should be noted carefully that accounts which will be provided relief under the “Resolution Framework 2.0 for Individuals and Small Businesses” will be subject to supervisory review with regard to its justifiability on account of the economic fallout from COVID-19. Therefore, features should be chosen and resolution plans implemented judicially and on merits.

v.       Existing securities shall continue to be extended for the restructured facilities.

vi.      The rate of interest to be applied as per prevailing interest rates applicable to the respective borrower account.

vii.    Processing fees of 0.25% will be applicable only for ad hoc facilities in the form of WCTL.

viii.  Share linkage to be applicable as per extant guidelines in case of WCTL.

 

VIII.  ASSET CLASSIFICATION, PROVISIONING AND OTHER:

i.         Asset classification of the restructured accounts of a borrower classified as standard may be retained as such, whereas the accounts which may have slipped into NPA category between April 1, 2021 and the date of implementation should be upgraded as ‘standard asset’, as on the date of implementation of the restructuring plan.

ii.       Provision shall be maintained from the date of implementation, which will be higher of the provisions held as per the extant IRAC norms immediately before implementation, or 10 percent of the renegotiated debt exposure of Bank post implementation (residual debt).

iii.      Residual debt, for this purpose, will also include the portion of non-fund-based facilities that may have got converted into fund-based facilities due to devolvement after the date of implementation.

iv.      Half of the above provisions may be written back upon the borrower paying at least 20 per cent of the residual debt without slipping into NPA post implementation of the plan, and the remaining half may be written back upon the borrower paying another 10 per cent of the residual debt without slipping into NPA subsequently.

Provided that in respect of exposures other than personal loans, the above provisions shall not be written back before one year from the commencement of the first payment of interest or principal or instalment (whichever is later) on the credit facility with longest period of moratorium.

v.       Subsequent asset classification for such exposures will be governed by the extant IRAC norms applicable to us as a UCB.

vi.      The provisions required to be maintained under this “Resolution Framework 2.0”, to the extent not already reversed, shall be available for the provisioning requirements when any of the accounts, where a resolution plan had been implemented, is subsequently classified as NPA.

Any resolution plan implemented in breach of the stipulations of the “RBI circulars on ‘Resolution framework 2.0 for Individuals and Small Businesses’ “shall be fully governed by the Prudential Framework for Resolution of Stressed Assets issued on 7th June 2019 (“Prudential Framework”).

 

IX.  BOARD APPROVED POLICY

A policy for implementation of the guidelines for “Resolution Framework 2.0” (combined for Individuals and Small Businesses, as also MSMEs) was placed before the Board meeting dated 31st May 2021 and the same met with approval. Accordingly, instructions for implementation of the part of the policy which pertains to Individuals and Small Businesses are being issued hereunder.

Bank may put in place Board approved policy on banks website in a prominent and easily accessible manner.

X.   IMPLEMENTATION

1.  Manpower Allocation -

All the Branches and Zonal Offices will assign manpower for implementation of Resolution Plan in respective branches/offices. The dedicated personnel will be responsible for entertaining a customer, assessing his/her/their requirement, collecting from him/her/them details/information, documents (mentioned in following Paras), and maintaining a separate register in respect of the Resolution plan.

2.  List of Documents and details to be collected

      Documents as per the following list should be submitted by borrower while applying for loan-restructuring under resolution plan.

a.   To be obtained from salaried individual borrower availing loan for consumption or personal use

·        Application with Declaration/ undertaking (detailing the adverse impact on business of COVID-19 pandemic) for Resolution Plan (Application Form attached as Annexure 1-A)

·        Salary slips from January 2021 up to the month of application for restructuring.

·        Detailed bank statements from January 2021 up to the month of application in respect of the account in which salary is credited.

·        Proof of job loss from previous employer (in case of job loss).

 

b.  Individuals with Non-salary income (having borrowings for consumption and personal use, and also having taken finance for business purpose)

·        Application with Declaration/ undertaking (detailing the adverse impact on business of COVID-19 pandemic) for Resolution Plan (Application Form attached as Annexure 1-A)

·        Detailed bank statements and/or operating account statements for the previous 12 months.

·        Income documents for the previous 12 months.

·        GST Returns for H1 FY 2020-21, H2 FY 2020-21 and completed months of FY 2021-22 (if applicable).

·        No statutory dues certificate from CA.

·        Sales and Purchase statement from January 2021 onwards (if applicable).

·        Audited financials for FY 2019-20, Audited/ Provisional financials for FY 2020-21 and projections for next 2 financial years.

·        Stock and receivables statement as on a recent date.

·        Viability study based on project report given by practicing Chartered Accountants with UDIN for exposure above Rs.25 crore for at least 5 years on realistic basis containing sensitivity scenarios for optimum-case and worst- case scenarios.

·        CA certificate with UDIN no. for certifying the exposure of the borrower from all lending institutions shall be obtained for exposure above Rs.15 Crore. In all other cases, branch to ensure that the aggregate exposure is not above Rs.50 Crore either through CIR report/ROC/ balance sheet.

 

c.  Entities in Non-individual category (Small business other than MSME)

·        Application with Declaration/ undertaking (detailing the adverse impact on business of COVID-19 pandemic) for Resolution Plan (Application Form attached as Annexure 1-B)

·        GST Returns for H1 FY 2020-21, H2 FY 2020-21 and completed months of FY 2021-22 (if applicable).

·        No statutory dues certificate from CA.

·        Sales and Purchase statement from January 2020-21 onwards (if applicable).

·        Audited financials for FY 2019-20, Audited/ Provisional financials for/FY 2020-21 and projections for next 2 financial years.

·      Stock and receivables statement as on a recent date.

·        Viability study based on project report given by practicing Chartered Accountants with UDIN for exposure above Rs.25 crore for at least 5 years on realistic basis containing sensitivity scenarios for optimum-case and worst- case scenarios.

·        CA certificate with UDIN no. for certifying the exposure of the borrower from all lending institutions shall be obtained for exposure above Rs.15 Crore. In all other cases, branch to ensure that the aggregate exposure is not above/Rs.50 Crore either through CIR report/ROC/ balance sheet.

 

d.  For Multiple Banking/ Consortium banking

·        All the documents applicable for sole banking would apply here.

·        The customers enjoying Multiple Banking/ Consortium banking would have to furnish following additional documents:

ü  Details of all the facilities- Funded and Non-Funded availed in all other banks and NBFCs.

 

e.  Documentation

Documents as under shall be obtained in each case sanctioned under resolution.

a.   Restructuring Agreement for credit facility to be signed by borrower and all guarantors for individuals and for small business (Annexure 2).

b.   In case of FITL or additional Working Capital Term Loan (WCTL), separate documentation as per extant guidelines to be obtained.

c.    Code no. 586- Borrower signing in language other than in English or are illiterate (wherever applicable).

 

3.  Customer Identification and Scrutiny:

a.   Only those borrowers whose accounts fit in the criteria for consideration under the Resolution Plan and who agree to comply with the conditions prescribed under the Bank’s policy therefor shall be considered for relief under the Resolution Plan.

b.   The documents collected from a borrower will be retained at the branch, or forwarded to the Zonal Office concerned or Head Office, as per levels mentioned in Para "Sanctioning delegation” below.

c.    Key aspects to be considered for scrutiny

The standard of scrutiny should be rigorous. The scrutinizing official should keep in view following aspects while appraising the proposal for a Resolution Plan.

 (While scrutinizing the proposal, separate Scrutiny Notes are to be used for Individual Borrowers & non-individual borrowers. Formats of Scrutiny Notes to be used for cases under the Resolution Framework are posted in Public Folder > Loan HO > Formats).

 

·        General

ü  Whether the borrower is making adequate disclosures about facts.

ü  Whether any contradiction is evidenced by the documents provided by borrower.

ü  Whether borrower has ability to repay the EMIs where moratorium is not opted for.

·        Self-employed and Other Loans

ü  Whether borrower has performed a preliminary assessment of business’s ability to overcome the problems faced due to COVID-19.

ü  Were there any interruptions in income generation/cash flow due to COVID-19 outbreak.

ü  Type of industry/ sector in which the borrower is operating. Effect of COVID-19 on that particular sector.

ü  Borrower’s ability to prepare timely financials or other required information.

ü  Impact of lost revenue & cash flow on business.

ü  Impact due to pricing and volume volatility.

ü  Impact of FOREX fluctuations including impact of hedging arrangements.

ü  Cost associated with temporary suspension of business.

ü  Inability to honor certain terms of contracts/ agreements leading to penalties.

ü  Impact of the ongoing slowdown of overall economy on the sector concerned and further resultant impact on cash flows of business.

5.  Selection of Adopted Feature(s) for Resolution Plan

Branch-In-Charge/Sanctioning Authority may discuss the features of the Prudential Framework adopted by Bank (see Para V above) with the customer so that the best possible alternative may be selected which will enable the customer to address the ongoing stress due to current COVID-19 Pandemic.

 

6.  Communication and Timelines

i.         A written communication to the borrower on the date of sanction or rejection of the proposal shall be addressed by the Branch-In-Charge to the borrower. Record of having sent such communication shall be maintained at the Branch.

ii.       The processes as per sub-paras numbering from 2 to 5 above shall be completed in a period not exceeding 30 days from the date of receipt of the application.

iii.      The above timeline shall not however extend beyond 30th September 2021.

iv.      Date of positive communication to be deemed as date of Invocation

v.       The chosen action must be implemented within 90 days of invocation of the Resolution plan.

 

XI.   PROVISIONING AND REVERSAL

The Provisioning and reversal of provisions to be made as indicated in Para VIII above.

 

XII.  Disclosures and Credit Reporting

i.     Lending Institutions that are required to publish only annual financial statements are required to make the required disclosures in their annual financial statements, along with other prescribed disclosures.

ii.   Credit reporting by the Lending Institutions in respect of borrowers for whom the resolution plan is implemented under the Resolution Framework-2.0 (excluding now reviewed/ recast working capital limits in respect of borrowers who were previously considered under Resolution Framework-1.0) must reflect the “restructured due to COVID-19” status of the account. The credit history of the borrowers will consequently be governed by the respective policies of the credit information companies as applicable to accounts that are restructured.

 

XIII.   CREATION OF NEW FIELD FOR RESOLUTION PLAN

1)  IT dept. has to create a new field for Resolution Plan 2.0 with four sub-fields, viz. (i) Moratorium RP 2.0 and (ii) Rescheduling of repayment RP 2.0, (iii) Conversion of interest RP 2.0 (iv) Ad hoc facility for working capital RP 2.0 - under New Loan Basic details which will facilitate the identification of loans under Resolution Plan for further generation of MIS report to be submitted to higher authorities/ regulators. Branches are instructed to enter data in related field only if benefit is extended to borrower’s account under Resolution framework 2.0. Incorrect data entry may result in wrong reporting to higher authority / Regulatory authority.

2)  MIS dept. will arrange to generate report of the credit facilities for which Resolution Plan is implemented.

3)  Branches shall maintain Register for the loans restructured under Resolution Plan in the format given in Annexure 3.

XIV.   Grievance Redressal Mechanism

1.   The bank has already developed Grievance Redressal Mechanism during Resolution Framework 1.0. with appointment of Mr. Rajesh B. Paralkar as Nodal Officer who will continue for Resolution Framework 2.0.

2.   If any borrower raises any grievance with the branch, officials at the branch or the Branch In-Charge is expected to redress the same to the extent possible.

3.   Branches and the Grievance Redressal Officer shall follow the same norms for grievance redressal in relation to Resolution Framework. The hierarchy for addressing the grievances is already in existence. V. All other operating guidelines specified in the Circular No. 260 dated 3rd November 2020 shall remain applicable.

 

XV. Please take suitable note of these instructions and bring contents of this instant circular to the notice of all the staff members concerned at your Branch/Office/Department and ensure meticulous compliance.
