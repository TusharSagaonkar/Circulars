Circular CR No. 291 dated 1st February 2022

Additional/modified Guidelines in respect of Bank Guarantees

Under RBI’s Master Circular dated 2nd November’ 2021

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 

I.    1. Instructions on the subject of Bank Guarantees (BG) are given in “Credit Policy W.E.F. 15.07.2019” and its Annexure No.24.

 

2. Instructions regarding postponement of recovery of commission pertaining to the claim period and the incidence of stamp duty(with formats of counter-guarantees provided in its Annexures), were issued through the Circular CR No. 192 Dated 2nd August, 2017 titled “Bank Guarantee Commission  for Claim Period to be Postponed till Actual Incidence”.

 

3. Subsequent instructions on the subject of “Reversal of Entries in respect of Expired Bank Guarantees for Management of Risk Capital” have been issued through Circular CR No. 287 dated 20th December 2021.

 

II. 1. Reserve Bank of India has recently come out with “Master Circular- Guarantees, Co-Acceptances & Letters of Credit – UCBs’bearing ref. no. RBI/2021-22/119/Dor.STR.REC.65/09.27.000/2021-22 and dated 2nd November 2021. RBI has updated guidelines in the matter of Bank Guarantees, Co-acceptances and Letters of Credit through the said Master Circular.

 

2. In light of the same, Circular CR No. 289 dated 18th January 2022 has already been issued on the subject of Letters of Credit. Our Bank does not engage itself in the business of co-acceptances.

 

3. Important parts of the RBI communication, which contain additional or modified instructions on the subject of BG are hereby being circularised internally.

 

III.  Additional or modified guidelines on the subject of Bank Guarantee

1. Bank Guarantee in respect of commodities covered under Selective Credit Control

i.   Our current Credit Policy prescribes that we should not issue, either to a Court or to Government, or any other person, a BG on behalf of or on account  of  any  importers guaranteeing payment of customs duty and / or import duty, or other levies, payable in respect of import of essential commodities without taking, as security for issue of such BG, a cash margin equivalent to the amount payable under the same (i.e. 100% cash margin). The term "essential commodities"  shall  mean such commodities as may be specified by the Reserve Bank of India from time to time.

ii. The recent Master Direction under reference from RBI, while keeping the description and definition of the situation and product same as above, indicates a relaxation that such cash margin may be equivalent to one half of the amount payable under the BG but not less than that (i.e. cash margin should be minimum 50%). It has been decided to adopt this relaxation of 50% cash margin against BG in respect of commodities covered under Selective Credit Control subject to discretion of the sanctioning authority to be exercised after due examination of genuineness of request from the applicant.

 

2. Period of Guarantee

i.   In conformity with the stipulation of RBI that UCBs should extend guarantees with short durations so as to manage risk and to protect their financial position, Bank’s current instructions contained in the Credit Policy to the extent of the following continue to hold good.

a.     It is desirable to confine guarantees to relatively short-term maturities up to 12 months.

b.    Branches are vested with powers to issue BG upto the period of 36 months as per requirements of the applicants.

c.     BG for a period exceeding 36 months should generally be against 100% cash security by way of FDRs.

d.    There is a further provision that powers to be flexible with margin while sanctioning issue of BG for a period exceeding 36 months will be vested with sanctioning authority.

ii.  However, under the latest Master Direction of RBI, Bank Guarantee SHOULD NOT, in any case, be issued for a period exceeding ten years.

 

3. Safeguards in Issuance of BG

i.   There are extant instructions  that BG of more than Rs. 1.00 Lakh should be signed by two signatories at the Branch/Department. (It is now being clarified that the signatures should be under respective rubber stamps mentioning Name and Staff Number). All the BGs must be signed and issued in triplicate, the first copy of which will be given to the beneficiary, the second copy to the respective Zonal Office/Regional Office for record, and, the third copy will be retained at the Branch.

ii. a. RBI has now made it mandatory for banks to make it binding on the

    beneficiary of any BG that they should seek confirmation from Controlling office/Head Office of the bank concerned in respect of the BG issued to them.

b. To comply with this, a specific stipulation as follows must now be invariably incorporated in the Bank Guarantee document itself after the three mandatory clauses.

 “Beneficiary is requested to seek confirmation of THE INSTANT Bank Guarantee from Zonal office/Regional Office situated at ________________”.

4. BG on behalf a customer maintaining current account/saving account

i.       We do not normally issue Bank Guarantee on behalf of a customer who maintains only current account/ saving account and does not enjoy any credit facility(ies) with the Bank, unless there is justifiable reason to issue it as a special case in accordance with the delegation of powers. If a decision is taken to issue BG by making such a situation as a special case, several stipulations regarding scrutiny to be done and genuineness of the need of the customer to be satisfied about, are mentioned in the Credit Policy, such stipulations and satisfaction being in accordance with RBI’s instructions on the subject.

ii.     However, when a customer enjoys credit facility(ies) with other banks, our Credit Policy as of now prohibits issue of a BG on behalf such a customer.(It has now been decided to change the policy as per iii. b. below).

iii.   a. The instruction contained in the latest Master Circular of RBI under

reference provides that in those cases where the customers enjoy credit facilities with other banks, the reasons for their approaching the bank for extending the BG should be ascertained and a reference should invariably be made to their existing bankers with whom they are enjoying credit facilities. This implies that after genuineness of reasons for the borrowers of other banks approaching us for BG is established, our Bank may issue BG on behalf of them after fulfilment of all the other necessary conditions.

b. Now that sub-Para a. above implies a permission for such an action in a special case, it has been decided to issue a BG in such a case after ascertaining genuineness of the need of the borrower and obtaining NOC from their existing bankers for the same.

5. Payment under invoked BG

i.       Current Credit Policy requires that Bank must fulfil commitment by making payment under an Invoked BG. However, Credit Policy also provides leeway of 3 days after receipt of the invocation letter during which branch should immediately seek approval of ZO/HO for making payment under such an invoked BG.

ii.   a. The  Master  Circular of  RBI  dwells  at  some  length on need for a

bank to honour commitment to the beneficiary promptly upon invocation of the guarantee and observes that reluctance on bank’s part to honour commitments in respect of invoked guarantees tend to bring the banking system into disrepute.

b. A reading of the recent Master Circular under reference reveals the position that there is no leeway of 3 days available for Bank to refer to matter to ZO/HO and seek approval for making payment under an invoked BG. Thus, it is necessary that prompt payment under a BG upon its invocation is a must.

 

IV.   All other instruction contained in the “Credit Policy W. E. F. 15.07.2019” and the circulars mentioned in Para I above on the subject of BG remain unchanged.

V.    Each Branch-In-Charge, and officials and executives in Zones, are advised to go through the additional or modified guidelines and internal instructions issued in accordance with them. Branch-In-Charge should hold a staff meeting of all the officials and the staff members concerned at the branch and make them aware of the contents of the instant circular.

DGMs / Incharges of Zones / Regional Office are advised to guide the branches under their control suitably. These instructions should also be discussed in the monthly meetings of the Branch-In-charges and Managers. It should be ensured that the proposals of BGs received at their end are processed strictly as per the guidelines contained in the Credit Policy and the circulars referred to above as modified by the instant circular in light of the said Master Circular.

Managing Director
