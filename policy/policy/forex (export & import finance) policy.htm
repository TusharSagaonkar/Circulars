Reserve Bank of India has granted our Bank Authorized Dealer Category - I Licence since November 2007.  As per existing RBI guidelines, our Bank is expected to extend 12% of our total advances towards Export Credit.  Presently, our Export credit is less than 1% of our total advances.  Hence, to achieve this 12% target, branches would be required to strive hard and market our export services extensively to all our customers. Previously, when we were not authorized to deal in foreign exchange, some of our customers must have shifted their accounts to other banks to meet their forex needs.  Now, we should try to bring back these customers in our fold, once more.

 

An Exporter may need short term financial assistance for execution of an export order as a working capital. The working capital cycle completes with the realization of the export bill. The Exporters may require the finance at any stage of this cycle.

 

Export finance can be granted at two different stages:

1.   Pre-shipment finance  &

2.   Post –shipment finance

 

Export Packing Credit

 

Export Packing Credit is granted to the Exporter for purchase of raw materials, processing or manufacturing, cost of packing or transportation for the purpose of execution of the export contract.

 

To fulfill his credit needs, following products may be offered to an Exporter under Pre-shipment credit –

i)             Export Packing Credit ( EPC) in rupees

ii)           Packing Credit in foreign currency (PCFC)

iii)         Advance against Government incentives covered under ECGC policy.

iv)          Advance against receivables- Duty Drawback scheme.

v)            Advance against cheques / drafts received as an advance exports.

 

·        Branch considering export finance should know the following-

1.   RBI guidelines for pre-shipment credit. The detailed guidelines are elaborated in the latest master circular.

2.   Foreign Trade Policy 2004 –09. By referring to this policy, it can be verified whether proposed export is allowed or not, provisions of deemed export etc. This policy is under revision presently.

3.   What are the different provisions of ECGC cover to the Exporter and the bank and also risk classification of country by ECGC.

Following points should be considered while processing the proposal -

·        The finance under pre-shipment limit / post shipment limit can be considered at any branch, but disbursement and operational aspects will be presently restricted at Foreign Exchange Department, Dadar.

·        While assessing the export credit limit to the Exporters, the branch may adopt any of the following method :

1.   Projected Turnover Method or

2.   MPBF Method or

3.   Cash Budget Method

·        Branch should assess and meet the genuine credit requirements of the Exporters promptly and in full measure.

·        While considering the proposal, required information should be obtained from the Exporter in one go. Queries in piece meal should not be made to avoid the delay in delivery of credit to Exporters.

·        Pre-sanction inspection should be carried out as per the information supplied by the Exporter and due diligence should be done as per KYC norms.

·        We may adopt a flexible attitude while considering the proposal such as Debt-Equity ratio, Margin and Security norms etc.

·        But there should be no compromise on the viability of the proposal and integrity of the borrower. These aspects must be thoroughly checked.

·        The Exporter must satisfy the branch about their capacity to execute the export order and that too within the required time frame of the contract.

·        We should judge the expertise of the Exporter to manage the export business.

·        Quantum of finance sought by the Exporter should commensurate with the expected turnover and the cost of inputs required for execution of contract.

·        Branch should find out the standing of the bank and its prime status, which has issued the L/C in support of the export order.

·        Branch should also study the terms of L/C and find out the acceptability of terms specified in the credit.

·        Where L/C has not been received against the export order, the branch may insist on the satisfactory status report on the foreign buyer.

·        Branch should also check the profile of the buyer’s country especially about the financial and political environment. The information may be obtained from HO or ECGC.

·        Dispensation of export credit should be done expeditiously and in time bound manner. The RBI requirement is as follows –

1.   New proposal in 45 days.

2.   Renewal of limit in 30 days.

3.   Ad hoc limit in 15 days.

·        The pre shipment and post shipment limit should be considered simultaneously and in total.

·        Branches are supposed to review the export limits annually. This exercise, they must meticulously do.

 

Customers who are eligible for Export Packing Credit:-

a)   Who have received confirmed orders for export of goods or has received in his favour an export letter of credit.

b)   Whose name is not appearing in the Specific Approval List (SAL) of the ECGC.

c)    They should be having Importer Exporter Code (IEC) Number issued by DGFT. 

 

Criteria for grant of Export Packing Credit:

1.   It is a purpose-oriented, need based advance.

2.   The advance will be granted to those Exporters who have received export order or export L/C in their favour.

3.   If the Exporter has received an export order, it should contain relevant information like- particulars of goods, rate at which the goods will be sold, quantity of goods and mode and date of shipment. It should also contain the mode of payment.

4.   Since the advance is granted for procuring raw materials, initially when the advance is disbursed, there will be no prime security. At that stage, the Exporter will be holding the amount disbursed as trustee till he procures the material for exports. Hence, at a later date they will submit stock statement

·        Disbursement of advance- the packing credit will be disbursed in the form of loan and each contract will have separate a/c. The aggregate position will be reflected in the loan ledger while each a/c will be opened in contract register.

·        It will be easier for the branch to monitor separate a/c and calculation of interest will be convenient.

·        Running account facility may also be given to those Exporters with good credit rating at least AA grading and who receive continuous export orders or deal in seasonal commodity. Running a/c facility may be given those Exporters who comply with  following conditions:

1.   If there is need for running a/c due to continuous order.

2.   The track record of the Exporter is good.

3.   The Exporter should be able to furnish letters of credit or confirm order within reasonable period.

4.   Bank should mark off first debit by way of export credit by first credit by way of negotiation or purchase of bill of FIFO basis.

5.   If the facility is misused, the running account facility should be withdrawn.

 

Quantum of finance-

1.   Quantum of finance is given on FOB value of the export order or L/C or domestic value of the goods, whichever is lower.

2.   Normally insurance and freight charges should not be financed at the initial stage of disbursement.

3.   If the Exporter wants to avail advance against the insurance and freight charges, that can be considered at the later stage of the pre-shipment operations, when the consignment is ready for shipment.

4.   If the export contract is on CIF basis, FOB value can be found out by deducting 15% in case of sea freight and 25% for air freight of the export goods to arrive at the FOB value of the contract.

5.   If the actual value of freight and insurance amount is known, the total amount can be deducted to arrive at the FOB value.

6.   After arriving at the FOB value, required margin can be deducted to finalise the Quantum of finance.

7.   Need based finance should be considered as per the requirement of the Exporter.

8.   Suitable margins should be considered.

 

Fixing margins in Export Packing Credit (EPC)-

1.   It should ensure stake of the Exporter in the finance.

2.   It should take care of the erosion in the value of the security. It helps the branch when the advance becomes NPA.

3.   The advance will not be against the profit margin.

 

Security Documents:

1.   DP Note

2.   Letter of Lien & Set Off

3.   Export Trust Receipt

4.   Deed of Hypothecation

5.   Letter of Guarantee

6.   Insurance Policy to cover full value of stock

7.   ECGC cover

8.   Collateral Security as per sanction terms

9.   Other usual documents.

 

Liquidation of EPC:

1.      The packing credit advance should be liquidated out of export proceeds by negotiation of bill or advance against bill sent on collection.

2.      Thus pre shipment credit gets converted in to post shipment credit.

3.      This conversion is necessary because the ECGC cover under pre-shipment credit will be available only up to the date of shipment.

4.      It is therefore, ensured that as soon as shipment takes place, the liability under pre shipment should be converted in to post shipment liability.

5.      Bank will have operational flexibility to extend following relaxation to Exporters having good track record:

a)      Pre shipment finance may be liquidated from the proceeds of export bill in respect of other contract covering the same or other goods.

b)      But before allowing above relaxation, it should be ascertained why the original proceeds are not available and whether it is commercially necessary and unavoidable.

c)      Existing packing credit may be marked off with proceeds of document against which no packing credit was availed. 

 

·        Whether the advance can be granted more than FOB value or contract amount?

·        Yes. It can be given in the following manner:

 

(I)      Where wastages are involved to execute the contract:

1.      Where the Exporter requires more raw materials due to wastage involved in processing of goods to be exported. This is in respect of agro based products like raw cashew nut.

2.      In this case, if the export order is for Rs.100.00, the Exporter may require stock of cashew worth Rs.125.00 to process the order as per the specifications.

3.      In this case, branch may allow Exporter to liquidate excess finance by sale of cashew shell oil.

 

(II)     Where partial domestic sale is involved:

1.      When the gradation is involved especially in respect of agro-based items like tobacco, paper, cardamom etc. In export of these items, the Exporter will have to hand pick products in to exportable and non-exportable items and then fulfill the specifications of contract.

2.      In such case, the purchase of goods will have to be more than the FOB or otherwise of the value of the contract.

3.      The excess amount of disbursement will be liquidated out of the sale of non-exportable grade of the items.

4.      The rate of interest for excess finance will have to be ECNOS from the ab-initio and this amount will not be eligible for RBI refinance.

5.      The bank will get ECGC cover for the advance.

 

·        Export of De-oiled / De-fated cake :

1.   Branches can give advance to the Exporter of HPS ground nut / de-oiled cake like Soya DOC to the extent of the value of raw material required even though the value thereof exceeds the value of export order.

2.   The advance in excess of the order, normally called as “Oil Proceeds”, is required to be adjusted by sale of oil in 30 days from the date of advance.

3.   If there is delay in deposition of oil proceeds, interest at ECNOS would be charged for the delayed proceeds. 

 

·        Period of Finance-

1.   It is short-term working capital finance.

2.   Normally, the pre-shipment finance will be for the operative cycle or 180 days. However, the period of finance may be extended up to 270 days on manufacturing cycle.

 

·        Rate of interest:

1.   Interest on export finance is charged on concessional basis.

2.   The interests are charged on slab wise basis.

3.   Rate of interest for 180 days is 3.5% below PLR, If the shipment takes place after 365 days, the rate of interest ab-initio will be ECNOS.

4.   If the shipment does take place, the advance will attract interest rate ECNOS + 2.0% penal or takes place partly,  the part amount which is paid out of local proceeds, will attract the same rate. 

 

·        Maintenance of EPC a/c: These a/cs are maintained separately from the domestic advance.

 

·        Follow up and Monitoring –

1.         End use of funds to be ensured after the disbursement of advance.

2.         Regular inspection of stock should be done as per the terms of credit.

3.         Value of stock mentioned in the stock statement should be checked. It should be as per invoice value or market value whichever is less. 

4.         ECGC cover should be obtained

5.         Payment of ECGC fees

6.         Stock statement should be obtained as per terms of sanction.

7.         Insurance for full value of stock should be obtained.

8.         Constant follow up should be made to ensure timely shipment of goods as per contract.

9.         If the export order is backed by an export L/C, ensure that the Exporter in compliance with the terms of credit should prepare the correct set of documents.

10.      If the export credit is misutilized or export does not take place, immediate remedial action should be taken for recovery of advance.

 

Substitution of contract- Substitution of contract is allowed as per RBI guidelines whenever the Importer backs out of the contract entered into for export of goods.

 

Liquidation of Packing credit:

 

The EPC may be paid off in any of the following manner-

1.   Negotiation / Purchase / Discount of Export Bill

2.   Proceeds of bill against which the EPC was not availed.

3.   Rupee finance against the bill sent on collection.

4.   Proceeds of undrawn bill.

5.   Proceeds of first credit in running a/c facility.

 

Sharing of export contract between merchant Exporter and the supporting manufacturer:

1.   While granting EPC, it should be ensured that there would be no double finance.

2.   The finance should be granted against the disclaimer of the Exporter. The banker of the merchant Exporter should preferably countersign it.

3.   It will be preferable if the back to back L/C is opened in favour of the supporting manufacturer.

4.   When export credit has been shared between the two, time limit for availing credit should not exceed the maximum period allowed under pre-shipment credit.

5.   Sharing of credit will be permitted for the goods and not for intermediaries.

 

Export credit to sub-supplier :-

Export Packing Credit may be shared between Export Order Holder (EOH) and sub suppliers by opening the inland L/C specifying the goods to be supplied by the sub suppliers as a part of export transaction.

1.   On the basis of the L/C, the sub suppliers’ bank may grant EPC to manufacture the goods or procure the goods to be exported.

2.   The total period of credit availed by the sub supplier should be within the normal production cycle of the goods.

3.   Total period of credit will be computed from the date of first drawal of packing credit to the date of submission of export documents by EOH.

4.   The EOH will be responsible for exporting of goods with in the time frame of export order or terms of L/C. If any delay takes place in the process, the EOH will be subjected to any penal provisions as per the scheme. This will not affect the sub supplier, once he plays his role in time.

5.    The scheme is intended to cover first stage of production and is not extended to cover suppliers of raw material / components etc to sub supplier.

6.   The finance will be cover by ECGC guarantee.

7.   The EOU/EPZ units supplying goods to another EOU/EPZ are also eligible to avail EPC.

 

Packing Credit for Deemed Export:

1.   In deemed export the goods do not leave the country.

2.   Advance should be considered to suppliers as per Foreign Trade Policy.

3.   It also involves supplies to IBRD / IDA/ ADB or any other multi lateral fund aided projects under global tender which are paid for in free foreign exchange.

4.   This can be granted to eligible Exporters at pre and post shipment stage.

5.   The pre shipment credit will be considered on the same guidelines as mentioned earlier.

6.   However, post supply credit will be available to eligible Exporter only for 30 days period.

 

POST SHIPMENT FINANCE

PURCHASE / NEGOTIATION OF DOCUMENTS

 

Post shipment finance means finance to the Exporter after the goods are shipped. Essentially it is an advance against the receivables, which will be in the form of shipping documents. Post shipment finance can be made available to the Exporters as follows:

          1.       Export Bills Purchased / Discounted

          2.       Export Bills Negotiated

          3.       Rupee Advance against foreign sight / usance Bills

4.       Rupee Advance against Bills sent for Collection

          4.       Advance against Export on Consignment Basis

          5.       Advance against Un-drawn Balances / Duty Drawback

 

Although all these are the ways for providing financial assistance to the Exporters, but the most common are Export Bills Purchased / Discounted and Export Bills Negotiated. The bank is required to follow some of the below mentioned Exchange Control Regulations for all post shipment finances-

 

1.       Exporter should have I.E.C. number and each shipment should accompany the prescribed declaration (GR/ SDF/ PP/ SOFTEX) form in which the value of export declared and duly certified by the customs authority.

 

2.       Shipping documents along with relative GR forms must be submitted within 21 days from the date of shipment. If there is any genuine delay, which is beyond the control of the Exporter, we may condone the delay and accept the shipping documents even after 21 days for the date of shipping.

 

3.       The payment should be received in an approved manner within the prescribed time limit but within a maximum period of six months from the date of shipment.

 

PURCHASE/ DISCOUNT OF EXPORT BILLS – (D/P OR D/A BILLS):

The export bills representing genuine international trade transaction and drawn as per terms of sale contract / firm order may be discounted or purchased by the Bank. Proper limit should be sanctioned for purchase of export bills. Since the purchase is not covered under L/C, risk of nonpayment may arise, more particularly in case of documents under acceptance. To cover such risk for bank as well as Exporter, ECGC offers cover of credit risk through their guarantee / policy at the post shipment stage known as WTPSG – Whole Turnover Post Shipment Guarantee. In addition to this Exporter should be advised to go for separate buyer-wise policy to avail the wide coverage in case of any risk.

 

The documents may be purchased or discounted based on whether the documents are against payment or against acceptance. In case of D.A. Bills Bank retains the control of the goods until the bill is accepted. The advance against such a bill becomes a clean advance. If the bill is not paid, the Importer can be sued for being liable on the bill. Finance should not be normally granted against accommodation bills.

 

If the bill is expressed in foreign currency, rupee equivalent is calculated at bill buying rate. If forward contract is booked, Rupee equivalent should be calculated at the rate at which it is booked. The exchange rate depends on tenor of the bill. Normal transit period is the period, which is between the date of negotiation of the bill to the date of payment. Normal Transit Period for various currencies are given by FEDAI which is presently 25 days.

 

NEGOTIATION OF EXPORT BILLS:

Bills drawn under Irrevocable Letter of Credit may be negotiated without any hitch provided the L/C is of Prime Bank and has been advised by a well-known bank. When export documents, drawn under L/C, are presented to the bank for negotiation, they should be scrutinized carefully with the terms and conditions of Letter of Credit. The operation of the Letter of Credit is governed by U C P D C of the International Chamber of Commerce, Brochure No. 600. Prime Bank may be taken as Banks up to 1000 in no. in world ranking plus all nationalized Banks (Indian PSU Banks) plus State Bank of India and its affiliates plus ICICI / HDFC / IDBI Bank.

 

All the documents tendered for negotiation should be strictly as per terms of L/ C.  It is to be noted that the L/C issuing bank undertakes to honour its commitment only if the beneficiary submits the stipulated documents conforming to L/C terms. Even the slightest deviation from the terms and conditions specified in the L/C can give an excuse to the issuing bank to refuse the reimbursement to the negotiating bank.

 

 

De-linking of foreign currency liability in respect of overdue export bills purchased / discounted / negotiated.

 

Exporters are liable to repatriate foreign exchange in respect of the export made by them. But many a times the export bills are not realized. In such cases the responsibility is cast upon the authorized dealers to make vigorous follow up to realize the bills negotiated or discounted or purchased by them.

 

If the bills are not realized in time, the foreign currency amount of the bills is subjected to exchange fluctuation. To avoid adverse impact of market forces, the Branch should follow FEDAI and RBI guidelines in regard to delinking.

 

The Branch should delink foreign currency liability in case of overdue bills. They should also reverse foreign currency liability from FBP/FBN portfolio

The guidelines are as follows: 

The delinking of liability should be done on the 30th day after -

·        normal transit period in case of unpaid Demand bills and

·        Actual or notional due date in case of Usance bills.

 

However, the bill may be crystallized before the said period of 30th day with specific understanding and written request from the customer.

 

In case the 30th day happens to be a holiday or Saturday, the export bill should be crystallised on the next working day.

 

For crystallization in to rupee liability, the branch should apply the TT Selling Rate or the original Bill Buying Rate whichever is higher. Foreign Exchange Dept. should remove the item from its outstanding foreign bills purchased position.

 

Interest should also be recovered on the bill amount from

·        the date of NTP to the date of crystallization or

·        the due date or the notional due date to the date of crystallization at “ECNOS” rate i.e. PLR + 3%.

 

Swap cost / gain for the period from the due date of the bill to the date of crystallization shall be absorbed by the bank.

Import Finance Policy

 

Generally Finance is made available for import of machineries and capital goods as well as procurement of raw materials. 

 

A machinery and capital goods are considered under Project Finance for which project appraisal has to be done.  The project viability both in terms of technical feasibility and economic viability has to be critically analyzed before a decision is taken to finance the project. 

 

Raw material imports is a part of current assets management.  Hence, assessment can be done on the basis of any one of the following 3 methods:

 

1. Projected Turnover Method

2. MPBF Method

3. Cash Budget Method

 

Normally, for any of the above facilities viz purchase of machinery / capital goods / raw materials customers generally demand issuance  of Letter of Credit.  Even though Letter of Credit is a non fund based facility while considering the same it should be clear that the contingency will be arising certainly and hence same conservative and precautionary attitude should be shown which is shown while considering a Loan proposal. 

 

It should be borne in mind that whenever any advance facility is considered where the import goods are concerned, then, we should also consider granting L/C facility.  Additionally, Forward Contract booking arrangement should be considered.

 

This facility can be extend to a member of a Bank and following precaution should be taken:

 

     i.        Branch should assess and meet the genuine credit needs of the Importers promptly and in full measures. 

 

   ii.        All the required information should be obtained from the Importer at one stretch avoiding piece meal information from the customers.

 

 iii.        There should be no compromise on the viability of the proposal and integrity of the borrower.

 

  iv.        They should be having Importer Exporter Code Number (IEC) issued by DGFT. 

 

    v.        They should not be on the caution list of the Reserve Bank of India. 

 

  vi.        The goods to be imported can be imported freely under the existing trade policy.  If the goods are appeared in the negative list they should be holding import licence for the same. 

 

vii.        It should be verified that when the documents are received they will be in a position to retire the documents from their own sources including the custom duty component.  Else, they should have sanction limit for term loan from where the amount will be disburse for retiring the documents. 

 

viii.        Issuance of letter of credit is subject to UCPDC (600) of ICC.

 

  ix.        Wherever LC is to be issued for higher amount exceeding Rs. 50 Lakh a credit report on the prospective overseas supplier should be obtained and kept on record. 

 

   x.        Generally Chartered party Bill of Lading as also lash bill of lading should be avoided.  If unavoidable, then Insurance cover should be taken to cover this risk.

 

  xi.        Deck shipment should be generally avoided but if it is unavoidable additional insurance cover for deck shipment should be taken. 

 

xii.        For high value LCs especially beyond Rs. 50 Lakh, shipping certificate issued by Lloyd’s or equivalent should be obtained.  Similarly pre-shipment inspection certificate should be insisted upon. 

 

xiii.        Cash margin of atleast 25% should be obtained at the time of opening the LCs.  If the customer tenders a Term Deposit Receipt for equivalent amount it may be accepted duly discharged alongwith a letter of appropriation and set off.  Lien should be marked in the ledger folio as also on the receipt. 

 

xiv.        The imported goods should be always consigned to the order of our Bank.

 

 xv.        If the LC is to be opened on usance basis for usance period beyond 90 days then if the documents are calling for a Bill of Exchange drawn on the Opener, it should be adequately stamped as per the Indian Stamp Act. 

 

xvi.        For meeting the import liability if the customer wishes to hedge his position by booking a forward contract it can be accepted.  Alternatively, we should insist that our Importer customer should book a forward contract in his own interest and a letter to that effect should be issued to him. 

 

xvii.        If Advance Remittance has to be effected it is permitted provided the overseas suppliers is insisting for the same and is not ready to ship the goods without advance remittance.  In such cases documentary evidence has to be obtained. 

 

xviii.        If the amount of advance remittance exceeds USD 1 Lakh or equivalent then before effecting the remittance foreign bank guarantee should be insisted upon which will clearly state that the amount remitted in advance will be refunded if goods are not shipped.

 

xix.        Documentary evidence by way of either Post Parcel Wrapper or Bill of Entry should be insisted upon wherever the value of the LC amount exceeds USD 25000.
