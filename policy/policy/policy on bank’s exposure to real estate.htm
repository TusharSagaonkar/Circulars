Reserve Bank of India vide letter No. UBD/MRO/BSSI/3420/12.07.006/     2008-09 dated 12.2.2009 has asked our Bank to frame the comprehensive Loan Policy including detailed guidelines on exposure to Real Estate Sector and to bring down the exposure to Real Estate Sector within the stipulated norms.

 

          In view of the above, following guidelines are framed on approval of the Board, on exposure to Real Estate Sector:-

 

I]       Definition:-

The Real Estate exposure is defined as Bank’s exposure to essentially fund/non-fund based credit line/outstanding, recovery of which ultimately relatable to and directly dependent on and the realisability of Real Estate property.

 

II]     Objectives:-

(i)           To provide proper and consolidated guidelines to operating units for extending finance to Real Estate Sector so as to ensure orderly growth of the portfolio.

(ii)          To establish an appropriate credit risk environment and control system in the Bank to manage credit risk inherent in financing the real estate business.

(iii)        To ensure proper pricing mechanism that the return compensates for the capital charge and for the risk involved.

(iv)         To put in place proper Management information system for the orderly development of the portfolio.

(v)          To ensure compliance with all the directives/guidelines issued by the Government/Reserve Bank of India and other legal/regulatory requirements.

(vi)         To have Board oversight on Real Estate exposure through a continuous and structured Reporting system.

 

III]    Classification of Advances under Real Estate:-

          Direct Exposure:-

 

(i)           Residential Mortgages (inclusive of mortgage loans granted for personal purposes against security of residential properties)

(ii)          Commercial Real Estate

(iii)        Investment in Mortgage Backed Securities and other Securitized Exposure- Residential and commercial.

(iv)        Exposure Relatable to other revenue streams, but are secured by and dependent on value of Real Estate property (e.g. Rent Securitization)

 

Indirect Exposure:-

Fund and Non-Funded exposure on National Housing Bank (NHB) and Housing Finance Companies (HFCs), Housing Boards, other public Housing Agencies etc.

 

Residential Mortgages:-

All Housing Loans sanctioned for construction/repair or purchase of houses for residential accommodation. Housing Loans are segregated under following broad heads:-

(a)          Home Loans upto Rs. 20.00 Lakh forming part of all Priority Sector Advances.

(b)          Other Housing Loans - Maximum Limit is fixed at Rs. 50.00 Lakh.

 

Commercial Real Estate:-

(i)           Advances secured by mortgages on commercial Real Estates (Office Buildings, Retail Space, Multi-Purpose Commercial Premises,          Multi-Family Residential Buildings, Multi-tenanted Commercial Premises, Industrial or Warehouse space, Hotels, Land acquisition, development and construction etc.) will broadly be covered under this head.

(ii)          Following categories of exposures will form part of commercial Real Estate:-

(a)      Advances to Builders/Developers

(b)      Advances for Office Buildings, Multi-Purpose Commercial Premises

(c)      Advances under Mortgage Scheme

(d)      Advances under Rent Securitization Scheme

(e)      Loans for construction of Hotels/Resorts/Amusements Park.

(f)       Advances for construction of Godown/Cold Storages

(g)      Loans/OD purely against the Title Deeds even though for purposes other than Real Estate activities.

(h)      Advances granted for construction of  Hospitals, Nursing Homes, Educational Institutions, Health, Club, Cinema Hall etc.

(i)       Advances to Civil Contractors for construction of factory/Building Houses.

(j)       Exposure to entities for setting-up Special Economic Zones (SEZs) or for acquisition of units in SEZs, which includes Real Estate.

 

Note: (i)  Bank shall not sanction advances merely for purchase of plots, industrial, residential or otherwise except where the plot is purchased for the project and entire project finance is considered.

         (ii) Our Bank has represented to RBI as well as IBA requesting them not to include loans given to Educational Institutions to be clubbed under Commercial Real Estate exposure in view of the fact that repayment is not dependent on Building and the advances is for infrastructure development of national priority. If considered by RBI, such loans will be excluded from the exposure to Real Estate.

 

 

 

 

 

IV]    Exemption to Real Estate Exposure:-

Credit facilities granted for the following purposes will not form part of Real Estate:-

(i)           Advances granted for construction for own Factory/Office/Shop/ Building/Staff Quarters/Guest House etc. to the Borrowers which are essentially an integral part of project funding.

(ii)          All infrastructure advances.

(iii)        All staff Housing Loans.

(iv)         Advances to Civil Contractors for works of Public Works Department (PWD)  and Irrigation Department.

(v)          Advances to civil contractors for construction of Factory/Building Houses where the civil contractors are working purely as construction contractors without having any stakes in the ownership of the property is not to be considered as exposure to Real Estate.

 

V]     Overall Exposure Ceiling:-

RBI has fixed a maximum limit of 15% of the total Deposits of the Bank for the previous year ending for the purpose of providing Housing Loans and other Block Capital exposure. We have further put exposure  ceiling of 15% of Gross Advances for Housing Loans, 16% for Loans for Educational Institutions and 10% for Builders within the overall exposure limit. Wherever, the present outstanding has exceeded the above limits, we shall  gradually reduce the outstandings until they reach to the limits fixed above.

 

VI]    Loans to Value Ratio (LTV):-

Whenever outstanding to loans exceeds LTV, branches should either recover the excess amount or obtain other collateral securities to cover the excess outstanding above prescribed LTV.

VII]   Margin:-

Margin as prescribed from time to time should be paid or maintained at the time of disbursement of loan. Margin paid by way of cash cannot  be  considered  unless duly stamped cash receipt is produced alongwith  Certificate from the practicing C.A./Auditors certifying the genuineness of the transaction and inclusion of the same in the Balance Sheet of the Borrower.

 

The present Margin for Housing Loan is 25% for Loan upto Rs. 20.00 Lakh and 30% for Loans  above Rs. 20.00 Lakh.  For Mortgage Loan the margin is fixed at 40% and for Builders & Developers 50%. In case of Builders & Developers, the Margin for land is 100% and for construction activities 50%. No loan shall be granted for the purpose of  purchase of land. 

         

VIII] Tenor of Loan:-

(i)       Tenor of loans shall not generally extend beyond 3 years in respect of single construction project and 5 to 7 years in respect of multiple housing projects. Wherever repayment is out of the rentals to be received from prospective tenants, the same shall be properly tied-up.

(ii)      For Residential Home/Housing Loans, the repayment period shall be upto 15 years in case of salaried people and upto 10 years for professional and business persons.

(iii)    For repayment EMIs will be fixed. However, in case Bank feels that the repayment at initial years to be reduced which will be compensated considering the increase in income of the Borrower in later years, Bank may consider fixing up of Graduated Repayment Schedule.

(iv)     In case of Rent Securitization loans to Graduated Repayment Schedule can be fixed considering the increase in the Rent amount after specific period. However, total repayment period under Rent Securitization shall not exceed the balance lease period or 10 years whichever is earlier.

(v)      While fixing repayment schedule, the Bank shall consider cash flows arising out of underlying project.

 

IX]    Assessment of Finance:-

(i)       Since financing to Real Estate ventures are generally short term in nature, the assessment of finance is required to be carried out on the basis of cash budget statement provided by the borrower. It is to be ensured that every component of cash budget is thoroughly vetted with respect to realistic value of construction cost, loan disbursals matching with actual progress in construction supported by Chartered Engineers/Architect Certificate, reasonable profit margin, advances from customers envisaged as part of means of finance should also form part of cash budget. The peak deficit envisaged in the project is the requirements of Bank finance.  It should also be ensured by field functionaries that the actual construction and the inflows/outflows made out in the cash budget are verified during field inspections at regular intervals.

(ii)      Minimum DSCR should be 2:1 for acceptability of the project, but repayment stipulation to be guided by 1.5:1 principle.

(iii)    In cases where repayment is out of sale proceeds of property (flats, houses etc.) proposed to be constructed, such sale  proceeds are to be factored while arriving at the DSCR.

(iv)     Debt Equity for the project shall be maintained at not more than 2:1.

(v)      Sanctioning Authority shall explore the possibility of establishing escrow arrangement whereby the sale proceeds are deposited directly in the designated account.

(vi)     Housing Loan for flats under construction shall not be considered unless the applicant gives ECS/SDL, or project is approved by the Bank or equivalent collateral security is given in addition to the proposed flat.

 

X]      Guarantee:-

(i)       Bank shall make an endeavour to obtain personal guarantee of Partners, Trustees and Promoter Directors in case of loans to Partnership Firms, Trusts and Companies in addition to two outside sureties.

(ii)      Wherever third party properties are offered as collateral security, guarantee of the property owners shall be obtained.

(iii)    All Borrowers, Property Owners and Sureties for loans above Rs. 25.00 Lakh shall be Regular Members.

(iv)     Sureties for all Loans below Rs. 25.00 Lakh shall be Nominal Members.  

 

XI]    Interest Rates:-

(i)       Interest Rates will be communicated to the Branches from time to time for different types of Advances against Real Estate and Commercial Real Estate Advances.

(ii)      Credit Rating (Grading) is compulsory for all loans above Rs. 25.00 Lakh except for Housing Loans, Premises Loans, Rent Securitization and Mortgage Loans. However, fixing of Rate of Interest is not linked to the Credit Rating in case of loans to Educational Institutions. In case of Educational Institutions if the Credit Rating is not completed before 31st December of the year for the reason of not submitting audited Balance Sheet and Books of Accounts, penal interest of 1% will be charged from 1st January of subsequent year until the Grading is completed.

(iii)     During the moratorium period, the Borrower has to service the interest on the outstanding loans regularly on monthly basis.

         

XII]   Valuation of Properties:-

The Bank shall comply with the following criteria/stipulations for empanelling “Valuers”.

(i)           The Bank shall empanel only those Valuers, who are members of the Institute of Valuers and registered as a Valuer under section 34B of the Wealth Tax Act.

(ii)          In case of professionals like Architects/Engineers who are being empanelled as Valuers/Engineers, the Bank shall empanel only those professionals who are members of their respective governing associations.

(iii)        Bank should obtain Valuation Reports from the approved Valuer of the Bank if the value of the property is above Rs. 15.00 Lakh.

(iv)         If the value of the property is below Rs. 15.00 Lakh, the Branch Manager has to give their opinion about the market value of the property.

(v)          Bank shall obtain minimum two independent Valuation Reports for properties valued at Rs. 5.00 crore and above.

(vi)         The Valuation Report is valid for the period of 3 years only. Hence, Branches should obtain fresh Valuation Reports once in a every three years from the empanelled Valuers.

 

XIII] Documentation:-

          The Documentation should be completed as per the detailed guidelines issued in Manual of Documentation of our Bank and further instructions issued from time to time in this regard.

(i)           All Housing Loans and other Commercial Real Estate Loans upto Rs. 50.00 Lakh shall be mortgaged by way of Equitable Mortgage.

(ii)          All other Commercial Real Estate Loans above Rs. 50.00 Lakh shall be mortgaged by Regd. Equitable Mortgage except whenever land is a part of mortgaged property.

(iii)        In all other cases, properties shall be mortgaged by way of Regd. Simple Mortgage.    

 

XIV] Reporting to RBI:-

          The Bank shall report to RBI on exposure to Real Estate in the prescribed format as given in the Annexure. The Branches should also submit the details in the format given in the Annexure.

 

XV]   Modifications & Review:-

          The Policy will be reviewed every year and also when needed to give effect to the guidelines that may be advised by the RBI from time to time.
