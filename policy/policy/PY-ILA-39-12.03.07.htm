Circular ILA No.  39 dated  12   March 2007

 

Interest Rates on Loans and Advances

 

           Interest Rates on Loans and Advances were last revised by our bank in June 2006. The revised rates of interest were introduced, effective 1 July 2006 and they continue to be in force  till date except  for the revision in respect of the interest rate on loans and advances granted to educational institutions which was reduced  w.e.f. 1 October 2006. Subsequently, very significant changes have taken place in the economy. Being concerned about the growth in the rate of inflation, Reserve Bank has initiated measures like raising  the  repo rates,  increased the CRR requirements and for ensuring quality of bank credit, it also raised the provisioning  requirements  on bank advances under standards assets   in certain categories of advances.  In order to offset the impact of these measures  taken by RBI,  the inflationary  pressure, shrinking  margins, etc. forced most of the banks to raise  their lending rates for advances to most of the segments of borrower  categories in the last 2/3 months.  However, in the interest of our customers and for attracting new/good borrowers, our bank has delayed  any upward revision of the lending rates for the past  some time but the position in this regard was, however, reviewed by the Board  in the meeting held on 21 February 2007 and it has been decided to revise the lending rates effective  1st  April 2007.

3.      Credit discipline:

 

          In order  to inculcate the desired credit  discipline  among the borrowers, branches are advised to address letters to all the borrowing customers specifically  emphasizing the importance of adherence to credit discipline and  that the bank will be levying penal interest for non-adherence to the discipline (Format B). It may also be pointed out to the borrowing customers that  penal interest @ 3% p.a. applicable  for the period of default will be levied in cases of the following defaults :

 

(i)      Non-repayment   of    loan   installments

 

(ii)     Non-payment of interest

 

(iii)    Availing    excess  over   sanctioned limit or excess over   Drawing Power

 

(iv)    Non-renewal/non-review above 3 months of cash credit limit due to non-submission of documents. In case of unreviewed CC A/cs default  period will  end  on 1st day  of next  month, after  receipt of all documents for review.

 

(v)     Bills discounted   on   due   dates

 

(vi)    Submission of audited Balance  Sheet  and  Profit   &   Loss  Account   for   March after 30th  November of the immediately following financial year. This is applicable  for  Credit Rating of borrowal accounts.

 

(vii)   Turnover in the account less than 25% of the limits.  Turnover  charges at the penal rate shall be applied for half yearly  period for borrowers enjoying  facilities for round the year business and at yearly intervals for borrowers engaged in business of seasonal nature in terms of guidelines in Circular OC No. 376 dated 14.12.2006.

 

(viii)   Non-submission of Stock and Book  Debt statement at monthly  intervals. Such statements showing the position of Stocks/Book Debts on the last day of the month should be received  latest by 15th of the following month, Circular OC No. 311 dated 14.7.2005 should be followed  for guidance  in this regard.

 

 

(ix)    Auditor certified   Book debts statement : As per  the decision of 35th  Audit Committee Meeting, borrower enjoying CC & WCTL limit with limit above Rs. 25.00 lacs and also with  component of Drawing Power  against  Book Debt  above Rs. 25.00 lacs, are required to submit auditor certified Book Debt statements as on 30th June  and 31st December every year.  These statements are required for Stock Audit purpose. These accounts will be charged penal interest @ 3% p.a. in case of non-receipt of Auditor certified Book Debt statements by 31st August and 28th February. However, Book Debt statements alongwith stock statements without certificate by Auditor will be accepted  by 15th  of succeeding month and reckoned for Drawing Power.

 

4.      Conclusion :

 

          It may be pointed out that our lending rates are in alignment with the market conditions and rates charged by other competing banks. Our customers are not, therefore, likely to switch over to other banks. On the other hand branches should strive 

to widen their loan business and customer base by offering efficient customer service ensuring at the same time that the quality of our loan assets remains good and the borrowers adhere to the prescribed credit discipline. In this context it may be kept in mind that of late RBI has manifested  concern for the quality  of bank  credit  and scaled  up provisioning  norms  even  for advances in some  standard  category  assets. Branches  therefore have the responsibility of  ensuring borrowers’ adherence to the standards of credit discipline for ensuring appropriate quality of credit assets.
