ABHYUDAYA CO.OPERATIVE BANK LTD.

 

Policy on Know Your Customer Standards and

Anti Money Laundering Measures.

1. Preamble 

In terms of the guidelines issued by the Reserve Bank of India on Know Your Customer (KYC) Standards and Anti Money Laundering  (AML) Measures, vide RBI Circular  UBD/ PCB  Cir  30/09.161.00/2004-05 dated 15 December 2004 the bank is required to put in place a comprehensive policy framework covering KYC Standards and AML Measures duly adopted by the Board. Compliance of the instructions is mandatory because the circular is issued u/s 35 of BR Act.  The guidelines issued by the Reserve Bank of India are comprehensive and the recommendations on AML Standards are also in line with international banking practices on combating financing of terrorism. The bank's policy on KYC Standards and AML Measures is formulated in conformity with the Reserve Bank of India's guidelines set out in the above circular and incorporates the bank's approach to customer identification procedures, customers profiling based on risk perception, procedures for monitoring of transactions on an ongoing basis to prevent misuse of bank's services for financial crimes like frauds, money laundering, financing of terrorism, etc. and management supervision over implementation.  Keeping in view Reserve Bank's directions in the matter the Board of Directors of the bank have laid down the policy framework detailed in the following paragraphs and have approved it for implementation.

2.  Scope 

The policy is applicable to all the branches and departments of the bank.  The Executives, Officers and the staff have to ensure that the policy is implemented effectively in their dealings with the customers so as to safeguard the bank and the society in general, against financial crimes and assist the law enforcing authorities in combating financing of crime and terrorism through the use of banking channel. This policy document contains not only the outline of the policy adopted by the bank on KYC guidelines and AML Standards prescribed by the RBI but also procedural instructions, operational guidelines, etc, for achieving the objectives of the Policy.  This policy document is to be read in conjunction the procedural instructions, operational guidelines, etc, on the related aspect like account opening procedure, instructions for monitoring of accounts, etc, issued from time to time in the past and as may be issued in future in respect of KYC/ AML procedures.   The existing instructions on the subject will continue to remain in force so far as they are not contradictory to or inconsistent with this policy and will stand supplemented/ amended to the extent of the policies and procedures indicated hereafter in this policy.

 

3.  KYC Standards and ALM Measures  

A.  Definition of a Customer:   A Customer of the bank means;

(i)               a person or an entity that maintains an account and/or has a business relationship with the bank.  

(ii)            one on whose behalf the account is maintained (i.e. the beneficial owner)

(iii)          beneficiaries of transactions conducted through the bank by professional intermediaries, such as Stock Brokers, Chartered Accountants, Solicitors etc. as permitted under the law, and

(iv)          any person or entity connected with a financial transaction which can pose significant reputation or other risks to the bank.

B.  Anti Money Laundering Measures

The KYC Standards and AML Measures recommended by RBI basically have the same objectives.  While KYC Standards will help containing financial crime because of proper customer identification, the AML Standards are a part of international finance community's effort to assist law enforcement authorities in combating financial crime and prevent financing of terrorism through banking channel.  The meaning of money laundering, its implications for banking in general and our bank's policy in the context of discharging the bank's responsibilities to assist the authorities within the framework of this policy are explained in this section.  The Key Elements of KYC/ ALM Policy are specified thereafter followed by detailed statement of procedural aspects of the policy.

 

        B.1    Definition of Money Laundering Measures

 

(i)     The Prevention of Money Laundering (PML) Act 2002 has defined the "offence of money laundering" as under:

 

"Whosoever directly or indirectly attempts to indulge or knowingly assists or knowingly is a party or is actually involved in any process or activity connected with the proceeds of crime and projecting it as untainted property shall be guilty of offence of money laundering".

 

 (ii)   For the purpose of this policy, the term 'money laundering' would also cover financial transactions where the end use of funds goes for terrorist financing irrespective of the source of the funds.

 

(iii)   Money launderers use the banking system for cleansing 'dirty money' obtained from criminal activities with the objective of hiding/ disguising its source.  The process of money laundering involves creating a web of financial transactions so as to hide the origin and true nature of these funds.

 

B. 2   Objectives of AML Measures : The major objectives of AML Measures will be as under:

(i)               To Prevent criminal elements from using the bank for money laundering activities.

(ii)            To enable the bank to know/understand the customers and their financial dealings better, which will help the bank to manage risks prudently.

(iii)          To put in place appropriate controls for detection and reporting of suspicious activities in accordance with applicable laws/ laid down procedures.

(iv)          To comply with applicable laws and regulatory guidelines.

(v)             To take necessary steps to ensure that the relevant staff are adequately trained in KYC/AML procedures.

 

        B.3    Obligations under PML Act 2002

Section 12 of PML Act 2002 places certain obligations on every banking company, financial institution and intermediary, which include

(i)               maintaining a record of prescribed transactions

(ii)            furnishing information of prescribed transactions to the specified authority.

(iii)          verifying and maintaining records of the identity of its clients.

(iv)          preserving records in respect of (i), (ii), (iii) above for a period of ten years from the date of cessation of transactions with the clients.

B.4    Money Laundering- Risk Perception

Banks are exposed to the following risks which arise out of Money Laundering activities:

 

·        Reputation Risk

Involvement/ association in money launder may cause loss of reputation of the bank.   This will be of concern, as the bank's business requires the confidence of depositors, creditors and the general market place.

 

·        Compliance Risk

Risk of failure of compliance with key regulations governing the bank's operations which may entail punitive  action by the regulators.

 

·        Operational Risk

Risk of loss resulting from inadequate or failed internal processes, people and systems, or from external events.

 

·        Legal Risk

Risk of loss due to any legal action the bank or its staff may face due to failure to comply with the law.

 

C.  Key Elements of the KYC/AML Policy

As the objective of the policy on KYC/AML Measures Standards is prevention of financial crimes and money laundering through the bank as indicated in the Preamble, the key elements of the Policy, consistent with and complementary to the objective   will be:

 

·        Customer Acceptance Policy

·        Customer Identification Procedures

·        Monitoring Transactions and

·        Risk Management

 

C.1  Customer Acceptance Policy

The bank will :

(i)               classify customers into various risk categories and based on risk perception decide on acceptance criteria for each category of customers;

(ii)            accept customers after verifying their identity as laid down in Customer Identification Procedures;

(iii)          not open accounts in the name of anonymous / fictitious / benami persons;

(iv)          not accept accounts of persons with criminal background and/or having connections with terrorist organization/s.  Information relating to persons with criminal background and/or having contacts with terrorist organizations will be shared with the branches from time to time.  Branches will also be guided by the information available in public domain (including RBI communications in this regard) for the purpose.

(v)             not open an account and will close an existing account, where its branch with which such account is opened/ maintained is unable to apply appropriate customer due diligence measures i.e. branch is unable to verify the identity and / or obtain documents required as per the risk categorization due to non-cooperation of the customer or non-reliability of the data/ information furnished to the bank and in case of closure, the bank (branch) will be giving due notice to the customer explaining the reasons for such a decision.

(vi)          ensure while carrying out due diligence that the procedure adopted will not result in denial of banking services to general public, especially those who are financially or socially disadvantaged.

C.2 Customer Identification Procedure

(i)               The Customer Identification Procedure will be carried out at the following stages:

·        while establishing a banking relationship;

·        notwithstanding identification provided at the time of establishing banking relations, also when the bank feels it is necessary to obtain additional information from the existing customers based on the conduct or behaviour of the account, it will obtain such information from the customer.

·        Wherever applicable, information on the nature of business activity, location, mode of payments, volume of turnover, social and financial status, etc. will be collected.

 

(ii)            The information to be collected for the customer identification purpose  before establishing banking relationship will include:

a)    the purpose and reason for opening the account or establishing the relationship.

b)    the anticipated level and nature of the activity that is to be undertaken

c)     the expected origin of the funds to be used within the relationship

d)    details of occupation/employment of the prospective customer and the sources of wealth or income that will channel through/ be utilized in operation in course of banking relationship of the prospective customer.

(iii)          The most important requirement under the customer identification procedures will be to be satisfied that the prospective customer is really the one who he/she claims to be. The identity has to be verified in such a manner for :

·        the named account holder (such as natural persons/individuals).

·        beneficial owners (such as demat  account holders).

·        signatories to an account (such as directors of a company) and

·        intermediate parties. (such as trustees, power of attorney holders, etc.).

(iv)          The prospective customer will be required to furnish latest photograph/s of the person that will be eligible to operate on the banking relationship account/s.

(v)             Another important requirement under this procedure will be to ensure that sufficient information is obtained on the nature of the business that the customer expects to undertake, and any expected, or predictable pattern of transactions.

 

The same Customer Identification procedures as above will be carried out also in respect of non-account holders approaching bank for high value one-off transaction.

The information for the customer identification procedure will be gathered mostly through the account opening form/application for credit facilities and will be used also as the customer profile.

C.4    Monitoring of Transactions

(i)               Ongoing monitoring is an essential element of KYC policy and procedure. Risk can be effectively controlled and reduced if there is an understanding of the normal and reasonable activities of the customers, so that transactions that fall outside the regular pattern of activity can be identified.  Monitoring of transactions will also be done taking into consideration the risk profile of the account. 

 

(ii)            In monitoring of account the following categories of accounts and  the under noted operational features observed in those as well as other accounts, should be subjected to special attention and detailed scrutiny where reasonable explanation/ justifications for types of operations cannot be found: -

  • Transactions in newly opened accounts.
  • Higher risk perception.
  • Accounts involving large amounts of cash transactions inconsistent with the normal/ expected activity in the account.
  • Regular transfers between group/ related accounts.
  • Large value transactions in accounts, which are inconsistent with the profile/ nature of business of the customer.

 

  • Special attention should be paid to all complex, unusually large transactions and all unusual patterns, which have no apparent economic or visible lawful purpose.

 

(iii)   A major indication of the transactions of suspicious nature will be that such transactions will be inconsistent with a customers known legitimate activity or with a normal business for the particular type of account.  An illustrative list of suspicious transactions is furnished as Annexure A.

 

(iv)   A transaction or a series of transactions would be considered "suspicious" if the transaction/s appears to be inconsistent with the customer's legitimate business or personal activities or known transaction profile or if it does not make economic sense.

 

Some examples of suspicious activities / transactions to be monitored by the operating staff will be as under:

·        Large Cash Transactions

·        Multiple accounts under the same name

·        Frequently converting large amounts of currency from small to large denomination notes.

·        Placing funds in term Deposits and using them as security for more loans

·        Large deposits immediately followed by transfers

·        Sudden surge in activity level

·        Same funds being moved repeatedly among several accounts.

·        Multiple deposits of money orders, Banker's cheques, drafts of third parties.

·        Transactions inconsistent with the purpose of the account.

·        Maintaining a low or overdrawn balance with high activity.

 

(v)    Branches will also continue to maintain proper record and report of all cash transactions (deposits and withdrawals) of Rs. 5 lakh and above as per extent practice.

 

(vi)   Anti  Money Laundering

 

Branches will also monitor transactions in customers' accounts with a view to ensuring compliance of the provisions of the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 and preventing financing of terrorist activity.  A check list of transactions suggestive of money laundering activity is given in Annexure B.

 

 (vii)  After due diligence at the level of the concerned Zonal Office and Inspection Dept and the GM in-charge of Inspection Dept the transaction of suspicious nature and/or any other type of transaction notified under PML Act, 2002 will be reported to the Principal Officer of the bank for onward reporting to the appropriate authority and a record of such transactions will be preserved and maintained for a period as prescribed in the Act.  The preservation period for records relating to transactions falling under the PML Act,2002 prescribed u/s 12 of said Act is 10 years from the cessation of the transaction.

 

        (viii)  Terrorist finance

Branches will also refer to the lists of terrorist organizations/ banned organizations circulated by RBI from time to time to check existence of account of such organizations and initiate appropriate action before opening of any new accounts in future.  The lists of such organizations/ individuals supplied to branches will be made available at all counters in the branches.  Branches will not open accounts in the names of any such terrorist/banned organization.

 

B.5    Risk Management

I.       Customer Profile

(i)     A Customer profile of each new customer based on risk categorization will be prepared.  The profile will contain details of customer's identity, social and financial status, nature and business activity, information about his clients' business, their location, etc. For the purpose of exercising due diligence on individual transactions in accounts. "Customer Profile" of individual account holders will be compiled in the account opening forms itself and would provide information regarding:

a)    Occupation

b)    Source of funds

c)     Monthly Income from various sources

d)    Annual turnover (if relevant)

e)    Date of Birth

f)      Existing credit facilities with bank details, if any

g)    Expected transaction activity in the account.

Care has to be exercised by branches that :

·        implementation of the KYC guidelines should not result in denial of banking services (including opening of account) to the public, especially to those, who are financially or socially disadvantaged.

·        introduction of large number of accounts by a single introducer (either account holder or staff) should be accepted with caution.

 (ii)   Customer profiles will be prepared/revised for all accounts.

·        when the branch feels it is necessary to obtain additional information from existing customer based on the conduct or behaviour of the account.

·        while opening accounts by transfer from other branches the customer profile will be updated.

(iii)          In the case of existing account holders, KYC procedures have to be completed for high and medium risk accounts while opening and or renewal of term/recurring deposits, if not already done except in cases where the deposits are placed with the branch under auto renewal procedure or issued to the debit of an account. Transactions in existing accounts will be continuously monitored for any unusual pattern  in  the  operation of the account for reviewing customer due

diligence measures.  The existing accounts of companies, firms, trusts, charities, religious organizations and other institutions will be subjected to minimum KYC standards which would establish the identity of the natural/ legal person and those of the 'beneficial owners'

 

(iv)          For Debit Card, (ATM Card), Credit Card, Internet Banking facilities and other new technology products, the KYC norms as applicable for that risk categorization of the customer will be followed for new accounts.

 

(v)             The information collected by the bank from the customer will be treated as confidential and it will not divulge any details thereof for cross selling or any other purposes.

 

II.     Risk Categorization of the Customers:

The due diligence to be done on the basis of customer's profile will be related to risk categorization of the customer. The broad  risk categorization as under will be observed by the bank. The customers in high risk and medium risk categories will be prone to using bank accounts for money laundering and branches have to observe special care in opening and monitoring this accounts.

 

High Risk Customers

 

(i)     a) who are engaged in certain professions where money laundering possibilities  are high e.g. Antique Dealers (individuals and entities).  Money Services Bureau (entities- not employees of theses entities) and dealers in arms.

b) who live in "high risk countries" (nationality will be irrelevant). An indicative list of such countries is given in Annexure C

c)     Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs) of foreign origin – The branches

should obtain, additional information disclosing the source of funds, that would be deposited in the account.

 

(ii)          Opening of the accounts of person falling under any of the High Risk category will be allowed with specific approval of the Zonal DGM in   case of deposit accounts and for accounts related to borrowing arrangements will be done with the specific approval of the GM/ Addl. GM/ in-charge of loans and advances.

 

(iii)         Branches will carry out intensive due diligence for  high  risk  customers  mentioned  above  as  well as customer for whom the sources of funds are not clear, like

 

a.    non resident customers,

b.    high net worth individuals,

c.     trusts, charities, NGOs and organizations receiving donations.

d.    companies having close family share holding or beneficial ownership.

e.    firms with sleeping partners.

f.      non face to face customers and

g.    those with dubious reputation as per public information available, etc.,

 

Medium Risk Customers

Customers will be classified in Medium Risk category if they fall within the following parameters:

  • any of the account holders who lives in a Medium Risk country.  An indicative list of such countries is given in Annexure D
  • Total aggregate credits to the account in excess of Rs. 5.00 lacs per year in all accounts (other than borrowal accounts) of any particular customer, other than customers where there is sufficient knowledge in the public domain which will enable the bank to classify such customers/ categories of customers, as low risk.

 

Low Risk Customers

 

·        All customers that are not High/Medium Risk will be classified as Low Risk customers.  Additionally, salaried employees whose salary structures are well defined, people belonging to lower economic strata of the society whose accounts show small balances and low turnover, Government departments and Government owned companies, regulators and statutory bodies etc. In such cases, only the basic requirement of verifying the identity and location of the customer are to be met.

 

·        All borrowal customers (other than high risk category) where due diligence is done at the time of granting the facilities will fall under the low risk category.

 

I        Monitoring Implementation of Risk Management

 

        An effective implementation of KYC Norms and AML Standards envisaged in the foregoing policy and procedures viz. adherence to proper customer acceptance norms, maintenance of customer profile, risk categorization of customers, monitoring operations in the account and other relevant information available to the branch, to prevent financial crime will be ensured through the framework covering proper management oversight, systems, controls and other related matters as follows:

 

        a) Bank's Internal Audit and Compliance functions will include an independent evaluation of KYC/AML policies and procedures including compliance of legal and regulatory requirements.  Concurrent/ Internal Auditors shall specifically check and verify the application of KYC/AML procedures at the branches and comment on the lapses observed in this regard.  The compliance in this regard will be put up before the Audit Committee of the Board at quarterly intervals.

 

          b) The bank will organize ongoing employee-training programme so that members of the staff are adequately trained in KYC/AML procedures.

 

        c) The Principal Officer designated by the bank will have the responsibility of managing oversight and coordinating with various functionaries in the implementation of KYC/AML policy. The Principal Officer shall be responsible for monitoring and reporting of all transactions and sharing of information as required under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 (PML Act), more specifically Chapter IV of the PML Act.  He will maintain close liaison with enforcement agencies, banks and any other institutions which are involved in the fight against money laundering and combating financing of terrorism.

 

4.  Conclusion

 

          The KYC/AML Policy and procedures set out in this document are aimed at preventing our bank's services being used for committing financial crime, money laundering and financing of terrorism.  It should be noted by all the staff dealing with the customers that while implementing the policy and procedures, a courteous approach in the process is very essential to take care that the customers are not driven away from the bank.  It is also important to ensure that due care is taken that customers are not tipped off that their account/s are under transaction monitoring.
